## Why Newark Properties See Flood Damage

Newark sits low in several key spots, and the Ironbound is the clearest example: tight to the Passaic River, with streets that were never regraded for the volume of stormwater the city now sees. When the river runs high or a storm surge pushes in, water finds the path of least resistance, and that's often a basement window well or a floor drain running backward. Citywide, Newark's combined sewer system means stormwater and sanitary waste share the same pipes. During heavy rain, that system can surcharge and send contaminated water up through basement drains in Downtown Newark, Vailsburg, and Weequahic alike, not just along the river. Add in the city's dense pre-war housing stock, brick multifamilies, frame two- and three-families with shared foundation walls, and Forest Hill's older colonials with original fieldstone or brick foundations, and you get basements that hold water longer and dry slower than anything built after 1980. A failed sump pump or a cracked foundation seam in one of these buildings doesn't just flood one unit; it can travel along a shared wall and affect a neighbor's space too.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Newark

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable pumps before it has time to wick further into plaster, subfloor, or masonry.
