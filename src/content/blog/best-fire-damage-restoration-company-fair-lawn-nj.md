## Why does IICRC certification actually matter for fire and smoke restoration?

IICRC certification means technicians are trained on the specific chemistry of soot and smoke residue, which behaves nothing like water damage and gets worse if handled wrong. Dry smoke from a fast, hot fire leaves a powdery residue that smears if wiped with the wrong cloth. Wet smoke from a smoldering fire leaves a sticky, oily film that needs a different cleaning agent entirely. Protein residue from a kitchen fire can be nearly invisible but carries an intense odor that regular cleaning won't touch.

The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) sets the S700 standard for fire and smoke restoration specifically for this reason: using the wrong method can drive soot deeper into porous materials or leave an odor that resurfaces every time the humidity rises. A certified firm identifies which type of residue you have before choosing a cleaning method, rather than running the same process on every job.

## What should a fire damage estimate actually include?

A proper fire damage estimate itemizes the scope by area and task, lists the equipment and labor hours involved, and separates mitigation work from contents handling so your adjuster can match it against the claim. It should not be a single lump-sum number with no breakdown.

At minimum, expect line items for structural cleaning (walls, ceilings, framing where accessible), HVAC and duct assessment if smoke traveled through the system, contents inventory and pack-out for items being cleaned or stored offsite, and an odor removal plan separate from surface cleaning, since smoke smell and soot residue are treated as two different problems. If an estimate skips the contents inventory or bundles odor removal into a vague "cleaning" line, ask why. ## How does a good fire restoration company work with your insurance adjuster?

A good fire restoration company documents everything before touching anything, photographing and logging damage room by room so the adjuster has a record that matches the claim. That documentation, not a verbal description after the fact, is what gets claims approved without a fight.

This matters because fire claims get scrutinized more than most water losses: insurers want to see pre-existing condition photos, a clear cause-of-loss note, and an itemized scope before they release funds. A company that photographs the loss as part of its standard process, rather than as an afterthought, gives you a stronger position with the adjuster.
