## Why Passaic Properties See Mold Issues

Passaic is one of the densest small cities in New Jersey, and its housing stock reflects it. Early 20th-century brick multi-families in Downtown Passaic and the Third Ward were built with cellar-level laundry and mechanical rooms that were never meant to stay dry year-round, and many still rely on original cast-iron drain lines that are decades past their design life. When those lines back up, or when a cracked sewer connection lets groundwater seep in slowly instead of all at once, the result is often a basement that smells musty long before anyone sees visible growth. Overcrowded rental units compound the problem: more people cooking, showering, and drying laundry in a space built for fewer occupants means more moisture load on walls that were never insulated or vented for it. Flooding from Irene in 2011 and again from Ida in 2021 left a lot of riverfront and low-spot properties with a hidden legacy, too. Floodwater that was cleaned up on the surface can still leave moisture trapped behind plaster, under subfloor, or inside wall cavities, and that's exactly where colonization shows up months later.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Passaic

We start with a visual and moisture-meter walkthrough of the areas most likely to be compromised, basements, crawl spaces, bathroom walls, and any spot with a known water history. In Passaic's older buildings, that often means checking behind baseboard trim in rooms that back up to a shared wall with a neighboring unit, since moisture migrates through masonry more than people expect. From there we take air samples to measure spore counts against an outdoor control reading, and we'll pull surface samples (tape lift or swab) from any suspect growth so the lab can identify genus and confirm whether what you're looking at is actually mold or just surface staining. Samples go to a third-party lab, and we walk you through the results in plain language, not just a spore count and a shrug. If testing confirms a problem, we scope what remediation would involve, but inspection and testing stand on their own as a diagnostic step, useful before a lease renewal, a home sale, or simply when something smells off and you want an answer instead of a guess.
