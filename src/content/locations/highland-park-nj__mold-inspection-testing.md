## Why Highland Park Properties See Mold Inspection Issues

Highland Park's housing stock skews old: 1910s through 1940s colonials, bungalows, and two-family homes concentrated around Downtown Highland Park and the Triangle, many still running original supply lines and cast-iron drains over finished basements. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out, and a slow drain leak behind a finished wall can feed mold colonies for months before a homeowner notices a stain or a smell. Add the borough's flood history along the Raritan, and you get a second pattern: basements that took on water during Ida or later nor'easters and were dried quickly on the surface but never fully dried inside wall cavities or under subfloor. Rutgers' proximity also means a fair number of multi-unit rentals in North Highland Park and near the Landing Lane Bridge, where turnover between tenants can delay anyone noticing a slow leak until the next lease starts.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Highland Park

We start with a visual and moisture-meter walkthrough of the areas most likely to hide a problem: basement rim joists, behind finished basement walls, under bathroom subfloor, and around any exterior wall that faced flood exposure. In homes near the river corridor, we pay close attention to sill plates and the lowest few feet of framing, since that's where floodwater wicking shows up even when the visible surface looks fine. If conditions warrant, we collect air samples and, where surface growth is visible, tape-lift or swab samples, then send them to an independent, accredited lab for spore identification and counts. You get a written report with lab results, not just a verbal opinion, which matters if you're making a decision about a home purchase, a landlord-tenant dispute, or an insurance claim tied to the 2021 flooding or a more recent pipe failure.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Testing is only as good as the questions it answers, so we calibrate sampling to what we find on the walkthrough rather than running a generic panel. That typically means a combination of outdoor and indoor air samples to establish a baseline comparison, moisture mapping with a penetrating meter on suspect framing, and in finished basements, we'll often recommend a small inspection cut if the moisture readings behind drywall are inconsistent with what's visible. We document everything with photos and meter readings tied to the written report, since that record is what an adjuster or a buyer's attorney will want to see, not just a summary.
