**Storm damage emergency in Brooklyn?** We answer 24/7, and when a nor'easter or the remnant of a tropical system drops a limb through a roof or pushes water into a Canarsie basement, getting a crew moving fast matters more than almost anything else. Storm damage restoration in Brooklyn means fighting two problems at once: wind-driven rain finding gaps in a century-old rowhouse roof, and storm surge or street flooding filling garden-level apartments before anyone upstairs even knows water is rising.

## Why Brooklyn Properties See Storm Damage Differently

Brooklyn's brownstone belt, Park Slope, Brooklyn Heights, and the blocks fringing Bed-Stuy, is mostly 1880s to 1920s masonry construction with garden-level and cellar units built low to the street. Those units flood first in a heavy storm, often through areaway drains and window wells rather than a failed roof. Ida's record rainfall turned that pattern into a citywide emergency and showed how little margin those below-grade spaces have. Coastal neighborhoods tell a different story: Canarsie, Coney Island boardwalk-adjacent blocks, and other low-lying areas still carry real storm-surge memory from Sandy, where saltwater intrusion damaged electrical systems and framing in ways that took months to fully surface.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Brooklyn

The first call after a storm is almost always about stopping active intrusion, a torn roof section, a broken window, a tree through a parapet wall. We tarp and board those openings before anything else so wind-driven rain stops entering while the storm system is still moving through. Once the property is secured, extraction starts on standing water, with attention to where it's actually sitting rather than where it's visible: plaster walls and old lath in pre-war rowhouses hold moisture longer than modern drywall, so drying timelines in a Brooklyn Heights brownstone often run longer than they would in a newer building with the same square footage of water intrusion. Downed limbs and storm debris get cleared as part of the same mobilization, since blocked egress and damaged trees are often tied to the same storm event as the water and roof damage.

## Brooklyn Insurance & HOA Coordination

A lot of Brooklyn storm work involves a co-op or condo board, a management company, or a landlord juggling multiple insured units in one building, not just a single homeowner's policy. We document room by room and unit by unit, with photos and moisture readings that hold up whether the claim goes through an individual owner's carrier or a building's master policy. In party-wall rowhouses, we also flag when damage or drying equipment placement affects a neighboring unit, since those shared walls mean one property's storm damage is sometimes two owners' problem.

## Local note

Brooklyn runs on a combined sewer system in most of the older brownstone belt, which means intense storms can push water backward up through floor drains and basement fixtures even after the rain itself has stopped. A cellar apartment near Prospect Park can take on water hours after the storm passed, simply because the sewer system is still catching up with the volume. We check floor drains and basement plumbing fixtures as a matter of course on storm calls in those neighborhoods, not just the obvious entry points like windows and roofs.
