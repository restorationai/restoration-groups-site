Mold remediation calls in Garfield tend to follow a pattern: a basement that took on water during a storm, dried out on the surface within a week, and then started smelling musty a month later. With so much of the city's housing stock built as early-1900s two- and three-family frame homes with finished basements, that lag between the water event and the visible mold is exactly where the real damage happens, often behind paneling or under carpet pad nobody has pulled up.

## Why Garfield Properties See Mold Issues

Garfield sits along the Passaic River on its western edge, and River Drive properties carry a flood history that got a lot worse in 2021 when Ida pushed river water into basements that had never flooded before. Saddle River tributaries add a second layer of risk, backing up into low-lying stretches near the Dahnert's Lake area after hard rain even when the Passaic stays in its banks. Add in the cast-iron drain lines common to homes in the Harrison Avenue district and Belmont, which slow drainage and back up under stress, and you get basements that hold moisture far longer than a homeowner expects. In the denser multifamily blocks along the Passaic Street corridor, shared walls and floors mean a leak in one unit can wick moisture into the one next door before anyone downstairs notices.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Garfield

We start with a moisture inspection to map where water actually traveled, not just where it's visible, since plaster and masonry foundation walls common to Garfield's older housing stock hold and release moisture differently than modern drywall. Containment comes next, and in multifamily buildings that means sealing off the affected unit and stairwell so spores don't migrate through shared hallways or HVAC returns, a bigger concern in Garfield's packed two- and three-family lots than in a detached single-family home. We run negative air pressure with HEPA filtration while removing porous materials that can't be salvaged, following the drying and verification steps outlined in the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard. Once the affected materials are out and the space is dried, we do a final check before clearing the containment, so you're not left guessing whether the job actually finished the problem.

## Garfield Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most Garfield properties are owner-occupied two- and three-family homes or small rental buildings rather than HOA-governed condos, so coordination usually means working directly with a landlord and documenting damage separately for each affected unit. That matters for insurance, because carriers often want clear evidence of which unit sustained the original water loss versus where mold spread afterward, especially on claims tied to the 2021 flooding pattern along River Drive. We photograph and document room by room as we go so that paperwork lines up with what the adjuster sees, whether the loss is a single finished basement or a multi-unit building near the Passaic Street business district.

## Local note

Homes near the Harrison Avenue district and parts of Plauderville on older cast-iron lateral lines often show a slow, intermittent basement odor that homeowners assume is sewer gas rather than mold, because the drain backs up just enough during heavy rain to wet framing behind a finished wall without ever flooding the floor. By the time the smell is noticeable at the surface, the mold has usually been established behind that wall for weeks. We check those lines and the framing behind them early in an inspection rather than waiting for visible staining to show up.
