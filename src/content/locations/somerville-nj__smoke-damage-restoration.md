## Why Somerville Properties See Smoke Damage Spread Fast

Much of Somerville's housing stock, especially the frame two-families and Victorians around Fairview, East Main, and Gaston Avenue, was built before central forced-air systems existed, so older duct retrofits and chimney chases often have gaps that let smoke migrate between floors far beyond the room of origin. Plaster walls common to this era absorb smoke odor rather than letting it sit on the surface, which means a kitchen fire in a Mercer Street two-family can leave a sulfuric, acrid smell in a second-floor bedroom closet with no visible soot at all. Downtown, the Main Street business district's mix of century-old storefronts and newer apartment conversions adds another layer: shared walls, common HVAC risers, and tight commercial units mean a single-tenant fire can push smoke odor into neighboring businesses or upper-floor residential units before anyone upstairs even knows there was a fire.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Somerville

We start with a walkthrough to separate dry smoke residue (from fast-burning, high-heat fires) from wet smoke residue (from smoldering, lower-heat fires), because the two require different cleaning agents and the wrong approach can smear soot deeper into porous plaster or drywall. In pre-1950 Somerville homes we test absorption on an inconspicuous section of plaster before committing to a cleaning method, since plaster holds odor compounds differently than the drywall found in newer downtown builds. From there we HEPA-vacuum loose residue, hand-clean or media-blast fixed surfaces depending on the material, seal and prime where staining has penetrated wood trim or built-ins, and run thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators to neutralize odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Contents that can be saved, upholstery, clothing, documents, go through separate ozone or odor-chamber treatment away from the structure.
