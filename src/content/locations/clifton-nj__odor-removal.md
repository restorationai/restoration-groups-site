## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Clifton

We start by identifying what's actually causing the smell, not just masking it. That means inspecting wall cavities, subfloor, and HVAC returns for hidden moisture, especially in homes near Weasel Brook Park where crawl spaces and basement perimeters tend to stay damp longer after a storm. Once the source is addressed (drying, removal of saturated materials, or sanitizing a sewage-affected area), we match the deodorization method to the material. Hard, non-porous surfaces respond well to direct cleaning and sealing. Soft goods, framing cavities, and brick assemblies in older Botany Village buildings usually need something that can reach into the material itself, which is where thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment come in. We document conditions before and after treatment so the record supports an insurance claim if the odor traces back to a covered water or fire loss.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

The right tool depends on what's in the air and who's in the building. Thermal fogging replicates how smoke particles originally traveled into cracks and seams, which makes it effective for fire and smoke odor in older plaster homes where the smell has settled deep into wall assemblies. Hydroxyl generators work through an oxidation process similar to ozone but don't produce the same byproduct, so they're safe to run in occupied homes and active commercial spaces, useful for a Route 3 retail tenant that can't shut down for days. Ozone treatment is more aggressive and faster for unoccupied structures or severe smoke and sewage odor, but it requires the space to be vacated and ventilated afterward, something we walk property managers through carefully on multi-unit buildings. For most residential jobs we pair one of these treatments with HVAC cleaning, since a system that recirculated smoke or musty air during the event often becomes its own odor source.
