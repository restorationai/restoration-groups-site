## Our Mold Remediation Process in Summit's Older Homes

Containment comes first, and in a home with original millwork, plaster ceilings, and finished basements built out for family use, containment has to protect what's outside the work zone as much as what's inside it. We build physical barriers and run negative air pressure so spores don't travel into the rest of the house during removal, which matters more in a deep Summit basement with finished rec rooms than in a simple unfinished crawl space. Moisture mapping comes next, using meters to find how far saturation has traveled through plaster and framing rather than guessing from visible damage alone. Affected materials are removed to the extent the IICRC S520 standard calls for, and anything salvageable, trim, flooring, cabinetry, gets documented and photographed so a homeowner's contractor has a clear record of original finishes before any wall is opened further. Where a home has significant furniture, rugs, or family contents in the affected area, we handle pack-out and storage separately from the containment zone so nothing absorbs odor or cross-contaminates during the work.
