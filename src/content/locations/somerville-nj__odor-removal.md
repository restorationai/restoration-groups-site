## Why Somerville Properties See Odor Removal Issues

Somerville's housing stock is a mix of early-1900s frame two-families, Victorians, and colonials in neighborhoods like Fairview, East Main, and around Gaston Avenue, many with finished basements that were never designed for the moisture loads they now handle. When those basements flood, even briefly, the carpet pad, drywall paper, and subfloor can hold onto musty and sewage-adjacent odors long after the visible water is gone. Cast-iron drain lines common in these homes also corrode internally over decades, which produces a sour, metallic smell that's often mistaken for a dead-animal odor. On the commercial side, the Main Street corridor's restaurants and storefronts deal with smoke, grease, and sprinkler-discharge odors that settle into HVAC systems and shared walls in the newer downtown apartment buildings built above them. In the West End and Downtown areas where residential and commercial space sit close together, odor migration between units is a recurring complaint we hear from property managers.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Somerville

We start by identifying the odor source rather than treating the air alone, since masking a smell without addressing saturated materials just delays the complaint. In finished basements affected by flood intrusion, that often means pulling baseboards and a section of drywall to check what's happening behind the surface before any deodorizing equipment runs. Once the source material is removed or treated, we select the method that fits the space: thermal fogging for fabrics, upholstery, and tight wall cavities where a dry fog can penetrate porous surfaces; hydroxyl generators for occupied homes and apartments, since hydroxyl treatment doesn't require evacuating the space; and ozone treatment for unoccupied units, vacant storefronts, or post-fire structures where stronger oxidation is needed and people and pets can be kept out during the cycle. We finish with a post-treatment walkthrough to confirm the odor is gone, not just reduced.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

The method matters as much as the equipment. Ozone treatment is effective against stubborn smoke and sewage odors but needs an unoccupied structure and a defined re-entry period, which works well for the vacant units in Somerville's newer downtown developments between tenants. Hydroxyl deodorization is slower but safe to run with people, pets, and plants in the space, which makes it the better fit for occupied colonials and two-families in Fairview or near Gaston Avenue where residents can't easily leave for a day. Thermal fogging handles textiles, closets, and HVAC ductwork that ozone and hydroxyl units can't reach directly, breaking down smoke and odor particles at a molecular level inside fabric fibers. We choose equipment based on occupancy, material type, and how deep the odor has traveled into the structure, not a one-size approach.
