## Why Hillside Properties See Mold Issues

Hillside's colonials, two-families, and postwar capes were mostly built with galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains that are well past their intended service life. When those fail quietly inside a wall or under a slab, moisture sits in the framing long before anyone notices a stain. Add the Elizabeth River running along the township's eastern side, and you get a second, more sudden path to mold: storm overflow and sewer backup pushing water into basements during hard rain. Ida in 2021 was the clearest example, but smaller versions of that same flood pattern happen most summers when sump pumps in Winans and Conant can't keep pace with a fast downpour. Mold doesn't wait for a major storm to take hold. Given the right humidity and an organic surface, like the paper facing on drywall or a damp sill plate, colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours, which is why a basement that flooded in June can still be growing something in September if it wasn't dried and checked properly.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Hillside

We start with a walkthrough that pays close attention to the parts of the house most likely to hide a problem in this town: the sump pit and discharge line, the rim joist where a foundation meets framing, and any finished basement wall that was built directly against masonry without a thermal break. From there we use moisture meters and infrared imaging to find elevated readings behind surfaces that look dry, then collect air and surface samples where conditions warrant it. Spore trap samples go to an independent, accredited lab, not an in-house test, so the results stand on their own if you need them for an insurance claim or a real estate transaction. You get a written report with lab findings, photos, and a plain-language summary of what we found and what, if anything, needs remediation.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Indoor air quality testing only means something if the sampling protocol is sound. We use calibrated hygrometers to log relative humidity in problem rooms, borescopes to look inside wall cavities without cutting large openings, and both viable and non-viable spore trap cartridges depending on what the situation calls for. For homes near the Conant Street industrial area or along the Route 22 retail corridor, where older commercial buildings sometimes have flat roofs and HVAC systems that recirculate air across multiple units, we'll often run comparative outdoor baseline samples alongside the indoor readings so the lab results actually mean something in context, rather than just producing a spore count with no point of comparison.
