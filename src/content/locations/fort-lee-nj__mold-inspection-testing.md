## Why Fort Lee Properties See Mold Issues

Fort Lee's housing stock splits into two very different problems. Along the cliffs, dense high-rise condo and rental towers stack plumbing runs through shared walls and chases, so a slow leak two floors up can show up as a musty smell or a ceiling stain in a unit that never had its own plumbing failure. In Coytesville and The Hill, older multifamily homes and detached houses sit on shallow foundations and crawl spaces that trap humidity, especially where storm runoff off the Palisades grade pools against a foundation wall after heavy rain. Bergen County saw what that runoff can do during Ida in 2021, and plenty of basements near Main Street and the lower grades still carry the moisture history from that storm in their framing and subfloor, even if nothing looks wet on the surface today.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Fort Lee

We start with a visual inspection and moisture mapping using a moisture meter and thermal imaging to find hidden dampness behind drywall, under flooring, or inside a dropped ceiling, which is common in Fort Lee's high-rise units with suspended ceiling grids hiding plumbing risers. If visible growth or conditions warrant it, we collect air samples and, where appropriate, surface tape or swab samples, and send them to a third-party lab for spore analysis. For condo and co-op units, we pay close attention to shared mechanical chases and party walls, since growth in one unit's wall cavity often traces back to a line serving a neighboring unit, which changes who's responsible for the repair side of things even before remediation starts. Results typically come back from the lab within a few business days, and we walk the homeowner or property manager through what the spore counts and species mean in plain language, not just a lab report full of Latin names.

## Fort Lee Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most of Fort Lee's high-rise buildings along the Palisades are managed under condo associations with their own insurance master policies, which usually cover common-element plumbing but push unit-level finishes and contents back to the owner's individual policy. That split makes documentation matter more here than in a single-family town. We photograph and log moisture readings by location and provide a written inspection report that both the unit owner's carrier and the association's management company can use, since association boards in buildings near Hudson Lights and along the Palisade corridor frequently require a documented mold assessment before approving any remediation work in common areas or shared chases.

## Local note

Fort Lee's older multifamily buildings in Coytesville and parts of The Hill often have crawl spaces that were never properly vented when the homes were built, and a fair number still don't have a vapor barrier over bare soil. In our inspections there, we check crawl space humidity even when the complaint is a smell on the main floor, because soil moisture wicking up through an unsealed crawl space is a more common root cause in that part of town than anything happening inside the living space itself.
