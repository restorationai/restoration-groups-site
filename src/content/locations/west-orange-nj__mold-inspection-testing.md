## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in West Orange

We start with a walkthrough of the areas you're concerned about and anywhere water has a history of getting in, basement corners, crawl spaces, around HVAC returns, behind kitchen and bathroom plumbing walls. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help us map where materials are holding water that isn't visible at the surface, which matters in older St. Cloud and Gregory section homes where plaster can look dry while the lath behind it stays saturated. From there we take air samples with a spore trap to measure what's circulating indoors versus a baseline outdoor sample, and surface or bulk samples from any suspect growth. Everything gets sent to an independent lab for analysis, and we walk you through the results in plain language, what was found, where, and what it means for next steps. We don't perform remediation off an inspection we conducted ourselves without disclosing that upfront, so you're never guessing whether the recommendation is objective.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Testing accuracy depends on calibrated tools and correct sampling technique, not just a visual look and a guess. We use moisture meters to check relative readings across drywall, subfloor, and framing, thermal imaging cameras to spot temperature differentials that often indicate trapped moisture behind finished surfaces, and spore trap air sampling pumps to capture an accurate snapshot of airborne mold concentration. Surface swabs and bulk material samples get bagged and sent to a third-party lab rather than analyzed in-house, which keeps the results independent of whoever performed the inspection. All of this follows the sampling and documentation approach outlined in the IICRC S520 standard for mold assessment.
