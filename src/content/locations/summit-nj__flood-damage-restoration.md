## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Summit

Flood cleanup in a Summit home starts with moisture mapping, not demolition. Deep finished basements under Brayton School area and Downtown Summit properties often have layered flooring, original plaster, and built-in millwork that a crew should not touch without first documenting what's salvageable. We extract standing water, pull moisture readings from walls, subfloor, and framing, and separate materials that can be dried in place from those that have to come out. Because so many of these homes carry high-value furnishings, rugs, and family pieces that predate the house's last renovation, contents are catalogued and either dried on-site or moved to controlled storage rather than piled in a driveway. Drying plaster and old-growth lumber takes longer than drying modern drywall and engineered flooring, and we set drying targets and equipment placement around that reality instead of a generic timeline. Once materials are dry and stable, we document moisture readings and affected finishes for whichever contractor handles the rebuild, so period details like plaster cove molding or original baseboard don't get guessed at later.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard here: a Summit basement can read "dry" at the surface within a day while the plaster lath behind it stays saturated for a week or more.
