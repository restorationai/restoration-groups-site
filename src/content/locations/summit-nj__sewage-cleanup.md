## Why Summit Homes See Sewage Backups

A sewer lateral failure in a newer build is a plumbing call. In a Summit home with a deep finished basement, original cast iron house trap, and decades of settling around the foundation, it's a different problem. Backups here tend to surface through the lowest fixture, a basement half-bath or a laundry sink, and spread across finished flooring before anyone notices the smell. Because so many basements in Summit are fully built out, a backup usually means saturated carpet pad, wet drywall at the base of the walls, and sometimes water wicking up into original wood trim that a homeowner doesn't want cut out if it can be saved.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

We treat every backup as a contaminated water loss from the start, following IICRC-informed category 3 protocols rather than guessing at severity. That means removing standing sewage and saturated porous materials that can't be sanitized, like carpet pad and baseboard, while documenting what's salvageable before anything gets disturbed. In homes with original plaster and millwork, that documentation step matters more: we moisture-map the walls, photograph trim and flooring condition, and flag anything worth hand-cleaning instead of discarding so a homeowner isn't losing finishes that can't be replicated. Once the space is cleared, we apply EPA-registered antimicrobials, run negative air where needed, and verify drying with moisture meters before calling the space closed, not just dry to the touch.

## Summit Insurance Coordination

Sewage backups from a failed lateral are typically covered differently than general water damage, and carriers often ask for separate documentation showing the source was sewage rather than clean or gray water. We photograph the point of entry, log contamination levels, and provide the paperwork adjusters expect for a category 3 claim. For homes bordering New Providence, where some streets sit on shared or older municipal lines, we've found it helps to document the lateral condition clearly in case liability for the line itself becomes part of the claim conversation between the homeowner and the township.
