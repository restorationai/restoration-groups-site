## Why Brooklyn Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Much of the brownstone belt, Park Slope, Brooklyn Heights, and the blocks pushing into Flatbush, was built between the 1880s and 1920s with masonry foundations and cellar-level apartments that sit close to grade. When storm sewers surcharge, that lower level is usually the first place water enters, often backing up through floor drains before it ever comes through a window well. Coastal pockets near the Coney Island boardwalk and out toward Canarsie carry a different risk profile: storm surge and tidal flooding that residents still measure against what Sandy did to those blocks. Ida's rainfall totals turned ordinary summer storms into a citywide basement flooding event, and it changed how seriously co-op boards and brownstone owners here take drainage, sump pumps, and backflow valves. Add century-old clay and cast-iron sewer lines under many streets, and a heavy rain event can overwhelm infrastructure faster than it would in a newer suburb.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Brooklyn

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out of basements, garden units, and ground-floor commercial spaces with truck-mounted and portable pumps sized for narrow Brooklyn entryways. From there, drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, but the materials dictate the pace: original plaster and lath found in pre-war brownstones hold moisture longer than modern drywall, so extraction can look complete at the surface while the wall cavity is still wet. We document moisture readings room by room, which matters when the building is a co-op or the claim involves a shared party wall with a neighboring unit. Contents in flooded basements, storage, seasonal items, sometimes a tenant's belongings, get assessed, photographed, and either dried in place or moved to controlled storage depending on what survived.

## Brooklyn Insurance & HOA Coordination

Flood losses in New York City almost always involve more paperwork than a simple homeowner's claim, particularly when the building is a co-op or condo with a managing agent in the loop. Boards typically want documentation that satisfies both the unit owner's policy and the building's master policy, and they want to know the work won't affect a neighboring unit without that owner's sign-off too. We photograph pre-existing conditions, moisture-map the affected areas, and keep records in a format most property managers and adjusters recognize, which keeps a claim moving instead of stalling over missing documentation. For commercial ground-floor tenants, landlord notification is usually part of the same conversation.

## Local note

We plan extraction and drying around that reality instead of treating the basement like empty crawl space.
