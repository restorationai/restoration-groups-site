## Why Highland Park Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Highland Park sits along the southern bank of the Raritan River, and that geography caught up with the borough hard during Hurricane Ida in 2021, when Donaldson Park and the low stretches along River Road took on significant water. Homes in the Meadow and Livingston Manor sections, along with stretches close to the river, sit on ground that's slow to drain after heavy rain, which means groundwater pressure against foundation walls lingers long after the storm passes. Layer that on top of the borough's housing stock, mostly 1910s through 1940s colonials, bungalows, and two-families, and you get original supply plumbing and cast-iron drain lines that were never built for today's water volume. Those pipes fail over finished basements more often than newer PVC systems, and when they do, the water sits against old plaster and subfloor that wasn't designed to shed moisture quickly.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Highland Park

The first call is always assessment: how much standing water, how deep, and whether it's clean groundwater intrusion or a drain backup that needs sanitizing as sewage-adjacent loss. In Highland Park's finished basements, that usually means pulling baseboard and lower drywall sections quickly, because the plaster-and-lath construction common in the borough's older colonials holds moisture inside the wall cavity long after the surface looks dry. We extract standing water, set drying equipment calibrated to the square footage and material type, and track moisture daily with meters rather than guessing based on how a room feels. For cast-iron drain failures specifically, we coordinate with a plumber before closing up any wall or floor cavity, since re-flooding from the same failed line a week later is a real risk if the root cause isn't fixed first.
