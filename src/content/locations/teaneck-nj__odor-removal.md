## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Teaneck

Odor work starts with finding the source material, not just the smell. Our crews inspect subfloor, wall cavities, and HVAC runs to identify what's actually holding the odor, whether that's smoke residue, sewage-contaminated wood, or moisture-damaged insulation. Porous materials that can't be fully deodorized (soaked drywall, some subfloor sections) get removed rather than masked. From there we select equipment based on the contamination type: thermal fogging to neutralize smoke odor particles in the same way smoke penetrated the space, hydroxyl generators for occupied buildings where residents or tenants need to stay in adjacent units, and ozone treatment for unoccupied spaces that need a deeper, faster reset. Teaneck's older homes, with plaster walls and tighter crawl spaces, often need a slower hydroxyl cycle than open modern construction because plaster releases trapped odor gradually rather than all at once.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Ozone and hydroxyl treatments solve different problems, and picking wrong wastes time. Ozone oxidizes odor molecules fast but requires the space to be vacated during treatment, which works well for a fire-damaged single-family home in West Teaneck but is harder to schedule in a mixed-use building on Cedar Lane with apartments above an occupied storefront. Hydroxyl generators run safely around people and pets, so they're our default for occupied homes and for commercial spaces near Fairleigh Dickinson University or Holy Name Medical Center where downtime isn't an option. Thermal fogging, which carries a deodorizing agent on heated particles that mimic how smoke traveled through a structure, is typically paired with one of the above rather than used alone. We also seal and prime any charred or sewage-affected wood framing before finish work begins, since odor trapped under new material will eventually work its way back out.
