## Why Manhattan Properties See Storm Damage Differently

Manhattan's building stock works against fast drainage. Flat prewar roofs rely on narrow scuppers and interior leaders that clog with storm debris, and once a roof drain backs up, water finds the nearest penetration, a conduit, a chase, an old chimney flue, and travels down rather than off the building. Hurricane Sandy's surge flooded basements and ground-floor mechanical rooms across the Financial District and the Lower East Side, and more recently Ida's rainfall overwhelmed street and building drains from Harlem down through Chelsea faster than the systems could move it. In a borough built mostly vertical, a single roof or facade failure during a storm event can affect a dozen units stacked on top of each other before anyone upstairs even knows there's a problem.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Manhattan

We start with emergency board-up or tarping to stop active intrusion, then move into extraction and floor-by-floor moisture mapping, since a leak on the roof of a twenty-story building doesn't dry the same way a single-family home does. Work inside occupied towers means coordinating freight elevator time with the building superintendent, staging drying equipment in stairwells and service corridors where space allows, and keeping noise and foot traffic to windows that boards and managing agents will accept. Every affected unit gets its own moisture log, which matters when three apartments on three different floors are all part of the same claim.

## Manhattan Insurance & HOA Coordination

Co-op boards, condo associations, and resident managers all expect the same paperwork before a crew sets foot past the lobby: certificates of insurance, a clear scope of work, and unit-by-unit documentation that can be handed to the managing agent and the building's master policy carrier. We work inside that process rather than around it, because a job that skips a step with the board usually gets stopped at the front desk. For ground-floor retail and office space, storm mitigation often runs overnight so a closed business doesn't turn into a second week of lost revenue on top of the repair itself.

## Local note

Many prewar buildings along Central Park West and in pockets of the Upper East Side still use roof scuppers only a few inches wide, a detail from an era with lighter rainfall design standards. When a super can't get onto the roof fast enough during a fast-moving storm, those scuppers clog with leaves and debris and water backs up onto the roof deck, then finds the nearest wall penetration instead of the drain it was supposed to use. Knowing which roof systems are prone to this helps us figure out where water actually traveled, not just where it showed up.
