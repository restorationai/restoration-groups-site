## Why Colonia Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Colonia's housing stock went up during the suburban building boom of the 1950s and 60s, and that era's plumbing is now reaching the point where failures cluster: galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, and cast-iron stacks develop pinhole leaks that often go unnoticed until a ceiling stain appears or a basement carpet feels damp underfoot. Add to that a water table that sits relatively high in parts of Middlesex County, and finished basements here are prone to seepage during sustained rain events, not just catastrophic storms. Homes with below-grade family rooms or converted basements are especially exposed, since flooring and drywall installed directly against a concrete slab will wick moisture upward long after the surface looks dry.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Colonia

The first visit is about stopping the loss and reading the structure, not just running a shop vac. We extract standing water, then use moisture meters to map how far water has traveled into subfloor, baseboards, and framing, because in a lot of Colonia's older homes the visible wet spot is smaller than the actual damage. Structural drying equipment goes in next, calibrated to the home's layout, basement ceiling height, and whether the area is finished or unfinished. We check back and adjust the drying plan daily, and we document moisture readings throughout so the numbers back up the work when a claim gets filed.
