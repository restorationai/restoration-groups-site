## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in New Brunswick

The first call we make on site is diagnostic: is this river-driven groundwater, a failed supply line, or sewage backing up through a floor drain. That distinction changes everything downstream. Groundwater intrusion near the river gets extracted and the slab monitored for continued seepage before drying begins, since pumping too aggressively in saturated soil can pull more water back in. A supply line failure in a Robeson Village two-family gets isolated at the source first, then extracted and dried per the IICRC S500 standard. Backups involving sanitary lines get treated as a contamination event, not just a water event, which means affected porous materials like baseboards and finished basement drywall often come out rather than get dried in place. In all three cases we're running moisture readings against the foundation walls and sill plate, not just the floor, because basement walls in this city's older housing hold water longer than people expect.
