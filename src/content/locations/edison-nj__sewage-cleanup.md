## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Edison

We start by identifying the category of loss and shutting off further intrusion at the source, whether that means working with a plumber on a failed lateral or confirming the backup has stopped before we touch extraction. Standing sewage and saturated porous materials, carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, come out first, because category 3 water is not something you dry in place. Hard surfaces and framing get cleaned and sanitized with EPA-registered antimicrobials, and we document moisture readings in the slab and framing as we go, since Edison basements with poured or block foundations can trap moisture in ways that aren't obvious from the surface. Air scrubbers and negative air setups come in for odor and airborne contaminant control while the space dries, and we don't close out a job until post-sanitization readings confirm the space is safe to rebuild finishes in.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Sewage Cleanup

Sewage jobs call for different equipment than a clean-water loss. Truck-mounted extraction handles standing water and solids, submersible pumps clear crawl spaces and low basement corners that gravity extraction misses, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously to manage the particulate and odor load while contaminated materials are bagged and removed. We use moisture mapping throughout, not just at the start, because a cast-iron drain failure in an older Edison basement often means the subfloor and sill plate have been wet far longer than the visible backup suggests.
