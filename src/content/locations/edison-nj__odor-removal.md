## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Edison

We start by identifying the odor's source material rather than just its location, since a smoke smell trapped in kitchen cabinetry behaves differently than a sewage odor sitting in saturated subfloor insulation. Porous materials that can't be cleaned, like soaked carpet padding or water-damaged drywall, usually need to come out before deodorization will hold. From there we match the method to the space: hydroxyl generators for occupied homes and tenanted multifamily units where people, pets, or plants need to stay on-site, and ozone treatment for vacant structures or commercial spaces that can be sealed off and aired out afterward, since ozone at working concentrations isn't safe to breathe. Thermal fogging gets used on fire and smoke jobs where the odor has worked into cabinetry, trim, and other finishes, because the fog particles penetrate the same pores the smoke did. Air quality and odor levels get rechecked before we close out the job, not just assumed clear because the smell is weaker.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Ozone treatment oxidizes odor molecules rather than covering them, which is effective on tough smoke and sewage odors but requires the structure to be unoccupied during treatment and ventilated afterward. Hydroxyl deodorization produces a similar oxidizing effect using a process safer for occupied spaces, which matters in Edison's denser apartment and condo buildings where a neighboring unit can't always be vacated. Thermal fogging distributes a deodorizing agent as a fine fog that mimics how smoke originally traveled into cracks and porous surfaces, making it the right call for fire-related jobs rather than water or sewage odors. We pair all three with source removal and, where moisture is involved, drying equipment set to IICRC S500 standards so the odor doesn't simply return once humidity climbs again.
