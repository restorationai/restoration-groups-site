**Flood damage in a Manhattan high-rise behaves differently than almost anywhere else we work.** When a supply line fails on the 14th floor of a prewar co-op, the water doesn't pool in a basement, it drops through plaster ceilings and hardwood subfloors into the unit below, then the one below that. We answer calls 24/7 across the borough, and getting the water stopped and mapped fast matters more here than almost anywhere in our service area.

## Why Manhattan Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Manhattan's building stock splits into two flood-prone categories, and both fail in their own way. Prewar co-ops in neighborhoods like the Upper West Side and Upper East Side still run original risers and cast-iron stacks that corrode from the inside, often failing without warning behind finished walls. Newer condo towers in Midtown and the Financial District concentrate plumbing into vertical chases serving dozens of units, so a single washing-machine hose or radiator valve failure on a high floor can cascade down through multiple ceilings before anyone on a lower floor notices a stain. Street-level flooding adds another layer: Sandy's surge inundated the Financial District and Lower East Side, and more recently Ida overwhelmed storm drains from Harlem to Chelsea, pushing water into below-grade mechanical rooms and ground-floor retail that wasn't built to handle sustained inundation.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Manhattan

We start with source control and a moisture map of every affected surface, not just the unit where water was first reported, since vertical intrusion almost always means checking the units above and below. Extraction equipment has to be sized for freight elevators and narrow service corridors, so we stage smaller, higher-capacity units rather than the larger trailer-mounted systems we might use on a single-family job in New Jersey. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture logs for each affected unit because boards and managing agents expect documentation, not just a dry-to-the-touch assessment. For ground-floor commercial spaces near Times Square or Grand Central Terminal, we coordinate overnight mitigation windows so a restaurant or retail tenant can reopen for business the next morning instead of losing a full day of revenue.

## Manhattan Insurance & HOA Coordination

Co-op boards and condo managing agents each have their own paperwork before they'll let a crew on-site: certificates of insurance naming the building, freight elevator reservations, and sometimes a building engineer sign-off before work starts. We handle that coordination directly with the resident manager or managing agent rather than leaving the unit owner to chase down forms mid-emergency. On the insurance side, Manhattan claims frequently involve both a unit owner's HO-6 policy and the building's master policy, and which one covers what, drywall versus original finishes, for example, depends on the proprietary lease or condo declaration. We document room-by-room with photos and moisture readings so whichever carrier is responsible has what it needs without a second site visit.

## Local note

One detail that trips up crews unfamiliar with the borough: many prewar buildings near Central Park and on the Upper East Side have radiator-based heating systems with steam risers that run through closets and behind walls shared between units. A failed valve on one of these doesn't always show up as an obvious leak, it can read as a slow ceiling stain two floors down that looks more like a roof problem than a heating system failure. We check riser rooms and shared chases early in the assessment specifically because of this pattern.
