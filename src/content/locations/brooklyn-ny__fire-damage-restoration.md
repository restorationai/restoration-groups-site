**Fire damage in a Park Slope brownstone doesn't behave like fire damage in a suburban split-level.** A kitchen fire two floors up can push smoke through original plaster walls, shared party-wall cavities, and dumbwaiter shafts that haven't been used since the building went up in 1905. Soot settles into wood trim, pushes through to the unit next door, and lingers in HVAC and radiator systems long after the flames are out. Getting ahead of that spread matters more in Brooklyn's attached housing stock than almost anywhere else we work.

## Why Brooklyn Fire Damage Behaves Differently

Much of the borough's housing, from the brownstone blocks of Park Slope and Brooklyn Heights to the rowhouses lining Bay Ridge and Flatbush, predates modern fire-stopping codes. Party walls that separate attached homes were built to share structure, not to seal off smoke, so a fire in one unit routinely leaves soot residue and odor in the neighbor's stairwell or cellar. In Williamsburg and Bushwick, converted industrial buildings and mixed-use loft conversions add another wrinkle: open ductwork and exposed brick mean smoke travels farther and settles into more porous surfaces before anyone can contain it. Garden-level and cellar apartments, common throughout the brownstone belt, often take on firefighting water in addition to smoke damage, which means mitigation has to address char, soot, and moisture at the same time rather than in sequence.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Brooklyn

We start with a walkthrough to separate what's salvageable from what isn't, documenting room by room for the insurance file before anything is moved or cleaned. Soft contents, upholstery, and textiles that can be saved go through contents restoration and pack-out rather than disposal, which matters in older apartments where replacement trim, moldings, or built-ins aren't easy to match. Structural surfaces get cleaned and treated per IICRC-informed methods (we're an IICRC Certified Firm), with HVAC and duct runs checked for soot migration since forced air is how fire odor spreads two or three units away in an attached building. Odor removal and deodorization typically runs longest in plaster and masonry, since those materials hold smoke odor deeper than drywall and release it slowly even after surface cleaning looks complete.

## Brooklyn Insurance & Co-op/Condo Board Coordination

A fire in a Brooklyn co-op or condo almost always involves more than one insurer: the unit owner's policy, the building's master policy, and sometimes a neighboring unit's claim if smoke or water crossed a party wall. Management companies in buildings near Prospect Park and the Brooklyn Heights promenade typically want a documented scope of work and photos before authorizing access for drying equipment in common hallways or shared mechanical rooms. We put together documentation formatted for that review, itemizing affected materials and the work performed so it holds up whether it's the co-op board, the owner's carrier, or both reviewing the file.

## Local note

In Canarsie and other coastal pockets of Brooklyn, post-fire mitigation sometimes overlaps with flood history: homes that took on water during past coastal storms may already have replacement drywall or newer subfloor in spots, which changes how smoke and soot behave compared to the original plaster elsewhere in the house.
