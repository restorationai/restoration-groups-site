## Why Westfield Homes Hold Onto Smoke Residue Longer

Westfield's housing stock, heavy with homes from the 1890s through the 1930s in neighborhoods like Wychwood, The Gardens, and Manor Park, means a lot of original plaster and lath instead of drywall. Plaster is porous. Smoke particles bond to it chemically, not just physically, which is why a quick wipe-down leaves a house smelling fine for a day or two before the odor creeps back. Add in the near-universal finished basement, home theaters, gyms, home offices built into what used to be unfinished space, and you have synthetic carpet, foam underlayment, and acoustic tile that trap smoke odor from a kitchen fire or a furnace puff-back on the floor above. Knob-and-tube era wiring updates and older cast-iron stacks also mean more wall cavities were opened and patched over the decades, giving smoke more hidden paths to travel.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Westfield

We start with a walkthrough to map how far the smoke traveled, not just where the fire was. In homes near Downtown Westfield's older blocks off Quimby Street, that often means checking the attic and basement even when the fire stayed in the kitchen, since smoke rises and settles through balloon framing. From there:

- Contents and furniture get assessed on-site for ozone or thermal fogging treatment, or packed out when a room needs full access.
- Soot is removed from hard surfaces with dry sponges and chemical sponges calibrated to protein, synthetic, or wet-smoke residue, since the wrong method can grind soot deeper into plaster or painted trim.
- Porous materials, plaster walls, insulation, some flooring, are evaluated for whether cleaning will fully clear the odor or whether the material needs to come out.
- HVAC systems and ductwork are cleaned separately, since recirculated smoke odor is one of the most common reasons a house smells fine for a week and then doesn't.
- Air scrubbers and thermal fogging close out the odor work once surfaces are clean, with a final walkthrough before we sign off.

Owners in Westfield tend to care a lot about matching original trim, flooring, and plaster finishes, so we document existing materials before any removal starts, which also helps when a claim needs that detail later.
