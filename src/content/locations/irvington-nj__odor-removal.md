## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Irvington

Odor work here starts with identifying the source material, not just masking the smell. In a Grove Park two-family with smoke damage, that means checking whether residue has settled into plaster pores and HVAC ductwork that serves multiple units, since odor can migrate between floors in these older buildings faster than in detached single-family homes. For basement odor tied to past flooding, we test moisture content in framing and subfloor before treatment, because treating a surface that's still damp underneath just delays the smell's return. Once the source is confirmed, we remove or seal contaminated porous materials that can't be fully deodorized, then apply hydroxyl or ozone treatment depending on whether the space is occupied during the work. Ozone is effective but requires the area to be vacated for a period, which matters in occupied multifamily buildings near Chancellor Avenue where tenants may be home during treatment. Hydroxyl generators run safely in occupied spaces and are often the better fit for Irvington's attached housing stock where neighboring units share walls and ventilation.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Thermal fogging recreates the particle size of smoke, pushing a deodorizing agent into the same cracks, seams, and wall cavities that smoke odor originally penetrated, which makes it effective on fire-related jobs in older plaster construction where surface cleaning alone won't reach embedded residue. For flood-related musty odor, we pair hydroxyl generation with targeted air movers and dehumidification to make sure we're not just covering up moisture that's still active behind baseboards or under flooring near the Olympic Park site area, where basement dampness is a recurring pattern in older homes. Air scrubbers with carbon filtration run throughout the job to manage particulate, and we document before-and-after odor conditions with the same documentation most insurance carriers expect for a covered loss.
