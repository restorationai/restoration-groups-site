## Mold inspection and testing in Newark starts with understanding why the city's housing stock holds moisture differently than newer construction. A century-old brick two-family in the Ironbound that took on floodwater during Ida doesn't dry the way a 1990s colonial does, and a musty smell in a Forest Hill basement often means something different than the same smell in a slab-on-grade home. Testing has to account for both the building and the water history before anyone can say what's actually growing.

## Why Newark Properties See Mold Growth

Newark's combined sewer system means heavy rain can push water back up through basement drains in neighborhoods well away from the Passaic River, not just in the Ironbound. That intermittent backup, paired with dense pre-war multifamily construction, brick party walls, and shared risers, creates conditions where moisture gets trapped inside wall cavities long after the visible water is gone. University Heights and Downtown Newark both have a mix of converted multifamily buildings and institutional-adjacent housing where one unit's plumbing failure or ventilation gap can quietly feed mold growth in a neighboring unit for weeks before anyone notices the smell.

Forest Hill's larger colonials and the frame two- and three-families found throughout Vailsburg and Weequahic add another variable: older plaster and lath assemblies absorb and release moisture slowly, so a leak that looks resolved at the surface can still be feeding growth behind the wall. Property owners often call us after a tenant complaint or a bad inspection report rather than after an obvious leak, which is exactly the scenario testing is built for.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Newark

We start with a visual walkthrough and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters, paying particular attention to exterior walls facing the street side in older brick buildings, where freeze-thaw cycles and aging mortar joints let moisture in gradually rather than all at once. Where visible growth or elevated moisture readings suggest a problem, we collect air samples (spore trap) and, where relevant, surface or tape-lift samples from suspect materials. Samples go to a third-party lab, and results typically come back within a few business days with spore counts and species identification compared against an outdoor control sample taken on-site.

The written report documents moisture source, affected materials, and sample results in a format that's usable for insurance claims, landlord-tenant disputes, or a property sale. We work from Fair Lawn across Union, Bergen, and Essex counties, so Newark jobs are a routine part of the coverage area rather than an outlier.

## Newark Insurance & Property Manager Coordination

A large share of Newark's mold calls come from landlords and property managers managing multi-unit buildings, not single-family homeowners, and that changes what the report needs to do. Insurance carriers and attorneys reviewing a habitability complaint want a report that separates moisture intrusion from pre-existing conditions and documents sampling methodology clearly. We build our reports to stand up to that scrutiny, and where a claim is tied to a known water event, sewage backup, storm damage, we coordinate timing with whatever mitigation work is already underway so the testing reflects current conditions rather than a stale snapshot.

## Local note

In Ironbound basement apartments near the Passaic River, we routinely find moisture readings elevated along the base of masonry walls even in units with no recent flood history, because groundwater and tidal influence keep the water table close to the surface in that stretch of the city. Testing there has to distinguish ongoing capillary moisture from an active leak, which is why we always pull a baseline moisture reading before deciding whether sampling is even warranted.
