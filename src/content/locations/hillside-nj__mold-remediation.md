## Why Hillside Basements Grow Mold

Most of Hillside was built out between the 1920s and the 1950s, with colonials, two-families, and postwar capes that still run on galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains. Those systems fail with age, and when they fail below grade, the water goes into a finished or semi-finished basement where it has nowhere to evaporate. The Elizabeth River crossing the eastern side of town adds a second pressure point: during heavy rain, low-lying streets flood and sewer lines back up into basements, a pattern that got especially bad during Ida in 2021 and still shows up every time a summer storm stalls over the area. Add a sump pump that quietly fails during a downpour, which is one of our steadier calls here, and you have the conditions mold needs: standing or wicking moisture, a finished space with carpet or paneling to colonize, and 24 to 48 hours of nobody checking.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hillside

We start by identifying the moisture source, because remediating mold without fixing why it grew just buys you a repeat visit. In an older Hillside home near Hillside Center or Saybrook, that often means checking where galvanized pipe meets newer PVC, or where a cast-iron drain has pitted through. Once the source is addressed, we isolate the work area with physical containment and negative air pressure so spores don't travel into the rest of the house while materials are removed, drying is done per the IICRC S500 standard for moisture control, and affected porous materials like drywall, carpet backing, and insulation are removed rather than treated in place. HEPA air scrubbing and a final surface cleaning finish the job before we clear containment.
