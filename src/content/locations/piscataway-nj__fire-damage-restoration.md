## Why Piscataway Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Piscataway's housing stock splits fairly evenly between postwar capes and split-levels in neighborhoods like Possumtown and New Market, and newer townhome and condo clusters such as Society Hill. The older homes often carry original electrical panels and knob-and-tube remnants that haven't kept pace with modern appliance loads, which is a common ignition source we see paired with plaster-and-lath construction that absorbs smoke residue deeper than drywall. The newer attached developments bring a different risk: shared walls and common attic space mean a fire in one unit can push smoke and heat into a neighbor's unit through party-wall penetrations, which is where HOA notification and multi-unit coordination come into play. Add in the Stelton commercial corridor and the warehouse and lab space near Rutgers' Busch and Livingston campuses, where overnight electrical and equipment fires in commercial buildings create their own set of ventilation and sprinkler-discharge cleanup challenges, and Piscataway ends up with a genuinely mixed fire-loss profile rather than one dominant pattern.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Piscataway

We start with board-up and tarping to secure the structure, since an open roof or blown-out window in a township this close to the Raritan River invites rain and wildlife into an already compromised building. From there we shut down HVAC systems early, because older split-levels often run on a single return duct that will recirculate soot through every room in the house if it's left running. Char and soot removal follows IICRC-based cleaning protocols calibrated to the surface: dry sponging and HEPA vacuuming on plaster and masonry, wet cleaning on sealed surfaces, and controlled demolition only where char has compromised structural integrity. Odor work typically runs in parallel using thermal fogging and sealing, since smoke odor in older homes tends to migrate into wall cavities faster than it does in newer, tighter construction. Where fire suppression water has saturated subfloor or basement framing, that moisture gets tracked and dried separately from the smoke and char work, not folded into the same pass.
