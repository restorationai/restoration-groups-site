## Why Hoboken Properties See Sewage Backups

Hoboken sits below the tide line of what was once a marsh along the Hudson, and the city's combined sewer system carries both stormwater and sanitary waste in the same pipes. During heavy rain, the kind that overwhelmed the system during Superstorm Sandy in 2012 and again with Ida in 2021, that combined flow can exceed capacity and push wastewater backward through floor drains, laundry lines, and basement toilets rather than out to the Hudson. Dense rowhomes and brownstones throughout Downtown Hoboken and Southwest Hoboken often have garden-level units sitting at or below street grade, which puts them directly in the path of any backup. Newer high-rises along the Waterfront generally fare better thanks to modern check valves and pumped drainage, but shared stacks in older party-wall buildings near Castle Point can carry a single unit's sewer failure into neighboring basements.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Hoboken

We start by containing the affected area and identifying the category of contamination, since raw sewage is treated differently from a gray-water backup under the IICRC S500 standard. Crews extract standing wastewater, remove saturated porous materials like carpet pad, baseboard, and drywall that can't be sanitized, and apply EPA-registered antimicrobial treatments to framing and subfloor. In a pre-war brownstone, that often means pulling plaster or lath that's held contamination longer than modern drywall would, and checking the crawl space or sub-basement common to these older foundations for wicking. Once materials are removed and surfaces treated, we verify drying with moisture meters before closing out the job, documenting each step for your insurance file.

## Hoboken Insurance & HOA Coordination

A meaningful share of Hoboken's housing stock is condo-converted brownstones and walk-ups, which means a sewage backup in one unit frequently involves a building-wide HOA or condo association, not just a single homeowner's policy. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings that both an individual carrier and a building's master policy can use, and we coordinate scheduling with property managers along the Washington Street business district corridor where ground-floor commercial tenants and upper-floor residential units sometimes share the same stack. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude sewer backup unless a rider was added, so we make sure the documentation is thorough enough to support whichever coverage applies.
