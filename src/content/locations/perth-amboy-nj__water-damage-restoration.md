## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Perth Amboy

We start with a moisture assessment that accounts for Perth Amboy's older construction, not just surface readings. In a pre-war frame house, plaster can read dry on the surface while the lath behind it is still saturated, so we check subfloor and wall cavities before calling a room dry. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water out of basements and crawl spaces common throughout Chase Avenue and the older downtown blocks. Then we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the humidity Perth Amboy gets off the bay, since coastal moisture in the air slows evaporation compared to drier inland towns. We monitor daily with moisture meters and adjust airflow and dehumidification until the readings confirm the structure is actually dry, not just visually dry, following IICRC S500 drying standards throughout.

## Perth Amboy Insurance Coordination

A lot of the water damage we see in Perth Amboy's multifamily buildings starts as a tenant-to-tenant claim: a failed appliance line or a cracked drain pipe on an upper floor sends water down into the unit below. That creates two policies and sometimes two carriers on a single loss, so we document the source, the path of the water, and the affected materials separately for each unit from the first visit. For flood-zone properties near the Waterfront or close to Bayview Park, we also see claims that cross between flood policies and standard homeowners coverage, and we provide the photos and moisture logs either adjuster needs to process the claim without extra site visits.

## Local note

Older Perth Amboy basements, especially in the frame houses built before the mid-1900s, often have fieldstone or parged masonry foundations rather than poured concrete. Water finds its way through these foundations differently than it does through a modern slab, seeping along mortar joints instead of pooling in one visible spot, which means a basement can look only lightly damp on the surface while the foundation wall itself is holding significant moisture. We check wall cavities and the base of these foundations specifically on older jobs rather than relying on a single floor reading, because stopping there misses where the water is actually sitting.
