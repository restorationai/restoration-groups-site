## Why Somerville Properties See Fire Damage Risks

Somerville's housing stock, Victorians, colonials, and frame two-families clustered around Downtown, East Main, Gaston Avenue, and Mercer Street, was largely built before modern electrical code existed. Knob-and-tube remnants, undersized panels carrying modern appliance loads, and space heaters pressed into service in uninsulated additions are common ignition points we see across the borough. Downtown and the Main Street business district add a second pattern: connected storefronts with shared attic or cockloft space mean a fire that starts behind one restaurant's kitchen hood can produce smoke damage in the shop two doors down before anyone smells it. Newer mid-rise residential buildings near the center of town bring their own exposure, since a unit fire or sprinkler discharge can push smoke odor and water damage through shared stairwells and HVAC chases to units that never saw flame.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Somerville

We start with a structural walkthrough to separate what's actually compromised from what only looks bad under soot. In Somerville's older homes that means checking whether plaster and lath held up structurally even where surface char is heavy, since replacing sound plaster adds cost without adding safety. Board-up and tarping come first if the fire opened a roof or window, which matters more here given how exposed Somerville's low-lying parcels near the Raritan River and Peters Brook are to wind-driven rain between the fire and the repair. From there we run HEPA air scrubbers and thermal fogging to pull smoke odor out of framing and subfloor, document everything photographically for the claim file, and sequence drywall, flooring, and finish work only after moisture and soot testing clear. On mixed-use Main Street buildings we coordinate timing with the business owner so a storefront can reopen in stages rather than waiting on the whole building.

## Somerville Insurance Coordination for Fire Claims

Fire claims in Somerville tend to fall into two buckets: single-family losses from an electrical or kitchen fire, and multi-tenant losses in Downtown storefronts or newer apartment buildings where more than one policy, and sometimes a landlord's commercial policy plus a tenant's renters policy, are involved. We photograph and itemize damage room by room, note pre-existing conditions separately from fire-caused damage, and provide the documentation adjusters need to move a claim without a second site visit. For properties near the Somerset County Courthouse and the rest of the Main Street corridor, where storefronts often share a single building envelope, we also document which damage originated in the loss unit versus which traveled from an adjacent space, since that distinction affects how a commercial carrier scopes the claim.
