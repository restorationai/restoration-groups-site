## Why East Brunswick Properties See Storm Damage Issues

East Brunswick's housing stock is mostly 1950s through 1970s ranches, split-levels, and colonials, nearly all of them built with finished basements that sit low relative to grade. When Lawrence Brook and the broader Farrington Lake watershed rise during a tropical remnant like Irene or Ida, those basements take on water first, often through foundation cracks or window wells rather than a dramatic breach. Add in mature tree canopy that lines older tracts near Tanglewood and the Village of the Golden Triangle, and a single severe wind event can bring down limbs onto roofs, siding, and fence lines across several blocks at once. Along the Route 18 retail corridor, flat commercial roofs and the scale of properties like Brunswick Square Mall mean storm damage shows up differently: ponding roof loads, skylight failures, and water tracking into drop ceilings and stockrooms long after the rain stops.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in East Brunswick

The first visit is about stopping the bleeding. That means emergency board-up and tarping for compromised roofs or broken windows, followed by water extraction if wind-driven rain or brook overflow has entered the structure. From there we document everything with photos and moisture readings, since East Brunswick claims involving brook flooding often get more carrier scrutiny than a straightforward wind claim. Drying is calibrated to the building: a finished basement in a 1960s split-level with paneled walls and a drop ceiling dries differently than a slab-on-grade townhome, so equipment placement and monitoring intervals get adjusted case by case rather than run on a fixed schedule. Debris from downed trees and damaged roofing is cleared and the property secured against further weather before we move to drying verification.

## East Brunswick Insurance & HOA Coordination

A fair amount of East Brunswick's newer housing is townhome and condo construction, which adds a layer most single-family storm claims don't have. Master insurance policies typically cover the building envelope and common roof areas, while interior contents and finishes often fall to the unit owner's HO-6 policy. We've found it saves time to identify which policy applies before adjusters get involved, and we document damage in a way that supports whichever claim path the property manager or homeowner needs to file. For single-family homes, we work directly with most major carriers and provide the documentation needed to support a storm damage claim from the start.
