## Why Paramus Commercial Properties See Water and Fire Damage

Paramus runs on two different building eras colliding. The Route 4 and Route 17 corridors, anchored by Westfield Garden State Plaza and Paramus Park, are dense with big-box retail, strip centers and office parks built with flat or low-slope roofing systems prone to ponding and membrane failure. Those roofs, combined with aging fire suppression lines in older retail buildings, generate a steady pattern of sprinkler-discharge and overnight roof-leak losses that nobody discovers until opening shift. Meanwhile, the office and light-industrial buildings tucked into West Paramus and East Paramus often sit on the same postwar-era plumbing and slab construction as the surrounding residential blocks, which means galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains that fail the same way a 1960s ranch's does, just at commercial scale. Properties closer to Van Saun County Park and the Saddle River floodplain carry an added layer of risk: heavy rain events, the kind that hit Bergen County hard during Ida in 2021, can push water into below-grade commercial space before anyone on-site realizes the parking lot is flooding.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Paramus

Commercial jobs start with a scope walk that's different from a residential one: we're mapping affected square footage across sales floors, stockrooms, server closets and mechanical rooms, not just a kitchen or basement. Extraction and structural drying follow IICRC S500 guidelines, but the equipment load is sized for the space, often meaning a larger fleet of low-grain air movers and commercial dehumidifiers running in zones so the business can keep partial operations going where it's safe to do so. For fire or smoke losses, we isolate affected HVAC runs early since shared ductwork in strip-mall and office-park buildings can carry smoke odor into unaffected units. Throughout, we photograph and log every affected material and fixture, because commercial claims tend to involve multiple stakeholders, an owner, a property manager, sometimes a tenant, all of whom want the same documentation.

## Local note

A detail that matters for commercial properties near the Route 17 corridor: many of the strip-mall and big-box buildings share common roof drains and scuppers across multiple tenant spaces. A clog or membrane failure over one unit can push water laterally across a shared roof deck and show up as a ceiling stain two or three storefronts away. When we're called to a leak in that corridor, we check adjacent units early, because the source is rarely directly above the damage.
