## Why Roselle Properties See Mold Issues

Roselle's housing stock runs mostly early-1900s through 1950s: Victorians and colonials near Chestnut Street, postwar capes further out toward East Roselle. Many were finished out below grade decades after they were built, which means the basement walls were never designed to manage vapor the way current foundations are. Add cast-iron waste lines nearing the end of their service life and sump pumps that get overworked during summer downpours, and you have a recurring pattern: water intrudes quietly, dries at the surface, and keeps feeding mold growth inside wall cavities and under flooring where nobody is looking. Ida in 2021 put water and sewage into basements across town, and plenty of those spaces never got a real moisture check after the initial cleanup. Mold can establish within 24 to 48 hours of a material staying wet, so a basement that "dried out fine" after a storm is often exactly where we find colonization during testing.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Roselle

We start with a visual walkthrough and moisture mapping, using a moisture meter and thermal imaging to trace dampness through finished walls, subfloors, and sill plates, which matters in a town where so many basements were finished after construction and hide the original masonry behind newer drywall. If conditions warrant it, we collect air samples to measure spore counts against outdoor baseline air, and we'll pull surface or tape-lift samples from any visible growth. Samples go to a third-party lab, and we walk the findings through with you in plain language, what's elevated, what's normal background, and what it means for next steps. We document everything in a format that works for insurance claims and for the independent remediation decisions that follow, since our inspection and testing is a separate, unbiased step from any remediation work.
