## Why Bayonne Properties See Faster Fire Damage Spread

Much of Bayonne's housing was built in the early twentieth century: tight-set frame two- and three-families, brick rowhomes, and postwar capes with finished basements that share wall cavities and attic space with neighboring units. That construction pattern means fire, heat, and smoke travel through balloon framing and shared party walls more readily than in newer platform-framed homes. Older knob-and-tube or early romex wiring, aging panel boxes, and space heater use during cold snaps off the Kill Van Kull are common ignition patterns we see across the peninsula. ## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Bayonne

We start with a structural and air-quality assessment, because smoke behaves differently in plaster-and-lath construction than it does in drywall. Pre-1950 plaster absorbs soot particulate deeper into the wall system, so surface cleaning alone rarely clears the odor. After securing the structure, we HEPA-vacuum and dry-chemically sponge hard surfaces, then move into thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor sitting in the framing itself. Contents in multi-family units, especially in Constable Hook and Midtown where units share stairwells and storage, get inventoried and either treated on-site or packed out, since cross-contamination between units is a real concern in attached housing. Our IICRC Certified Firm status guides how we document the loss and structure the drying and deodorization plan from there.
