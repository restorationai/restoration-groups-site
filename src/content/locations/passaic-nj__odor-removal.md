## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Passaic

We start by identifying the source material, not just the smell. A technician traces odor to its origin, whether that is water-damaged subfloor near St. Mary's General Hospital, smoke residue clinging to plaster near Pulaski Park, or sewage-contaminated insulation in a Downtown Passaic rental. Porous materials that have absorbed contamination, like drywall, carpet pad, or soft insulation, often need to be removed rather than treated, because odor molecules bond to the material itself. What remains gets cleaned and then treated with the method matched to the contamination type: thermal fogging for smoke odor that has penetrated wall cavities, hydroxyl generators where occupants or pets are still on-site since hydroxyl treatment doesn't require evacuation, and ozone treatment for sealed, unoccupied spaces where we need to break down stubborn organic odor at the molecular level. We document conditions with photos and moisture readings throughout, which matters for insurance claims tied to the original water or fire loss.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Not every odor source calls for the same tool. Ozone treatment is effective but requires a sealed, vacated space for a set period, so we schedule it carefully in occupied multi-family buildings common in Rutherford Heights and near the Prospect Park border, where a shared hallway or adjoining unit changes the plan. Hydroxyl deodorization runs safely with people and pets present, which makes it the better fit for rental units where a tenant can't relocate for a few days. Thermal fogging reproduces the way smoke particles originally traveled, pushing a dry fog into the same cracks and cavities the odor penetrated, which is often the only way to reach smoke residue trapped behind older plaster. We pair whichever method fits with HEPA air scrubbing and, where contamination involved sewage or standing floodwater, cleaning protocols consistent with IICRC S500 guidance.
