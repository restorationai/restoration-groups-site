## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Parsippany

We start by identifying the source, not just the smell. That might mean pulling baseboard in a Lake Hiawatha basement to check framing moisture, or inspecting attic insulation in a Mount Tabor cottage where smoke has settled into century-old lath and plaster. Once we know what we're dealing with, porous materials that have absorbed smoke, sewage, or mold-related odor often need to be removed rather than just treated, because fibers and wood can hold odor compounds deep in their structure. What remains gets cleaned, and then we apply the right deodorization method for the situation: thermal fogging to replicate how smoke particles originally traveled and penetrated surfaces, ozone treatment for unoccupied spaces where oxidation can work overnight, or hydroxyl generators when people or pets need to stay on-site during treatment since hydroxyl technology doesn't carry the same occupancy restrictions ozone does. We verify the result before calling a job finished rather than assuming a treatment worked.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

The right tool depends on the space and who's in it. Hydroxyl generators are our go-to for occupied homes and active offices in the Waterview district, since tenants and staff don't need to vacate during treatment. Ozone generators handle stronger, more stubborn odor loads, like a sewage backup in an older Troy Hills basement, but they require the space to be sealed and unoccupied for a set period. Thermal fogging matches smoke's own behavior, pushing a deodorizing fog into the same cracks, HVAC returns, and wall cavities where smoke residue settled, which makes it effective in fire-related jobs where surface cleaning alone leaves a residual smell behind. We pair all of it with air scrubbing and, where source materials were removed, targeted sealing to keep porous replacement material from reabsorbing odor from an adjacent, still-affected area.
