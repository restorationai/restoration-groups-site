## Why Scotch Plains Sees Repeat Storm Damage

The township's geography works against it during heavy rain. The Green Brook and Cedar Brook sit along the southern edge of town, and once they rise, the low-lying streets near the Fanwood border and parts of Southside take water onto lawns and into window wells faster than storm drains can clear it. Add in the housing stock: a lot of Scotch Plains is 1950s and '60s split-levels, ranches, and colonials with finished basements built over cast-iron drain lines and original supply piping that's now well past its intended service life. A storm that knocks out power for a sump pump, or drives water against a foundation that was never fully waterproofed, turns a finished basement into a loss in a matter of hours. Commercial properties along the Park Avenue business district and the Route 22 corridor carry a different risk profile, mostly flat or low-slope roofs that pond water and fail at seams, plus storefronts with older storm drains that back up during heavy downpours.

## Our Storm Damage Process in Scotch Plains

We start with the life-safety items: downed limbs or full trees on a roofline, exposed interior spaces, and any active water intrusion. Emergency board-up and tarping go on first to stop the bleeding, especially on the older roof pitches common near downtown Scotch Plains and around Shackamaxon. From there, crews extract standing water and pull wet flooring and drywall as needed, with drying set up and monitored to the IICRC S500 standard, which is our benchmark for verifying a structure is actually dry rather than just surface-dry. For homes with finished basements, we pay close attention to wall cavities and subfloor framing since trapped moisture behind paneling or under carpet pad is where mold gets its foothold within 24 to 48 hours. Tree and debris removal is coordinated alongside the drying work, not after it, so the property isn't sitting open to weather any longer than it has to.

## Insurance Coordination for Scotch Plains Claims

Storm claims in Scotch Plains often split into two categories that insurers treat differently: wind and tree damage versus flood or rising water. A tree through a roof near Ashbrook or Willow Grove is usually a straightforward homeowner's policy claim, but water that enters from the Green Brook overflow or from street-level flooding frequently falls under separate flood coverage, which not every property carries. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings that distinguish between these causes, because that distinction affects what a carrier will pay and how fast.
