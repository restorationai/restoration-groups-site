## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in East Orange

We start with a room-by-room assessment to separate dry smoke residue (brittle, easier to vacuum) from wet smoke residue left by smoldering synthetic materials, since each requires a different cleaning agent and approach. In older plaster-and-lath buildings, we test absorption before committing to a cleaning method; aggressive scrubbing can pull plaster off the lath, so we favor dry chemical sponges and controlled HEPA vacuuming on original surfaces first. Soft contents, upholstery, and clothing go through ultrasonic or ozone-chamber treatment off-site when on-site deodorizing isn't enough to reach fibers. HVAC systems get inspected and cleaned separately, since duct-borne soot is one of the main reasons smoke odor returns weeks after a job looks finished. We close out with thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment depending on the severity of the odor and how occupied the building will be during the process.
