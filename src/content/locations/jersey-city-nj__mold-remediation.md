## Why Jersey City Properties See Mold Issues

Jersey City is really two building stocks sharing one city, and mold shows up differently in each. The waterfront towers around Exchange Place and Newport are mostly post-1990 construction with centralized HVAC and stacked plumbing risers, so a single supply-line failure on the 14th floor can push moisture into the units below before anyone notices a drip. In the Heights, Bergen-Lafayette, and Greenville, the housing stock runs from 1890s brownstones to 1920s frame two-families, many with cellars below grade and clay or cast-iron lateral lines that back up during heavy storms. Those combined-sewer surcharges, the kind Hurricane Ida made impossible to ignore citywide, leave standing water in crawl spaces and foundations that can sit unnoticed for days. Either scenario gives mold exactly what it needs: a food source in old joists or paper-faced drywall, a stable temperature, and standing moisture.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Jersey City

We start with a moisture map, not a guess. In older Heights and Greenville properties, that means checking behind plaster and lath separately from the drywall layer above it, since the two materials hold and release moisture at different rates. In Newport and Exchange Place condos, it means documenting the specific unit and any shared wall or ceiling cavity affected, since building management typically needs a unit-by-unit record for the association's carrier. Containment goes up next, negative air pressure and physical barriers so spores don't migrate into hallways or adjoining units, which matters especially in multifamily buildings with shared HVAC returns. Affected porous material gets removed per IICRC S520 guidelines, surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated, and we run air scrubbers until clearance conditions are met before anyone moves contents back in.

## Jersey City Insurance & HOA Coordination

Condo boards along the waterfront and co-op associations in Journal Square both tend to have specific documentation requirements before they'll release funds for mold work, usually unit-by-unit moisture readings, photos at each remediation stage, and a written scope tied to the original water event. For landlord-owned rowhouses and two-families in Bergen-Lafayette and Greenville, the documentation is simpler but just as important if a tenant dispute or habitability question comes up later.
