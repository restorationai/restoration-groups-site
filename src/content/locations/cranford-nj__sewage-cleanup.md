## Why Cranford Sees Sewer Backups and Septic Overflow

Cranford earned the nickname "Venice of New Jersey" because the Rahway River runs straight through town, and that same geography is why sewage backups here are so common. During heavy rain, Cranford's storm and sanitary lines can surcharge, pushing wastewater back up through basement drains and toilets in homes nowhere near the riverbank. Blocks along the Riverside Drive area and parts of Downtown Cranford flood from the river directly, while Cranford West, Sunny Acres, and Lincoln Park East more often see sewer-driven backups when the municipal system can't keep up with volume. Add in a housing stock built mostly between the 1900s and 1940s, colonials and capes with full basements and clay or cast-iron lateral lines that shift and crack with age, and you get a town where sewage intrusion is as much a plumbing age issue as a weather issue.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Cranford

We treat every sewage call as a Category 3 water loss, meaning anything porous that absorbed contaminated water, carpet pad, baseboards, drywall below the flood line, often can't be saved and has to come out rather than be dried in place. Our crew extracts standing wastewater, removes and bags contaminated materials for disposal, then applies EPA-registered antimicrobials to framing, subfloor, and masonry that can be sanitized and dried per the IICRC S500 standard. In a Cranford basement that's flooded more than once, we also check for residual contamination inside wall cavities from a prior Floyd, Irene, or Ida-era event, since sewage that dried without full sanitization can leave odor and bacterial residue years later. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in the subfloor and framing confirm the space is dry, not just when the surface looks it.

## Cranford Insurance and Documentation

Many Cranford homeowners have filed a flood or sewer backup claim before, and they know adjusters want proof, not just a clean basement.
