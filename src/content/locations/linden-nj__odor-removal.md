## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Linden

We start by identifying what's actually generating the smell, not just masking it. That means pulling baseboards or probing drywall near grade in finished basements, checking HVAC returns for smoke residue circulated during a fire event, and testing porous materials like subfloor and insulation that may need to come out rather than be treated. Once the source is addressed, we apply hydroxyl generators in occupied homes where people and pets are present, since hydroxyl treatment doesn't require evacuation the way ozone does. For vacant properties, unoccupied commercial space near the industrial corridor, or jobs with heavy smoke penetration, ozone treatment and thermal fogging get deployed together, matching the fog's carrier solvent to the odor type, whether that's smoke, decomposition, or mildew.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Thermal fogging works by vaporizing a deodorizing agent into a fog fine enough to follow smoke's own path into cracks, wall cavities, and fabric fibers, which is why it's effective on the wood-paneled rec rooms still common in Linden's older basements. Hydroxyl generators produce the same radical compounds sunlight does outdoors, breaking down odor molecules instead of covering them, and they run safely for days in a home where a family is still living upstairs. Ozone is reserved for situations where nothing organic is left in the space to react badly with it, typically a vacant unit or a commercial space that can be sealed off overnight.
