## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Roselle

The first move is always stopping active intrusion: emergency board-up and tarping on a damaged roof or broken window, sandbagging or pumping where surface water is still rising, and shutting off power to any circuit that's been wet. From there we extract standing water, pull saturated flooring and drywall that won't dry in place, and set up air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the room, not a generic rule of thumb. Cast-iron waste lines and galvanized supply pipe are still common in Roselle's older homes, and when a storm surcharges the sewer system, that old plumbing is more likely to back up under basement slabs than newer PVC. We check for that early rather than assuming the water is only rainwater, because sewage-contaminated water changes how materials get handled and what can be dried versus what has to come out. Every loss is documented with moisture readings and photos as we go, both for the drying log and for your insurance file.

## Local note

One thing that surprises a lot of homeowners here: the storm damage and the sewer backup often aren't the same event on paper, even though they happen in the same basement on the same night. If the Elizabeth River pushes water up through a floor drain while wind is still tearing at the roof upstairs, that's technically two different causes of loss, and carriers may handle them under different endorsements.
