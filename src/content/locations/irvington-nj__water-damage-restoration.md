## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Irvington

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable pumps before it has a chance to wick further up plaster walls or under subflooring. In Irvington's older buildings, that wicking matters more than in newer construction: plaster and horsehair lath hold moisture longer than drywall and release it slowly, so a wall that reads dry at the surface can still be saturated behind it. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find that hidden saturation rather than guessing from what's visible. Once the water is out, we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and material mix, and we track drying progress daily against the IICRC S500 standard rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule.
