## Why Cranford Floods the Way It Does

Cranford earned its "Venice of New Jersey" nickname honestly, and that same river charm is why the town carries the most serious repeat-flood history in Union County. Floyd, Irene, and Ida each put whole blocks under water, and the damage splits into two distinct patterns. Along the Riverside Drive area, water comes straight from the Rahway River overtopping its banks. Everywhere else, including Downtown Cranford and the blocks around Nomahegan Park, the problem is storm sewers that simply can't move volume fast enough, so water backs up through floor drains and window wells instead. Add in a housing stock that's mostly 1900s to 1940s construction with full, unfinished or partially finished basements, and you have a town where standing water has a lot of old joists, plaster, and masonry to soak into before anyone calls for help.

## Our Emergency Water Removal Process in Cranford

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on basement access, which matters on the narrower lots common in Cranford West and Lincoln Park East. From there we moisture-map the structure, because older lath-and-plaster walls hold water differently than drywall and can read deceptively dry at the surface while staying saturated underneath. In homes near the river, we also check crawl spaces and sill plates for wicking, since a high water table can keep feeding moisture into a foundation even after the visible water is gone. Drying equipment stays in place and gets monitored daily until readings confirm the structure, not just the surface, is dry per IICRC S500 guidelines.

## Cranford Insurance & NFIP Documentation

A lot of Cranford homeowners, especially in flood-prone pockets near the Rahway River Parkway, have been through this before and carry NFIP flood policies alongside their homeowner's coverage. That history makes documentation non-negotiable. We photograph affected materials before extraction begins, log moisture readings throughout drying, and keep records in a format that works whether you're filing with the NFIP, a private flood rider, or a standard carrier for storm-driven sewer backup. Good documentation is often the difference between a claim that moves and one that stalls over what was actually wet and for how long.
