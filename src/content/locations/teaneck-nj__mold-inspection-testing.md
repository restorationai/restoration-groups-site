Teaneck's housing stock skews heavily toward 1920s-40s brick Tudors, colonials, and center-hall homes, many of them in West Englewood, The Grange, and the Whittier section, with finished basements added decades after original construction. ## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Teaneck

An inspection starts with a visual and moisture-mapping walkthrough, checking rim joists, below-grade walls, and HVAC returns, since these are the spots where Teaneck's older homes tend to trap humidity. We use a moisture meter to find elevated readings behind baseboards and around foundation walls, which in pre-1950 construction often means checking both the finished surface and the original masonry behind it, since plaster and old block hold moisture differently than modern drywall. Where conditions suggest a problem, air samples are taken both inside the suspect area and from an unaffected reference point in the home, a comparison that's especially useful in center-hall colonials near the Bryant School area where finished basements sit directly below living space. Surface swabs are collected from any visible growth for lab analysis. Results typically come back from the lab within a few business days, and we walk homeowners through what the spore counts and species findings actually mean before recommending next steps, including when mold remediation is warranted and when it isn't.

## Teaneck Insurance & HOA Coordination

Mold claims in Teaneck often trace back to a covered water event, a burst supply line, a sewage backup, or storm flooding, rather than mold itself, since most policies treat long-term moisture and humidity as a maintenance issue rather than a covered peril. That makes the documentation from an inspection valuable: clear photos, moisture readings, and lab results give an adjuster something concrete to tie back to the triggering event, particularly for finished basements that flooded during Ida and were only recently reopened or sold. We provide written reports that homeowners can hand directly to their carrier or share with a property manager handling a multi-unit building near the Cedar Lane commercial district.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Testing relies on calibrated moisture meters, thermal imaging to spot temperature differentials that indicate trapped moisture behind finished surfaces, and both air and surface sampling collected under controlled conditions. Samples go to an independent lab for species identification and spore count, keeping the testing separate from any remediation recommendation, which protects the homeowner from a conflict of interest between diagnosis and treatment.
