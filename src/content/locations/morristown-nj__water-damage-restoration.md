## Why Morristown Properties See Water Damage Issues

Morristown's housing stock is part of what makes water losses here different from a newer suburb. Around The Green and through the Historic District, you've got Victorians, colonials, and brick multifamily buildings dating to the 1800s and early 1900s, many still running galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks into finished basements. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, and when it finally fails, it's often a slow weep behind a wall for weeks before a full rupture. Cast iron cracks along its length rather than blowing a clean hole, which means sewage and gray water backups tend to spread wider than homeowners expect.

The Whippany River runs along town, and low-lying parcels near it took on water during Ida's remnants in 2021. That storm pattern isn't a one-time event for this area. Heavy, fast-moving rain events have become a regular part of the New Jersey climate picture, and basements in Speedwell and the lower blocks near Franklin Corner are the first to show it. On the commercial side, the Speedwell Avenue and South Street corridors, plus the restaurant row around the Green and the properties near Morristown Medical Center, generate a steady run of storefront leaks, roof failures, and overnight losses where water sits for hours before anyone finds it. The newer luxury apartment towers downtown add a different failure mode: shared supply lines and stacked units mean one failed braided hose on floor nine can mean water damage on floors one through eight by morning.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Morristown

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable units before anything else, because water sitting on original hardwood or century-old subfloor does more damage per hour than it would on modern plywood. From there we moisture-map the structure with meters and thermal imaging, which matters more in Morristown's older buildings than almost anywhere: plaster walls and true dimensional lumber hold and release moisture on a different timeline than drywall and engineered joists, so a wall that reads dry at the surface can still be saturated behind the lath.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to match the specific assembly, not a generic grid. Cauldwell and Little Italy homes with finished basements get particular attention to wall cavities and sill plates, since that's where cast-iron drain failures tend to hide moisture long after the visible puddle is gone. For commercial spaces along South Street or near the Green, we work around operating hours where we can, because a restaurant kitchen that's dry by close of business is a restaurant that doesn't lose a second night of revenue.
