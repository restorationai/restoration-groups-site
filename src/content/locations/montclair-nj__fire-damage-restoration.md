## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Montclair

We start with board-up and tarping to secure the structure, which matters more here than in flatter towns because many Montclair roofs still carry original slate that's brittle and heavy to work around safely. From there we assess soot penetration room by room. Plaster and wood lath hold smoke odor differently than drywall, so a kitchen fire in a 1910 Tudor often needs deeper cleaning of wall cavities and exposed framing than the same fire would in a 1995 build. We remove charred and smoke-damaged materials, run air scrubbers and ozone or hydroxyl treatment for odor, and dry the structure using moisture readings rather than a fixed schedule, since old plaster and horsehair lath release trapped moisture more slowly than modern materials. Contents that can be saved, furniture, textiles, photographs, go through our pack-out and cleaning process off-site rather than in a smoke-filled home.
