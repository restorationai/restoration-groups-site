## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Clark

Flood water in a Clark basement is rarely clean, even when it starts as rainwater. By the time it's pooled against a foundation wall or backed up through a floor drain, it's picked up groundwater, lawn chemicals, or sewage, and we treat it accordingly. Extraction comes first with truck-mounted pumps, followed by moisture mapping of the slab, framing, and any finished walls to find out how far the water traveled behind drywall and under flooring. Clark's finished basements usually mean carpet pad, paneling, or drop ceilings sitting directly on or near a slab that was never meant to manage standing water, so we pull saturated materials early rather than trying to dry around them. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying, with air movers and dehumidification sized to the basement's square footage and the amount of porous material still in place. Where contamination is suspected, we sanitize affected surfaces before drying equipment goes in, not after.
