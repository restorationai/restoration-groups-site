## Why Brooklyn Properties See Board-Up & Tarping Emergencies

The borough's housing stock works against it during severe weather. Brownstones in Park Slope and Brooklyn Heights were built with coal chutes, garden-level entries, and cornices that catch wind uplift, and a lot of that roofing has been re-covered multiple times without full tear-off, so one bad gust or ice buildup can open a seam that's been failing quietly for years. Coastal stretches from Canarsie down toward the Coney Island boardwalk carry real storm-surge exposure that long-time residents still measure against Sandy, and a nor'easter can take out a window or door in minutes. Fire and smoke events add another layer: a kitchen or electrical fire that burns through a roof or wall leaves an opening that needs to be sealed the same day, both to stop weather intrusion and to keep the structure secure until an adjuster or engineer signs off.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Brooklyn

We start by assessing what actually failed, whether it's a blown-out window, a fire-damaged section of roof deck, or a door frame that's no longer square after storm impact. Plywood goes up over windows and doors sized and fastened to the actual opening rather than a generic panel, which matters on older rowhouses where window openings were never standardized. Roof tarping uses industrial-grade poly sheeting battened down with furring strips, not just staples, because wind off the harbor and the open sightlines near the Brooklyn Bridge approach can rip a loosely anchored tarp apart in a single night. Every board-up and tarp job gets photographed before and after, with measurements, since that documentation is what insurers and building management companies expect to see before they'll authorize further work.
