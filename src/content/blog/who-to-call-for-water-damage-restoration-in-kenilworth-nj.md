That said, the right first call depends on what is happening right now:

- **Water is still actively flowing** (burst pipe, failed supply line, running appliance): Call a licensed plumber first to shut off the source. Then call The Restoration Group. Extraction cannot outrun an open supply line.
- **Electrical hazard** (outlets submerged, panel near standing water, sparking): Call 911 or your utility provider (PSE&G for most of Kenilworth) before anyone enters the space. Water and live electricity is a life-safety issue, not a restoration call.
- **Gas odor**: Leave immediately, call 911 and PSE&G from outside. Do not re-enter until cleared.
- **Sewage backup** (dark water, sewage smell, toilet overflow): This is [Category 3 water](https://www.iicrc.org) under the IICRC S500 standard, meaning it carries pathogens. Call The Restoration Group directly. Do not attempt to clean it yourself.
- **No active hazard, water source is stopped**: Call The Restoration Group at **(855) 650-7422** immediately. Every hour of delay allows moisture to migrate further into framing, insulation, and subflooring.

## When Should I Call My Insurance Company?

Call your insurance company after you call the restoration company, not before. The Restoration Group can begin mitigation immediately and will generate the moisture readings, photo documentation, and written scope that your adjuster needs. Starting mitigation fast also protects your claim: most homeowners policies require the policyholder to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Waiting for adjuster approval before extracting water can result in a denied claim for secondary damage.

3. **Assessment and moisture mapping**: The crew uses calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to find where water has traveled, including inside walls and under flooring. This is not guesswork. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) defines the protocols they follow.
4. **Water extraction**: Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water first. This step is measured in minutes, not hours.

6. **Daily monitoring**: Crews return to read moisture levels and adjust equipment until the structure reaches drying goals. This is what prevents mold. The [EPA notes](https://www.epa.gov/mold) that mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event.
7. **Documentation for your insurer**: Every moisture reading, every photo, every equipment log is captured and formatted for your adjuster. The Restoration Group handles this as part of the job, not as an add-on.
8. **Written scope before any reconstruction begins**: No work starts without a written scope you approve. That scope is what your insurer reimburses against.
