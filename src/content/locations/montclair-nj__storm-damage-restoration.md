## Why Montclair Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Montclair sits on a slope that runs down from the Watchung Mountains toward the Third River (the Yantacaw), and that elevation change is exactly why storm damage here doesn't look the same street to street. Up near Upper Montclair, wind and falling limbs do most of the damage: slate roofs shed tiles, old chimneys crack, and large oaks come down on detached garages. Lower ground in Frog Hollow and the South End takes on water instead, as storm sewers backed up by the Third River push flow back into basements and crawl spaces. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Montclair

We start with a structural walk-through, checking roof lines, soffits, and foundation walls for the kind of damage that's easy to miss from the street: a cracked slate tile, a lifted flashing seam, hairline cracks in old stucco or stone foundations common in Montclair's early-1900s Tudors and center-hall colonials. If water has entered, extraction comes first, followed by moisture mapping of plaster walls and subfloor, since plaster behaves differently than drywall and holds moisture longer than it shows on a surface reading. Downed limbs or compromised roof sections get boarded up or tarped the same visit to stop further intrusion. From there we document everything for your adjuster, set drying equipment calibrated to the room's construction (finished basements dry differently than open crawl spaces), and track moisture levels daily until the structure reads dry per IICRC S500 guidelines.
