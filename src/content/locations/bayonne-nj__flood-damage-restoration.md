## Why Bayonne Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Bayonne's housing stock is part of the problem. Much of the city was built out in the early 1900s with two- and three-family frame homes, brick rowhomes, and postwar capes, many of which were later finished out with basement living space over cast-iron drain lines and original supply plumbing. That plumbing is failing on a predictable schedule now, and when it fails during a heavy rain event, the basement has nowhere for water to go. Low-lying blocks in Constable Hook and the waterfront streets near Newark Bay carry the added exposure of storm surge, a risk the city saw firsthand during Sandy in 2012 when industrial and residential blocks alike took on water. Tight rental stock around Uptown and Midtown also means a single pipe failure or flooded unit can displace multiple tenants at once, and basements that stay damp even a few days start growing mold against exposed foundation walls.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Bayonne

The first visit is about stopping the loss, not just documenting it. Crews pull standing water with truck-mounted extraction, pull baseboards and flooring where moisture has tracked behind finish materials, and check crawl spaces and sill plates for wicking into framing, which is common in Bayonne's older foundations. Because so much of the city's basement square footage was finished after the original build, we pay close attention to what's behind the drywall rather than just what's visible: insulation batts, sill plates, and subfloor over cast-iron drains can hold water long after the surface looks dry. Drying is monitored with moisture meters and logged against the IICRC S500 standard until readings confirm the structure is actually dry, not just dry to the touch. If the loss involves sewage backup from an overwhelmed line, that work is scoped and sanitized separately under our sewage cleanup process rather than treated as a standard water loss.
