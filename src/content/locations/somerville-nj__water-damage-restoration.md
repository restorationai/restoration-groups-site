## Why Somerville Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of Somerville's housing predates 1950, which means galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out and cast-iron drain stacks that crack quietly for years before they fail outright. Many of these homes, especially around East Main and Fairview, have finished basements added decades after the original build, so a slow leak or a backed-up drain can sit inside wall cavities and under laminate flooring long before anyone notices a smell. Low-lying parcels near the Raritan River and Peters Brook carry a different risk entirely: Somerville took on floodwater during Ida in 2021 and again during Floyd and Irene in earlier decades, and basements in those stretches still take on water during heavy regional rain events even without a direct river overflow. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Somerville

The first hour is about finding where the water actually went, not just where it's visible. In Somerville's older homes we're checking behind plaster and lath, inside finished basement stud walls, and around cast-iron cleanouts, because moisture readings at the surface routinely understate what's happening inside the assembly. We extract standing water first, then set containment and air movers calibrated to the structure: plaster and masonry hold and release moisture more slowly than drywall, so a Victorian on Mercer Street or Gaston Avenue may need a longer drying cycle than a newer build even if the water volume was smaller. Moisture mapping and daily monitoring follow IICRC S500 drying guidelines, and we document readings at each visit so there's a clear record for the insurance file. Where cast-iron drains or galvanized supply lines were the source, we flag the condition for the homeowner's plumber before we close out drying, since restoring the finish without addressing the pipe just restarts the clock.
