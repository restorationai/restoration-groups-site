## Why Hoboken Properties See Fire Damage Spread Between Units

Most of Hoboken's housing stock, particularly in Uptown, Midtown, and the blocks around Castle Point, is nineteenth- and early twentieth-century brick rowhome construction built with common party walls and, in many cases, balloon framing. That framing method leaves continuous open stud cavities running from the basement to the roofline, which means a fire that starts in one unit can travel vertically and laterally into a neighbor's walls long before anyone smells smoke on the other side. Multi-unit buildings near the Washington Street business district add another layer: ground-floor restaurants and retail with commercial kitchen exhaust and sprinkler systems that, when they activate, leave both fire and water damage to sort out. Near Stevens Institute of Technology, a dense concentration of student rentals and converted multi-families means fire and smoke events often affect several leased units at once, each with a different landlord or tenant insurance policy.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hoboken

Once the scene is released, we start with emergency board-up and tarping to secure broken windows, damaged roofing, or a compromised party wall, which matters most on attached rowhomes where an open gap lets weather and odor migrate straight into the unit next door. From there we assess soot and smoke penetration room by room, since soot behaves differently on plaster, exposed brick, and the painted trim common in older Hoboken interiors than it does on modern drywall. Any water used during suppression gets extracted and the structure is dried following the IICRC S500 standard before we move into soot removal and deodorization. Salvageable contents, furniture, textiles, electronics, are documented and either cleaned on-site or packed out for off-site restoration when a unit needs to stay closed up for odor treatment or structural drying.

## Local note

In the older attached rowhomes around Downtown and Uptown Hoboken, the shared attic or "cockloft" above a row of connected units is often one continuous open space with no firestopping between addresses. Even when flames stay confined to a single unit, smoke routinely banks into that cockloft and settles into the attics of two or three neighboring homes, which is why a fire damage assessment on a rowhome block usually needs to include the adjoining units, not just the one that burned.
