## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Newark

We start by identifying what's actually generating the odor rather than guessing based on what it smells like. That means pulling baseboards, checking under subflooring, and inspecting ductwork in the pre-war buildings common around Downtown Newark and University Heights, where decades of paint layers and plaster can trap odor sources that a surface cleaning never reaches. Once we've found the source material, whether it's charred framing, sewage-contaminated subfloor, or smoke-saturated insulation, we remove or treat it directly. Hydroxyl generators handle occupied buildings where residents or tenants need to stay on-site, since hydroxyl treatment doesn't require evacuation the way ozone does. For vacant units or post-fire units with heavier smoke odor, we use ozone treatment and thermal fogging to drive deodorizing agents into the same porous materials, wood, plaster, fabric, that absorbed the original odor. Sealing and encapsulating treated framing is often the last step on fire jobs, locking in the treatment before any finish work begins.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Which tool we reach for depends on the building and the odor source. Thermal fogging works well on smoke odor in multifamily units because the fog particles mimic how smoke itself traveled, reaching the same cracks and cavities. Ozone treatment is effective but requires an unoccupied space for a set period, which matters in Newark's dense rental stock where full-building vacancy isn't always possible. Hydroxyl generators run safely around people, pets, and plants, making them the better fit for occupied apartment buildings near Prudential Center and NJPAC where turnover and tenant schedules don't allow for a multi-day vacate. We pair whichever treatment fits with HEPA air scrubbing to pull particulate out of circulation while the deodorizing agents work on the materials themselves.
