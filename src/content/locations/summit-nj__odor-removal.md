## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Summit

We start by identifying the odor's actual source rather than treating the smell itself, because deodorizing over an active moisture or sewage problem never holds. That means moisture mapping behind plaster and under original hardwood, especially in the deep finished basements common throughout Summit, where a reading that looks dry at the surface can still be saturated a few inches in. Once the source is addressed, we match the deodorization method to the material: thermal fogging for smoke residue that has worked into porous wood and fabric, hydroxyl deodorization in occupied living spaces where people or pets are present during treatment, and ozone treatment reserved for unoccupied areas like a sealed basement or garage, since ozone requires the space to be vacated during the cycle. On jobs where the home has period millwork, built-ins, or hardwood that the family wants preserved for the finish carpenter rather than replaced, we document the original material condition before treatment so nothing gets second-guessed later.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Hydroxyl generators do the slower, gentler work in rooms where furniture, rugs, or upholstered pieces need to stay in place, breaking down odor molecules without the ventilation requirements ozone needs. Thermal foggers push a deodorizing agent into the same cracks and fibers that smoke particles reached, which matters in homes with original plaster and wood trim where the odor isn't sitting on the surface, it's inside the material. For sewage-related odor from a lateral backup, sanitization comes first and deodorization follows once the affected subfloor and framing have been treated and dried. We calibrate dwell time and equipment placement to the room, not a standard checklist, because a sealed 1920s sunporch behaves differently than an open finished basement.
