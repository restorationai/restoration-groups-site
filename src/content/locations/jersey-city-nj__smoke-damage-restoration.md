## Why Jersey City Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

Jersey City's housing stock creates two very different smoke problems. In the high-rise towers around Newport and the Exchange Place waterfront, shared HVAC returns and pressurized stairwells can pull smoke from a single unit fire across multiple floors before anyone calls it in, meaning a fire that started in one condo can leave soot film on contents and finishes several units away. In the Heights, Bergen-Lafayette, and Greenville, the building stock runs older, often 1890s to 1920s frame construction with plaster walls, original wood trim, and balloon-frame cavities that let smoke travel vertically between floors in ways modern platform framing doesn't. Plaster and old wood absorb smoke odor more readily than drywall and release it slowly, which is why a rowhouse that looks fine two days after a fire can still smell strongly a week later if the sub-surface layers weren't treated.

Jersey City has also had its share of regional wildfire smoke events in recent summers, when Canadian wildfire haze settled over the metro area for days at a time. That kind of airborne smoke doesn't come from a fire on the property, but it still infiltrates through window gaps, roof vents, and older HVAC systems, leaving a fine particulate film on interior surfaces and a smell that lingers in soft goods, drapery, and upholstery long after the sky clears.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Jersey City

We start with a walkthrough to classify the type of smoke (protein, synthetic, or wet smoke from a slow-burning fire), because each reacts differently to cleaning agents and each sits on surfaces differently. From there the work typically moves through HVAC and duct inspection, since recirculated air is often the reason smoke odor shows up in rooms far from the fire origin, then surface cleaning calibrated to the material, dry sponge and chemical sponge methods on plaster and painted trim, solvent-based cleaning on hard surfaces, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize odor at the molecular level rather than mask it. Contents that can be saved get cleaned and deodorized off-site when the loss is significant enough to warrant it. We document every step with photos, because both condo association insurers and individual homeowner policies want a clear record of what was affected and what was done.

## Jersey City Insurance & HOA Coordination

Condo buildings at Newport and Exchange Place usually carry a master policy through the association plus individual unit owner policies, and a single fire can trigger both. Association property managers typically need unit-by-unit documentation (moisture and smoke readings, photos, a scope per unit) before their carrier will release funds, and we build our reporting around that requirement rather than treating the building as one undifferentiated loss. For landlords and multi-family owners in Bergen-Lafayette, Greenville, and the Heights, we document pre-existing condition versus fire-related damage carefully, since older properties often already show wear that an adjuster needs separated from the current loss.
