## Why Woodbridge Properties See Mold Growth

Much of Woodbridge Proper, Fords, and Colonia was built out with capes, split-levels, and colonials from the 1950s and 60s, many since finished out below grade with drywall over masonry foundation walls. Those walls wick ground moisture slowly and steadily, and in a finished basement that moisture has nowhere to go but into the paper facing of the drywall. Add in supply lines and drain stacks original to the house, and a slow pinhole leak behind a vanity or washing machine can run undetected for weeks. In the lower-lying sections near Sewaren and Port Reading, tidal creek flooding and storm surge push groundwater up through foundation joints even without a direct storm hit, which is a different mechanism than a roof leak and it changes where we look first.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Woodbridge

We start with a visual and moisture-mapping walkthrough, using a moisture meter on baseboards, framing, and subfloor near any known wet history, plus a borescope where we need to see inside a wall cavity without opening it up. If conditions warrant, we collect air samples and sometimes surface tape lifts, which go to an independent lab for species identification and spore counts. For a Fords or Avenel split-level with a finished basement, that usually means sampling both the suspect area and a baseline room upstairs, so the lab report can show whether spore counts indoors are actually elevated compared to outdoor air, not just present. Results typically come back from the lab within a few business days, and we walk the report through with you line by line rather than just emailing a PDF.

Homes within a few blocks of the Woodbridge train station and the older sections of Woodbridge Proper often still have the original cast-iron drain stack running through a finished basement wall.
