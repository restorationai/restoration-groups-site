## Why Sewage Backups Are a Recurring Problem in Elizabeth

Elizabeth runs on an aging combined-sewer system in much of the older city grid, meaning stormwater and sanitary sewage share the same pipe during a heavy rain event. When that system surcharges, the path of least resistance is often a basement floor drain or a low-lying toilet, especially in Elizabethport and Bayway, where tidal influence from the Elizabeth River and Arthur Kill adds backpressure during storm surge or even a hard nor'easter. Add in the city's stock of early-1900s two- and three-family frame homes, where a single sewer lateral often serves multiple units stacked close together, and one blockage or municipal surcharge can contaminate more than one household's living space at once. Cast-iron laterals from that era are also prone to root intrusion and pipe collapse, which turns an ordinary slow drain into a full backup with little warning.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Elizabeth

Black water carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites, so the process starts with containment, not demolition. We isolate the affected area, extract standing sewage with truck-mounted or portable pumps, and remove porous materials that can't be sanitized, carpet pad, drywall below the flood line, baseboard. Hard surfaces and framing members that can be saved are cleaned, treated with an EPA-registered disinfectant, and dried in place using the same moisture-mapping approach we'd use on a clean-water loss, because sewage-affected structural wood still has to come down to a safe moisture content before anything gets closed back up. In multifamily buildings common in Peterstown and Elmora, we document which units were affected separately, since landlords usually need that paperwork split out for insurance and for tenant notification.
