## Why Smoke Spreads Differently in Bayonne's Older Homes

Much of Bayonne's housing stock, from the frame two-families near Bergen Point to the brick rowhomes in Downtown Bayonne, predates modern fire-blocking standards. Balloon-frame construction common in this era creates open wall cavities that run straight from basement to attic, so smoke and heat from a fire on one floor can pull soot particles vertically through the walls themselves, not just through the air. Add in original plaster and lath, which is more porous than drywall and holds onto combustion odor longer, and a fire that looks contained to a kitchen or a single bedroom can leave protein smoke residue in rooms nobody thought were touched. Postwar capes with finished basements add another wrinkle: smoke and soot settle on exposed joists and HVAC trunk lines before recirculating through the rest of the house.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Bayonne

We start with a room-by-room assessment to separate wet smoke residue (thick, oily, from smoldering fires) from dry smoke residue (powdery, from fast-burning fires), because the cleaning agents and techniques differ. HVAC systems get inspected and, where contaminated, sealed off to stop redistributing soot while we work. Hard surfaces are cleaned with HEPA vacuuming and dry sponges or chemical sponges depending on residue type, upholstery and soft goods are either treated on-site or packed out, and we finish with thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators to neutralize odor at the molecular level rather than mask it. For homes near the Newark Bay waterfront, we also check for corrosion on metal fixtures and appliances, since smoke combined with the area's humid air accelerates oxidation on anything metallic within hours of a fire.
