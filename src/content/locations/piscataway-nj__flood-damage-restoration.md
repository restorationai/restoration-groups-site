## Why Piscataway Properties See Flood Damage Issues

The township's flood exposure isn't hypothetical. River Road and the Johnson Park flatlands took on serious water during Ida in 2021, and the same low-lying stretch had already flooded during Floyd and Irene. That repeat riverine exposure means basements and crawl spaces in those areas carry a flood history that shapes how we approach drying, even on a loss that looks minor at the surface. Housing stock adds another layer: postwar capes and split-levels throughout Possumtown and Stelton often still run galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains into finished basements, and those systems corrode and fail differently than modern PVC. Larger developments like Society Hill bring their own pattern, with shared foundation walls and below-grade parking that can move water between units. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Piscataway

We start with an assessment that maps moisture against the specific construction we're dealing with, since a 1960s cape with plaster walls dries on a different timeline than a Society Hill townhome with drywall and engineered flooring. Extraction comes first, using truck-mounted and portable units sized to the space, followed by moisture readings taken at the studs and subfloor, not just the surface. On homes with cast-iron drains or galvanized supply lines, we inspect those systems for flood-related corrosion or backflow before closing up any wall or ceiling cavity, since a hidden leak behind fresh drywall defeats the purpose of the dry-out. Dehumidification and air movement run until moisture content in the affected materials reads at or below the level dried per the IICRC S500 standard, and we document every reading for the insurance file.

## Piscataway Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most standard homeowner's policies exclude flood water that rises from outside the home, which is a distinction that matters a lot along River Road and near Johnson Park where separate flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program is common. We document the source of water, affected materials, and extent of loss in a way that works for either a standard carrier or an NFIP claim, since the paperwork and inspection requirements differ. In Society Hill and similar attached-home developments, we also coordinate with property management or the HOA when common walls, shared utility runs, or below-grade garages are involved, since those claims sometimes split between a unit owner's policy and the association's coverage.
