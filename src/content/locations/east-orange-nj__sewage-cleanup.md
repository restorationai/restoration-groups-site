## Why East Orange Properties See Sewage Backups

Those materials corrode, root-intrude, and settle differently than PVC, and a partial blockage in a shared lateral can send sewage backward into multiple units at once in a three-story walk-up. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in East Orange

Category 3 water, the technical term for sewage-contaminated water, carries bacteria and pathogens that make standard mopping and air-drying unsafe. Our crews arrive in protective equipment, extract standing waste water, and remove any porous materials that have absorbed contamination, carpet, carpet pad, baseboards, and in many cases the bottom few inches of drywall. In East Orange's older brick buildings, that often means working around horsehair plaster and lath that don't dry or sanitize the way modern drywall does, so we test moisture content before deciding what stays and what comes out. Once contaminated materials are removed, we clean and disinfect remaining structural surfaces, then dry the space using commercial air movers and dehumidifiers monitored against the IICRC S500 drying standard. For multi-unit buildings around Brick Church station or Ampere, we coordinate with building management early since a lateral backup in one unit can signal a problem that affects neighboring apartments on the same stack.

## East Orange Insurance Coordination for Sewage Losses

Sewage backup coverage is often a separate rider from standard water damage coverage, and adjusters want documentation of the contamination category, the extent of material removal, and the sanitization steps taken before they approve a claim. We photograph the loss at each stage, note affected square footage and materials, and provide documentation most carriers need to process a sewage backup claim. Landlords managing two- and three-family properties near Doddtown or Elmwood should also loop in their building insurance separately from any tenant policy, since responsibility for the lateral line itself, as opposed to the fixtures and interior finishes, often falls on the property owner rather than the city.
