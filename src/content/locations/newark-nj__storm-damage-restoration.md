## Why Newark Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Newark's combined sewer system was built for a smaller, drier city, and when a storm drops a few inches of rain in a short window, those lines back up into basements across Weequahic, Vailsburg, and University Heights alike. Add in the Ironbound's low elevation along the Passaic, and you get a neighborhood where storm surge and river overflow show up in the same event, sometimes before the wind damage upstairs has even been assessed. On the housing side, Newark's stock is mostly pre-war: tight-set brick multifamilies, frame two- and three-families with original rooflines, and the grander colonials in Forest Hill with slate or aging asphalt shingle roofs that weren't built for sustained straight-line winds. A missing shingle or a cracked chimney flashing on one of these roofs can let a storm's rain load find its way into a third-floor ceiling within a single event, and because these buildings sit close together, one failed gutter or downspout routinely sends water into the foundation of the building next door too.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Newark

First call is triage: we need to know if the roof envelope is still holding, whether water is actively entering, and whether downed trees or branches have compromised structural members or electrical service. Emergency board-up and tarping go up first to stop the bleeding, particularly on the exposed brick parapets common on Downtown Newark's older commercial buildings and the steep gable ends you'll find on Forest Hill's colonials. From there we extract standing water, pull wet insulation and drywall that won't dry in place, and set drying equipment calibrated for Newark's older wall assemblies, plaster and lath hold moisture longer than modern drywall and need extended drying cycles to avoid trapping water behind the surface. Tree and debris removal from roofs and yards happens alongside the drying phase rather than after it, since leaving a fallen limb on a compromised roof invites a second collapse before the first repair is even scheduled.

## Newark Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most Newark storm claims we see involve a mix of wind damage to the roof and water intrusion from either rain entry or sewer backup, and carriers often want those documented as separate causes of loss even when they happened in the same storm. We photograph and log each separately, roof and siding damage, interior water intrusion, tree impact, so the claim isn't contested on causation later. For multifamily owners and property managers in Downtown Newark's apartment stock or the university-adjacent buildings near University Heights, we provide the kind of itemized, insurance-ready documentation that satisfies both a homeowner's policy and a commercial landlord's coverage.
