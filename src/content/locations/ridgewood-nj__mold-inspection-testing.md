Mold inspection and testing in Ridgewood isn't a generic walkthrough with a flashlight. ## Why Ridgewood Properties See Mold Issues

The building stock here works against quick drying. Plaster-and-lath walls, original fieldstone foundations, and finished basements with carpet or paneling over the original masonry all trap humidity in ways drywall-over-studs construction does not. Add a brook that has flooded downtown streets and nearby basements, most notably during Ida in 2021, and you get a recurring pattern: a wet event, a surface-level cleanup, and mold that shows up weeks later in a spot nobody checked. Homes in Willard and along West Side streets closer to the brook see this more often than properties up in the Heights, simply because of proximity and grade. Older slate and flat roofs on historic homes add a second entry point, with ice-dam backups and storm intrusion driving moisture into attic spaces and top-floor ceilings that then gets missed in a basement-only inspection.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Ridgewood

We start with a visual and moisture-mapping walkthrough of the whole structure, not just the area where a leak or flood was reported, because moisture migrates along joists and behind baseboards in these older homes in ways that aren't always intuitive. A moisture meter and thermal imaging camera help us find elevated readings behind finished surfaces without cutting into plaster unnecessarily. Where conditions warrant it, we collect air samples and, when surface growth is visible or suspected, surface swab or tape-lift samples, which go to an independent lab for analysis. We document everything with photos and a written report, which matters for insurance correspondence and for anyone selling a Ridgewood property where a mold disclosure or buyer-requested inspection is in play.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Because so much of Ridgewood's older housing stock hides moisture behind plaster, rubble foundations, or old coal-chute voids, we lean on a borescope to look inside wall and foundation cavities before deciding where sampling makes sense. Spore trap air samples give us a baseline comparison between indoor and outdoor air, which is the standard way to judge whether indoor mold levels are elevated relative to what's naturally in the outdoor air that day. Combined with moisture readings and visual findings, that comparison is what actually tells a homeowner whether remediation is warranted, rather than guessing from smell or a few visible spots.
