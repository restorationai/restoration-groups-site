## Why Summit Basements Flood

Most of the water calls we get near Downtown Summit and the Franklin School area trace back to the same culprits: clay sewer laterals running under mature street trees that have had eighty or ninety years to find their way into a joint, cast iron or galvanized supply lines failing inside exterior walls, and grading around 1890s-1930s foundations that was never designed for today's storm volume. In the Brayton School area and toward the New Providence border, lots pitch more sharply, and a heavy spring downpour can push water through a foundation seam or window well faster than an older sump pump can keep up. Add uninsulated radiator and supply lines in unheated wings near the back of the house, and a hard freeze can split a pipe that doesn't show itself until the thaw.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork, documenting how far water traveled up plaster, under baseboards, and into subfloor before we move a single piece of furniture. Deep finished basements common in Summit's older homes often have built-ins, paneling, and plaster over masonry, so extraction equipment and airflow placement get adjusted room by room rather than run on a standard template. Contents such as rugs, upholstered pieces, and family furniture are inventoried and moved to a drying or storage area on-site when possible, since many of these homes hold furnishings families expect handled carefully, not stacked in a driveway. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture readings logged against your home's baseline so the drying log, not just a technician's word, tells you and your adjuster when the space is actually dry.

## Summit Insurance Coordination

Sewer backup and sump pump failure are two of the most common basement claims we see in Summit, and they're frequently covered under separate riders rather than a standard policy, so we document the source of water (storm surface water, sewer backup, or supply line failure) clearly from the first visit. That distinction matters to a claims adjuster, and getting it right the first time avoids a second inspection delaying your payout. As an IICRC Certified Firm and a New Jersey Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we provide the photo documentation, moisture logs, and scope notes most carriers ask for before approving mitigation work.
