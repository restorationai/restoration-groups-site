Springfield Township's housing stock is part of the problem. When storms like Ida turned Route 22 into a river, those finished basements absorbed water through the slab and foundation walls as much as through window wells, and the carpet and drywall assemblies held onto that moisture for days. ## Our Mold Remediation Process in Springfield

We start with containment, not demolition. Given how many Springfield basements are finished living space, we isolate the affected area with physical barriers and negative air pressure before anything is removed, so spores don't travel into the rest of the house or, in a Route 22 corridor retail or office space, into an adjoining unit. From there we remove and bag contaminated porous materials (drywall, carpet, insulation) per IICRC guidelines, HEPA-vacuum and wipe down all affected surfaces, and run air scrubbers through the containment zone. Because our firm is IICRC Certified Firm #210213, we document moisture readings and visible growth at each stage so the file holds up if a carrier or an HOA board asks for proof of the work performed. We don't close out a job until surface and ambient conditions read within a normal range for the structure.

## Springfield Insurance & HOA Coordination

Mold claims in Springfield tend to hinge on whether the carrier can tie the growth to a covered water event, like a sudden pipe failure, versus long-term seepage, which most policies exclude. We photograph and document the moisture source alongside the mold itself so your claim has a clear chain of causation, not just photos of the fungus. For the townhome communities near the Millburn line, HOA bylaws often require the board to approve any work touching shared wall cavities or HVAC chases before we start, so we build that lead time into scheduling rather than showing up and getting turned away at the door.
