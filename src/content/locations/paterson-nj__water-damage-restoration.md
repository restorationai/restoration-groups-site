## Why Paterson Properties See Water Damage Issues

A large share of Paterson's housing predates 1950, including dense brick multi-family walkups and two- and three-family frame houses built for silk-mill workers. Many still carry original cast-iron waste lines and knob-and-tube wiring remnants, both of which complicate a water loss: cast iron corrodes from the inside and fails without warning, and old wiring near a wet wall is a real hazard, not a hypothetical one. In South Paterson and Hillcrest, converted silk-era industrial buildings now operate as apartments, and their retrofitted sprinkler and standpipe systems are a recurring source of multi-unit losses when a head fails or a riser freezes. Add in overcrowded rental stock across the city, where a single bathroom backup can travel through three units before anyone upstairs even notices, and you get a pattern of water damage calls that looks very different from a suburban single-family loss.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Paterson

Extraction comes first: standing water in a basement or first-floor unit gets pulled with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment depending on access, which matters in rowhomes with narrow interior stairwells common across Lakeview and the Totowa Section. From there we document moisture readings in the affected materials, because plaster and lath behave differently from drywall. Plaster can read dry at the surface while the lath behind it is still saturated, so we monitor it over several days rather than pulling equipment early. Drying is set up and tracked according to the IICRC S500 structural drying standard, with dehumidification and air movement sized to the room, not a flat equipment count. On jobs involving cast-iron drain failures or sewage backup, we isolate and sanitize before drying begins, since drying around contaminated material just locks the problem into the structure.
