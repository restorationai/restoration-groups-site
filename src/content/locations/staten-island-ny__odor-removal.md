## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Staten Island

We start by identifying the material actually holding the odor, whether that's subfloor under New Dorp carpet, wall cavities in a Port Richmond two-family, or ductwork after a kitchen fire. Source materials that can't be fully cleaned, like saturated insulation or charred wood, get removed rather than treated over. From there we match the method to the space: thermal fogging for smoke and fire odor that has penetrated porous surfaces, hydroxyl generators for occupied homes where people or pets are staying on-site, and ozone treatment for vacant properties or sealed-off work areas, since ozone requires the space to be empty of occupants during treatment. We verify the result with a walkthrough before closing out the job, not just a timer on a machine.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Ozone and hydroxyl treatments solve different problems. Ozone is aggressive and fast but can't be run around people, pets, or plants, which makes it a better fit for an unoccupied Tottenville rental between tenants than for a family staying in the home during work. Hydroxyl generators produce a similar oxidizing effect without the occupancy restriction, so they're our default for lived-in homes. Thermal fogging, which carries a deodorizing agent into the same cracks and pores that smoke penetrated, tends to perform better on the plaster-and-lath construction common in Staten Island's older homes than on modern drywall alone, since plaster holds odor compounds deeper into the wall assembly.
