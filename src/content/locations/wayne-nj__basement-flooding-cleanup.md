## Why Wayne Properties See Basement Flooding Issues

Wayne's flood risk isn't evenly spread, and that matters for how we approach a job. The Hoffman Grove and Mountain View sections sit close enough to the Pompton River that residents remember specific storms by name, Irene in 2011 and Ida in 2021 both pushed water into basements that had been dry for years before. Further from the river, the Packanack, Pines, and Lionshead Lake communities bring a different problem: midcentury ranches and split-levels built right up to the waterline, many with finished basements that were never meant to handle standing water, let alone the hydrostatic pressure that builds when a lake rises after heavy rain. Away from the water, Wayne's broader housing stock, 1950s to 1970s colonials and capes, relies on sump pumps and aging clay or cast-iron drain lines that were adequate forty years ago and struggle during a sustained downpour today. A failed sump or an undersized discharge line is one of the most common calls we get from this town that has nothing to do with river flooding at all.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Wayne

The first hour determines a lot. We extract standing water, then assess whether the basement is finished or unfinished, because that changes the drying plan entirely. A finished basement near Packanack or Pines Lake with carpet pad, drywall, and baseboard trim needs those materials pulled back quickly, since saturated drywall begins wicking moisture upward and can compromise framing above the flood line within a day or two. An unfinished block-foundation basement near Mountain View dries faster on the surface but can trap water in the cores of the block itself, so we monitor moisture readings over several days rather than calling it dry the moment the surface reads normal. Dehumidification and air movement follow IICRC S500 drying standards, and we document moisture levels throughout so the drying record holds up if your insurer asks for it later.
