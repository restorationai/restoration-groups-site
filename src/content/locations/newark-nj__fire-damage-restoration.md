## Why Newark Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Newark's housing stock works against containment. The Ironbound and Vailsburg are full of frame two- and three-family houses built close together, sometimes with only a few feet of airspace between structures, so a fire that starts in one building can scorch siding, melt gutters, and push smoke into the home next door before the first truck arrives. Forest Hill's larger colonials and Downtown Newark's brick multifamily stock have their own weak point: shared plumbing risers and old electrical runs that were never sized for modern appliance loads. When a riser fails or a fire starts near a chase, it's common for smoke and the water used to fight the fire to travel down through two or three floors, soaking units that never saw flame. University Heights, with its mix of rental housing near Rutgers-Newark and NJIT, adds another layer: landlords and property managers juggling multiple tenants who all need somewhere to stay while the unit dries out and is cleaned.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Newark

We start with board-up and a safety walkthrough, since a fire-damaged structure in a dense block needs to be secured fast to keep it from becoming an open liability for neighboring properties. From there we document every room with photos before anything is moved, which matters in Newark where multiple units and sometimes multiple insurance carriers are involved in one loss. Soot and smoke residue get removed from hard surfaces, fabrics, and contents using HEPA vacuuming and chemical sponging appropriate to the material, since drywall, plaster, and old wood trim all hold smoke odor differently. Once surfaces are clean, we treat for odor with thermal fogging or ozone depending on how deep the smoke penetrated, and we check basements and lower floors for water that traveled down from firefighting efforts or a failed riser before closing out the job.
