## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hillside

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out of basements and crawl spaces with truck-mount and portable units, because every hour water sits against baseboard, subfloor, or cast-iron drain lines is an hour closer to secondary damage. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms with meters, not guesswork, checking subfloor, framing, and the backside of finished basement walls that often hide water long after the surface looks dry. In older Saybrook and Coe's Corner homes with plaster-and-lath basement ceilings or partition walls, that material holds moisture longer than drywall, so we adjust drying time and airflow placement instead of pulling equipment early and risking mold behind the wall. Once the structure reads dry per IICRC S500 guidelines, we document everything, photos, moisture logs, and equipment run time, for your records and your adjuster.

## Hillside Insurance Coordination

Most Hillside claims we see fall into two categories: storm-and-sewer-backup claims tied to the Elizabeth River flooding, and plumbing-failure claims from aging basement supply lines. Sewer backup often requires a separate rider or endorsement that not every policy includes, so we document the source of the water carefully, because whether it's classified as flood, sewer backup, or plumbing failure changes how a carrier handles the claim. We photograph affected materials, log drying equipment and run times, and submit that documentation directly to most major carriers so you're not stuck assembling the paperwork yourself while you're also dealing with a wet basement.
