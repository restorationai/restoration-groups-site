## Why Brooklyn Properties See Water Damage Fast

Most of the brownstone belt, Park Slope, Brooklyn Heights, and the blocks pushing toward Bed-Stuy, was built between the 1880s and 1920s as masonry rowhouses with below-grade cellars and garden-level apartments. Those spaces sit close to the water table and close to aging combined sewer lines, so they're the first to flood when a storm drain backs up or a cast-iron stack fails. Hurricane Ida's rainfall turned this into a citywide emergency, with basement and garden-level units across the borough taking on water in a matter of minutes, not hours. Further out, Canarsie and the stretch toward the Coney Island boardwalk carry a different exposure: storm surge. Residents there still measure storms against Sandy, and tidal flooding behaves less like a plumbing failure and more like the bay briefly reclaiming a basement.

## Our Emergency Water Removal Process in Brooklyn

The first call is triage: is this a clean supply line break, a sewer backup, or storm surge intrusion, because each changes the extraction plan and the safety gear our crew wears on-site. In rowhouse basements we extract with portable pumps and truck-mounted units staged on the street, since narrow stoops and tight interior stairwells in buildings near Brooklyn Heights and Williamsburg rarely allow full-size equipment past the front door. Moisture readings get taken at the baseboard, mid-wall, and subfloor, because plaster and old-growth lath hold water differently than the drywall in a newer build, and drying has to be paced to the material, not a generic timeline. Everything is logged and photographed as we go, since that documentation is what makes the difference between a claim that moves and one that stalls.

## Brooklyn Insurance and Co-op Board Coordination

A meaningful share of Brooklyn housing stock is co-op or condo, which means a flooded unit often answers to two parties: the owner's insurer and the building's managing agent or board. Boards in Park Slope and Brooklyn Heights buildings frequently want their own moisture logs and drying verification before they'll sign off on repairs to shared walls or risers, separate from whatever the unit owner's carrier requires. We document for both from day one, including readings on party walls shared with a neighboring unit, so the paperwork doesn't become the bottleneck after the water is already gone.

## Local note

Party walls in Brooklyn's attached rowhouses share more than a property line, they often share moisture. Water that saturates a cellar wall in a Park Slope brownstone can migrate laterally into the adjoining building before it ever shows as a stain on either side, which is why we check the neighbor-facing wall even on a call that looks contained to one address.
