## Why Brooklyn Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of Brooklyn's sewer infrastructure, especially in the brownstone belt running through Park Slope, Brooklyn Heights, and the Bed-Stuy fringe, carries both stormwater and wastewater in the same pipe. When a heavy rain event overwhelms that combined system, the water has nowhere to go but back up through the lowest fixtures in a building, which in an 1880s to 1920s rowhouse usually means the garden-level or cellar apartment. Hurricane Ida made that dynamic a citywide emergency, and it's a pattern that repeats on a smaller scale every time a summer downpour outruns the storm drains. Coastal pockets from Red Hook toward Canarsie and Coney Island carry an added layer of risk: storm surge, remembered clearly from Sandy, can push sewer mains into surcharge even without direct flooding at the property.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Brooklyn

We treat every sewage loss as Category 3 contaminated water from the start, which dictates how much gets removed rather than dried. Standing wastewater is extracted first, then we remove and bag porous materials that can't be sanitized, baseboards, carpet and pad, and often the bottom few inches of drywall, especially in finished basements where moisture wicks up fast through old masonry. Affected surfaces get cleaned and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and the space is dried and monitored per the IICRC S500 standard until readings confirm it's actually dry, not just dry to the touch. In party-wall rowhouses we're careful about cross-contamination into a shared cellar or an adjoining unit's foundation wall, since brownstone construction often shares structural elements between addresses.

## Brooklyn Insurance & Co-op Board Coordination

Co-op and condo boards in buildings near Prospect Park or along the Brooklyn Heights promenade often require documentation before a unit owner's insurer or the building's management company will sign off on cleanup, photos of affected materials, moisture logs, and a clear scope of what was removed versus treated. We put that documentation together as we go and bill most major carriers directly, which matters when a shared building system, like a main stack serving multiple floors, is the source of the backup rather than something isolated to one unit.

## Local note

In brownstone-belt buildings across Park Slope and Brooklyn Heights, the clay sewer lateral connecting the house to the street main is frequently original to construction and considerably narrower than a modern PVC line. That means a backup in one of these buildings is often a lateral blockage rather than a city main problem, so we check the house trap and cleanout before assuming the issue is on the street side. It's a small step that saves a homeowner from an unnecessary call to the city when the real fix is a plumber clearing the lateral.
