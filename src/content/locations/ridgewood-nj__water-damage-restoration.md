Older slate and flat roofs common on Ridgewood's historic homes also let storm water in at valleys and flashing long before a homeowner notices a stain on the ceiling below. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Ridgewood

We start by finding where the water actually went, not just where it's visible. In a Somerville or Old Country Club basement with finished walls, that usually means checking behind baseboards and under subfloor before deciding how much material comes out. Extraction comes first, using truck-mounted and portable pumps for standing water, followed by moisture mapping of framing, subfloor, and masonry walls. Older plaster and lath found throughout Ridgewood's pre-war housing holds moisture longer than modern drywall, so drying timelines are set by readings, not guesswork, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and monitored until wood and masonry moisture content returns to a stable range consistent with the IICRC S500 drying standard. Where flood water from the brook has reached a basement, we treat it as a contamination concern and sanitize affected materials before closing the job out.
