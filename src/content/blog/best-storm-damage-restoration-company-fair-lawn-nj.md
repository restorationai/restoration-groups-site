## What should a storm damage estimate actually include?

A complete storm damage estimate should separate emergency mitigation (tarping, board-up, water extraction) from any later repair work, with each line item explained in writing, not bundled into a vague lump sum. If a quote doesn't distinguish the two, ask why.

Look for: the specific square footage or linear footage of roof tarped or openings boarded, the equipment deployed for water extraction and drying (air movers, dehumidifiers, moisture meters with logged readings), and a written timeline for follow-up drying checks. The Restoration Group provides a written scope before work begins so homeowners know exactly what's covered and what isn't.

One honest distinction worth knowing: mitigation companies like The Restoration Group stabilize the structure and prevent further damage, water extraction, tarping, drying, debris and contents protection. They are not roofers or general contractors. If your roof needs replacement shingles, new decking, or structural framing repair after the storm, that's separate rebuild work handled by a licensed roofing or construction contractor, often coordinated after the mitigation company's scope is complete.

## Does homeowners insurance cover storm damage mitigation in NJ?

Most standard NJ homeowners policies cover sudden, storm-caused damage like wind-driven rain intrusion, a tree limb through a roof, or wind-torn siding, but coverage details vary by policy and carrier. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) outlines how wind and storm perils are typically treated versus flood, which usually requires a separate NFIP or private flood policy.

That distinction matters in Fair Lawn specifically: wind and rain damage to a roof is typically a homeowners-policy claim, while rising water from the Passaic or Saddle River overflowing its banks, the kind this area saw during Ida in 2021, usually falls under flood insurance instead. If you're not sure which applies to your situation, our [storm damage insurance claim checklist](/blog/storm-damage-insurance-claim-checklist/) walks through what to document before you call your carrier. New Jersey homeowners can also review coverage basics through the [NJ Department of Banking and Insurance](https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_consumers/insurance/homeowner.htm).
