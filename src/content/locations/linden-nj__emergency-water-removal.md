## Why Linden Properties See Water Emergencies

Linden's housing stock is dominated by 1940s through 1960s capes, colonials, and split-levels, many with finished basements built on the original supply lines and cast-iron drains. Those systems are well past their expected service life in a lot of Wheatsheaf and Bower homes, and a failed joint or a cracked stack can dump water into a finished space overnight with nobody awake to catch it. The city also sits low along the Rahway River and Morses Creek, and areas near Tremley Point and the Arthur Kill took on serious flooding when Ida pushed those waterways over their banks in 2021. Even without a named storm, heavy rain events back up storm drains in low-lying blocks and send water in through foundation cracks and window wells. On the commercial side, the Route 1 & 9 industrial corridor and the refinery zone generate a different kind of loss: sprinkler discharge, overnight pipe breaks in large commercial buildings, and water damage that spreads fast across open warehouse floors before anyone notices.

## Our Emergency Water Removal Process in Linden

The first call is about stopping the source and getting water off the floor and out of the walls before it migrates further. Crews extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how the property is accessed, pull baseboards and drill weep holes where water has tracked up behind drywall, and set up air movers and dehumidifiers positioned to follow the actual moisture path rather than just the visible wet spot. In Linden's postwar basements, that path usually runs along the sill plate and into the floor joists, which is where moisture meters and sometimes a thermal camera come in: a slab can read dry on the surface while the framing above it is still saturated. We document moisture readings at each visit and dry structural materials to levels consistent with the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing based on how a room looks or smells.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Emergency Water Removal

Extraction equipment is chosen by the type of loss, not a one-size approach. Submersible pumps handle deep standing water in a flooded basement, while truck-mounted extraction units pull water faster from carpet and pad in living spaces. Once bulk water is gone, low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers and axial air movers do the structural drying, and we track progress with daily or near-daily moisture readings until materials hit dry standard, not just dry to the touch. Where cast-iron drain failures or sewer backup are part of the loss, water is treated as contaminated per category guidelines before any drying equipment goes in.
