## Why Fort Lee Basements Flood

Fort Lee sits on top of the Palisades, which means water doesn't just fall here, it runs. Storm runoff moving down the cliff grade toward the Hudson finds the path of least resistance, and in older neighborhoods like Coytesville and The Hill that path is often a fieldstone or block foundation poured decades before current drainage codes existed. Those foundations were never fully waterproof to begin with, and hydrostatic pressure after a heavy storm pushes groundwater straight through the mortar joints.

The borough's newer high-rise stock along Main Street and around the Hudson Lights complex has the opposite problem: stacked plumbing. A supply line or appliance failure several floors up travels down through risers and utility chases, and by the time it reaches a below-grade garage or storage level it's no longer a small leak, it's standing water across a shared structure. We saw the scale of what regional storm flooding can do across Bergen County during Ida in 2021, and the same drainage patterns that overwhelmed streets then are still in play every time a heavy system moves through.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Fort Lee

The first step is always extraction, but what we extract from and how changes by building type. In a Linwood or Central Fort Lee single-family basement, we're usually pulling water off a slab or dirt-floor cellar and pulling saturated insulation or framing away from foundation walls so they can air out properly. In a condo tower, we're often working a mechanical room or parking level with floor drains, sump pits, and shared utility runs that need to stay operational for the rest of the building while we dry around them.

After extraction, we map moisture in the concrete, framing, and any finished basement materials with meters, not guesswork, because a slab can read dry on the surface while it's still saturated two inches down. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in sized to the cubic footage and the construction type, since a dense high-rise substructure holds humidity differently than an open single-family cellar. We monitor daily until readings return to a normal range for the building, not just until the floor looks dry.

## Fort Lee Insurance & HOA Coordination

Condo and co-op buildings near Hudson Lights and along Palisade Avenue typically carry a master policy that covers the building's common structure, while anything inside an individual unit or storage space often falls to the owner's HO-6 policy. That split matters when a basement flood starts in a shared riser but damages tenant storage units below. We document the source, the path, and the affected materials with photos and moisture readings so your adjuster, and the HOA's if there is one, can see exactly where responsibility lines fall instead of guessing after the fact.

## Local note

A number of the prewar homes in Coytesville and The Hill still have cellars with partial dirt floors rather than full concrete slabs. Standard extraction equipment works, but drying takes longer because exposed earth keeps releasing moisture into the space well after the visible water is gone. We factor that into how long we leave drying equipment running so the basement doesn't just look dry, it actually is.
