## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Livingston

A visual inspection comes first, checking sill plates, basement corners, crawl space framing, and areas around HVAC returns where spores circulate fastest. We map moisture with a meter rather than guessing from a stain, since drywall and plaster can look dry on the surface while the stud bay behind them stays saturated. Air sampling follows, comparing indoor spore counts against an outdoor baseline sample, which is the only way to know if indoor air is actually elevated versus just reflecting the season. Where we see visible growth, a surface swab goes to an independent lab alongside the air samples, and the written report comes back with species identification and spore counts you can hand directly to an insurance adjuster or a buyer's attorney during a real estate transaction.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Moisture meters and thermal imaging find the wet material that a flashlight misses, which matters in Livingston's split-levels where a leak on the upper level can travel along a header and show up two rooms away. Air sampling pumps pull a measured volume of air through a spore trap cartridge in minutes, and hygrometers log relative humidity in basements and crawl spaces where levels above 60% start favoring mold growth. All lab analysis goes to a third-party laboratory rather than being read in-house, so the results aren't something we're grading ourselves on.
