## Why Springfield Properties See Storm Damage So Often

Springfield's geography works against it during severe weather. The west branch of the Rahway River collects runoff coming down from the Watchung ridges, and the township's lowest elevations, several of them within a few blocks of Morris Avenue and the Route 22 commercial strip, flood in nearly every major storm cycle. The housing stock compounds the problem. A lot of Springfield's 1920s to 1950s colonials and capes were built with basements that were never meant to be living space, and when they were finished over the decades, the waterproofing underneath often wasn't upgraded to match. Newer townhome complexes toward the Millburn line fare a little better structurally, but shared drainage systems mean one unit's water problem can quickly become three or four units' problem. Add wind-driven limb and tree damage from storms moving through the wooded sections near Baltusrol Golf Club and Meisel Avenue Park, and you get a town where roof, basement, and tree damage frequently show up on the same call.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Springfield

We start with safety and containment: securing a damaged roofline or broken window with board-up or tarping before anything else, especially on homes near the Mountain Avenue corridor where storms often bring down branches onto roofs and gutters. From there it's extraction, moving standing water out of basements and crawl spaces before it migrates further into framing and insulation. Drying follows the IICRC S500 structural drying standard, with moisture readings taken at the walls, subfloor, and any finished basement materials that took on water. For properties with both storm and tree damage, debris removal and structural drying typically happen in parallel so the home isn't sitting exposed any longer than necessary.

## Springfield Insurance & HOA Coordination

Homeowners filing storm claims in Springfield usually fall into one of two buckets: standard homeowner's policies for the colonials and capes near Springfield center, or HOA master policies for the townhome developments closer to the Millburn line. Those master policies often cover the building shell differently than a unit owner's individual policy covers interior finishes and contents, which can mean two separate claims for one storm event. We document the loss with photos and moisture logs and bill most major carriers directly, and as a NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor we can coordinate directly with property managers on multi-unit storm claims when that's how the building is set up.

Homes along the Mountain Avenue corridor and near Baltusrol Golf Club tend to sit on clay-heavy soil that drains slowly off the Watchung ridge runoff.
