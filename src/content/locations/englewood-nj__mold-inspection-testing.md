## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Englewood

We start with a visual walkthrough and moisture mapping using a meter against walls, subfloor, and any area with a known water history, whether that's a finished East Hill basement or a West Englewood crawl space. Where moisture readings or visible growth suggest a problem, we collect air samples to measure spore counts against an outdoor baseline, and surface samples when we need to identify a specific species on drywall, wood trim, or HVAC components. Samples go to an independent lab, not one we own, so the results are not something we have an incentive to shade one way or another. The written report documents moisture source, affected materials, and spore data, and it's the same report an insurance adjuster, property manager, or an HOA board at a Fourth Ward co-op would need before authorizing remediation work.

## Local note

In the stone-foundation homes common on the East Hill and near the Englewood Cliffs border, moisture meters can give misleadingly low surface readings even when the wall cavity behind the plaster is saturated, because stone and old masonry release moisture more gradually than modern block. We factor that into how we read instrument data on older Englewood properties rather than taking a single surface reading at face value. On the newer slab construction common in West Englewood, the opposite problem shows up: surface readings spike fast near a leak but don't tell you how far the moisture has traveled under the slab edge, so we rely more heavily on air sampling in those homes to catch what the meter alone would miss.
