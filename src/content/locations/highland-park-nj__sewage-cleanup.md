Cast iron drains from the pre-war and early postwar building boom were built to last decades, not forever, and many Highland Park homes are now past the point where failure is a matter of if rather than when. Add to that a sewer and storm system that runs close to capacity during heavy rain, and the Raritan River corridor along the borough's southern edge, and you get a pattern we see repeatedly: backups that show up first in the lowest fixture in the house, usually a basement floor drain or laundry hookup, right after a storm event. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Highland Park

Raw sewage is a Category 3 loss by IICRC S500 standards, and we treat it that way from the first walkthrough. Our crews extract standing contaminated water, remove and dispose of porous materials that can't be sanitized, like saturated carpet, drywall, and baseboard, and then clean and disinfect all affected hard surfaces. In Highland Park's older homes, that often means working around finished basements with limited egress, cast-iron stacks that need inspection before we close anything back up, and sump pits that may need temporary bypass while repairs happen. We set containment to keep contaminated air and material away from the rest of the living space, then bring in air movers and dehumidification calibrated to the structure, not a generic timeline. Antimicrobial application and a final sanitization pass happen before we call a space clear.
