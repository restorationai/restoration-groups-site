A kitchen fire or an electrical short in a Park Slope brownstone doesn't behave like one in a modern condo. The smoke has somewhere to go: original lath-and-plaster walls, pocket doors, pressed-tin ceilings, and the shared party walls that connect one 1890s rowhouse to the next. Soot particles are acidic and they migrate fast through old mortar joints and floor gaps, which means a fire that starts in one unit can leave a sour, smoky smell two floors away before anyone calls it in.

## Why Brooklyn Buildings Hold Smoke Longer

Much of the borough's housing stock, from the brownstone belt around Park Slope and Brooklyn Heights to the pre-war walk-ups near Flatbush, was built before central HVAC existed. Smoke traveled through transoms, dumbwaiter shafts, and the gaps between joists instead of a duct system, and it settles into those same gaps today. Williamsburg and Bushwick have a different mix, converted industrial buildings and newer infill with shared mechanical risers, where smoke odor can ride the ventilation into neighboring units fast. In co-ops near Bay Ridge and Canarsie, the building's management often gets pulled into the claim before the homeowner does, because smoke that crosses a party wall becomes a building-wide issue, not just an apartment one.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Brooklyn

We start with a walk-through to separate what's salvageable from what isn't, since soot on plaster behaves differently than soot on drywall or on painted steel fire escapes. Porous materials like plaster and unfinished wood hold residue deep in the surface, so we test before committing to full removal versus cleaning in place. From there, crews HEPA-vacuum loose soot, hand-clean hard surfaces and woodwork, and use thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize odor that's worked into fibers, dried per IICRC S500/S700 guidance. Contents that can travel, rugs, upholstery, family photos, go through our pack-out process for cleaning off-site, which matters in rowhouse units where there's no room to run equipment around furniture for days.

## Brooklyn Insurance & Co-op Board Coordination

Most smoke damage claims in brownstone and co-op buildings involve two parties: the unit owner's policy and the building's master policy, and the two don't always agree on where the line falls. We photograph and document room by room, separate structural soot damage from contents loss, and provide the kind of itemized report that both a homeowner's adjuster and a co-op board's managing agent will accept without a second site visit. For party-wall situations, that documentation often ends up doing double duty for a neighbor's claim too.
