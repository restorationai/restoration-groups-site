Mold doesn't wait for a convenient moment to show up, and in Ridgewood it often shows up in the same deep, finished basements that flooded during Ida back in 2021. If your home near the Ho-Ho-Kus Brook or Saddle River took on water that year, or any year since, there's a real chance moisture worked into framing and subfloor that never fully dried, and mold remediation becomes less about a quick wipe-down and more about tracing where the colony actually lives.

## Why Ridgewood Properties See Mold Issues

Ridgewood's housing stock works against it here. The Village's grand early-1900s Victorians, Tudors, and colonials, especially the older homes around Willard and the Heights, were built with plaster walls, stone or brick foundations, and supply lines that have been patched and repiped piecemeal over a century. Those assemblies hold moisture longer than modern construction, and a slow basement seep can go unnoticed for weeks before a musty smell gives it away. Add in the brook and river flooding that regularly backs up into low-lying streets near downtown, plus aging slate and flat roofs on older homes that let storm water intrude at flashing and valleys, and you have a city where mold gets a head start most homeowners don't realize they've given it. Deep finished basements, a West Ridgewood pattern as much as a Village one, compound the problem because mold often colonizes behind finished walls where nobody looks until there's a smell or a stain.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Ridgewood

We start with containment, because an older Ridgewood home with plaster walls and original lath doesn't behave like new-construction drywall. Containment barriers and negative air pressure keep spores from migrating into the rest of the house while we're working, which matters more in these homes because HVAC returns in century-old houses were often added later and don't always isolate rooms the way a modern system does. From there it's removal of affected material, usually drywall, insulation, or in older sections of a home, plaster and lath, followed by HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment of the structure left behind. Because plaster releases moisture slowly compared to drywall, we run drying equipment longer in those sections even after moisture meters read close to normal, otherwise the colony comes back in a wall cavity nobody's checking. Final clearance means visible mold is gone and moisture readings are back in a normal range before we close containment.

## Ridgewood Insurance Coordination

Mold claims in Ridgewood are frequently tied to a documented water event, a basement flood near the brook, a roof leak from an ice dam on an older slate roof, a slow pipe failure in a home with original supply lines. We document the source and the damage with photos and moisture readings and bill most major carriers directly, which matters because a mold claim without a clear water-intrusion trail is often the one that gets questioned. If your property sits in an HOA or a historic-district overlay near Van Neste Square or The Stable, we coordinate any required approvals before removing or altering finished materials, since some of those older structures carry additional review for exterior or structural changes.
