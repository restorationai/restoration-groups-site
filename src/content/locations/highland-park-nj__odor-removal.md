## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Highland Park

We start by identifying the actual source material, whether that's subfloor insulation soaked during a storm event, a cast-iron joint that's been weeping for months, or smoke residue clinging to drywall and trim. Surface cleaning comes first: affected materials are cleaned or removed, because no vapor treatment fixes a problem that's still physically present in the wall cavity. From there we select equipment based on whether the space is occupied. Hydroxyl generators run safely around people and pets, which matters in owner-occupied colonials in North Highland Park or Meadow where families can't always relocate during the work. Ozone treatment is reserved for vacant units or unoccupied commercial spaces, since ozone requires the area to be sealed and cleared of people, plants, and animals during treatment. For fire- or smoke-related odor, thermal fogging reaches the same micro-cracks and crevices that smoke particles penetrated, neutralizing odor at a molecular level rather than masking it.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

HEPA air scrubbers run throughout the job to capture particulate before it resettles, which is especially important in older Highland Park homes with plaster walls and original trim that we're trying to preserve rather than replace. Porous materials that can't be fully cleaned, like insulation or certain subfloor products, typically get removed rather than deodorized, since trapped odor in insulation tends to return within weeks if it's just sprayed over. For commercial losses along Raritan Avenue, we coordinate duct cleaning alongside deodorization, since grease-laden ductwork will keep reintroducing odor into dining areas no matter how well the open floor space is treated.
