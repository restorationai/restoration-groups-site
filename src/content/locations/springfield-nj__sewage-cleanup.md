## Why Springfield Sees Sewage Backups

Springfield Township sits low against the Watchung ridges, where the Rahway River's west branch collects runoff before it moves east. When heavy rain pushes that branch past capacity, as it did during Ida, water doesn't just pool in yards; it backs up through municipal sewer laterals and into the lowest fixtures in a house, usually basement floor drains and laundry tubs. The housing stock compounds the problem. A lot of Springfield center and the Mountain Avenue corridor is still 1920s through 1950s colonials and capes with clay or older cast-iron lateral lines that were never sized for the stormwater volume these systems see now. Add a finished basement, which most of these homes have, and a sewer backup turns into a contamination event across carpet pad, drywall, and stored belongings before anyone notices the smell.

Commercial properties along the Route 22 corridor and the Route 22 commercial strip face a related issue: grease and solids buildup in older shared lateral lines serving strip retail and restaurant space, which means a backup at one unit can originate from a blockage well outside that tenant's control.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

We start by identifying the contamination category, since Category 3 water (raw sewage) requires removal of porous materials that can't be fully disinfected, not just drying. That means carpet, pad, and often baseboard and lower drywall come out in affected rooms. We extract standing material, remove and bag contaminated debris per standard handling practice, and apply EPA-registered disinfectant to all affected hard surfaces. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in once the space is physically clean, not before, because drying equipment run on contaminated surfaces just recirculates bacteria through the air. For finished basements, which describes most of what we see in Springfield, we also check wall cavities behind paneling and under subfloor, since sewage wicks upward through drywall faster than clean water does.

We document everything with photos and moisture readings as we go, which matters both for insurance and for confirming the space meets sanitation benchmarks before we close the job.
