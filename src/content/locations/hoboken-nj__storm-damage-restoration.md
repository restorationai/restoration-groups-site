## Why Hoboken Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Hoboken's combined sewer system was built for a much smaller, lower-density city, and it still struggles when heavy rain arrives alongside a storm surge or high tide on the Hudson. That's the mechanism behind what happened during Superstorm Sandy and again with Ida in 2021: water backed up through floor drains and window wells faster than it could be pumped out, flooding basements and below-grade apartments across the city rather than just along the waterfront. Add in wind events that strip shingles, snap tree limbs, and rattle loose the parapet flashing on pre-1900s brownstones, and you get a storm risk profile that's as much about drainage and roofline as it is about rainfall totals. Properties near Pier A Park and the Waterfront see storm surge push first; inland blocks around Midtown and Uptown see the sewer backup problem once the system is overwhelmed.

## Hoboben Insurance Coordination

Most Hoboken storm claims we see involve either wind and roof damage or water intrusion from sewer backup, and carriers tend to treat those two categories very differently. Sewer backup coverage is often a separate rider from standard flood or storm coverage, so we photograph the water source and entry point carefully, since that documentation is often what determines whether a claim gets paid as storm damage or gets kicked back as a sewer line exclusion. We bill most major carriers directly and can walk you through what documentation your specific policy will likely ask for before the adjuster visits.

## Local note

Garden-level and below-grade units in older Hoboken brownstones, especially around Castle Point and the Uptown blocks, often have a single sump pump handling both the foundation drain and a window well that was never properly tied into it. When a storm overwhelms that setup, water backs up through the window well first and the sump alarm doesn't trip until the unit is already wet, which is why we check both systems separately rather than assuming one pump covers the whole load.
