## Why Westfield's Older Homes Present Unique Fire Damage

Many homes in Wychwood, Manor Park, and the streets around Tamaques Park date to the 1890s through the 1930s, with knob-and-tube remnants, aging cast-iron stacks, and plaster-and-lath walls that were never designed for the electrical loads modern households put on them. When fire does start in these walls, it can smolder inside the cavity before it's visible, and the resulting soot penetrates old plaster in a way that drywall doesn't absorb. Add in the near-universal finished basement, often a home theater, office, or gym, and a fire or the smoke and soot that migrates downstairs through HVAC returns can turn a contained kitchen fire into a basement-level loss as well. Original millwork, wide-plank flooring, and plaster crown detail common in these homes also means the rebuild has to match materials that aren't on a shelf at the lumber yard, which changes both the timeline and the documentation we do up front.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Westfield

We start with a damage assessment that separates structural char, surface soot, and smoke odor penetration, because each requires a different approach. Board-up and tarping come first if the roof or windows were compromised, protecting the interior from Westfield's weather while the claim gets underway. From there we run HEPA air scrubbing to address airborne soot before it settles deeper into plaster, upholstery, and the household's belongings, and we dry-clean or wet-clean affected contents depending on the material. For the homes with original woodwork or plaster detail around Downtown Westfield and the Gardens section, we photograph and catalog original trim and finishes before any demolition so the restoration crew and insurance adjuster both have a clear record of what needs to be matched. Odor removal is its own phase: smoke odor binds to plaster and lath in a way it doesn't bind to drywall, so thermal fogging or ozone treatment often runs longer than it would in a newer home.
