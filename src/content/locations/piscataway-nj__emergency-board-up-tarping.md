## Why Piscataway Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Piscataway's position along the Raritan River means wind and water events hit differently depending on where you are in town. River Road and the Johnson Park flatlands take on standing water during heavy rain, and the same storms that flood those low-lying blocks also snap tree limbs onto roofs in Arbor and New Market. Postwar capes and split-levels throughout the city have older roof decking that doesn't hold up well to wind-driven debris, and a cracked roof plane left uncovered overnight can mean soaked attic insulation and ceiling drywall by morning. On the commercial side, the Stelton corridor's warehouse and lab buildings see roof membrane failures and storm-driven skylight breaks that need heavy-duty tarping, not a quick patch.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Piscataway

We start with a structural walk of the damaged area, whether that's a shattered picture window in a Society Hill townhome or a torn section of shingle roof near SHI Stadium. Plywood is cut and fitted to the actual opening rather than a generic size, sealed at the edges, and fastened into solid framing so it holds through follow-on weather, not just the current storm. Roof tarping gets anchored with batten strips and sealed at penetrations rather than relying on tape or staples alone, since a loose tarp in a river-corridor wind gust does almost no good. On properties near Rutgers' Livingston and Busch campuses, where student housing turnover means we're sometimes securing a unit between tenants, we document pre-existing conditions with photos before any board or tarp goes up, which protects both the owner and the next occupant's security deposit conversation.
