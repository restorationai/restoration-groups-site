## Why Roselle Homes Hold Onto Smoke Damage Longer

Older plaster-and-lath walls common near Chestnut Street and Fifth Avenue absorb smoke odor more aggressively than modern drywall. Plaster is porous and slightly alkaline, which bonds readily with the acidic byproducts of combustion, so a fire that looks cosmetically minor on the surface can leave odor compounds locked into the wall assembly for months if it's not treated correctly. Add in older forced-air systems with decades of dust buildup in the ductwork, and soot gets recirculated through the whole house every time the furnace or AC cycles, which is why homes near Warinanco Park and across the older sections of town often need full duct cleaning alongside surface restoration, not just a wipe-down of visible rooms.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Roselle

We start with a walkthrough to classify the smoke type, protein, wet, dry, or fuel-based, because each reacts differently to cleaning agents and dictates the equipment we bring. From there: contents are assessed and either cleaned on-site or packed out, HVAC and duct systems get inspected and cleaned if soot has migrated through them, hard surfaces are treated with the appropriate solvent or detergent based on residue type, and porous materials like drywall, insulation, and carpet padding that can't be fully deodorized get removed. Odor neutralization, through thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators depending on severity, happens last, after source removal, because masking odor before the source is gone just delays the problem. Work is documented and performed in line with IICRC S700 fire restoration guidelines as a neutral industry reference point throughout.
