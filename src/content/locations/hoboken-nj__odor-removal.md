## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Hoboken

We start by identifying the actual source, not just masking the smell, because odor that's been pushed into porous building materials will come back once ambient conditions change. That means pulling baseboards, checking subfloor in below-grade units, and inspecting shared party walls in rowhomes near Midtown and Southwest Hoboken, since odor and moisture can travel along a shared stack between units. Once the source material is cleaned, removed, or sealed, we apply deodorization treatment matched to the odor type and the space: thermal fogging for smoke and fire residue that has settled into fabric and porous surfaces, hydroxyl generators for occupied spaces where people or pets are present during treatment, and ozone treatment for unoccupied units that need a deeper reset, followed by a thorough air-out period before anyone re-enters. Every step is documented for insurance, and we hold an IICRC Certified Firm credential, so the deodorization protocol follows an established industry framework rather than guesswork.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Ozone is effective but it's an oxidizer, not something to run in a home with people, pets, or plants present, so we reserve it for vacant units or scheduled windows when tenants are out, common in Castle Point student housing tied to Stevens Institute turnover. Hydroxyl generators do similar oxidation work more slowly but safely in occupied spaces, which matters in a city where most odor jobs happen in multi-unit buildings and we can't always clear the whole building. Thermal fogging matches the particle size of smoke residue, which lets the deodorizer penetrate the same fabric, wood, and drywall surfaces the original smoke did. HEPA air scrubbers run alongside all of it to pull particulate out of circulation while the treatment works.
