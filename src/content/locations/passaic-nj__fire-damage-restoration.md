## Why Passaic's Building Stock Raises Fire Risk

Passaic is one of the densest small cities in New Jersey, and a lot of that density is early 20th-century brick multi-family housing: two- and three-family frame houses, walkup apartments, and rowhomes built before modern fire-stopping codes existed. Many of these structures still carry knob-and-tube wiring remnants and balloon-frame wall cavities that run uninterrupted from basement to attic. That framing style is efficient for fire, letting flame and smoke travel vertically through the walls long before anyone upstairs smells it. Combine that with overcrowded rental occupancy in buildings around Downtown Passaic and the Fourth Ward, and a single-unit kitchen fire can leave smoke odor and soot residue in every apartment on the stack.

The Main Avenue commercial corridor adds a different pattern: mixed-use storefronts with apartments above, older electrical panels feeding retail equipment never designed for it, and overnight fires that aren't discovered until a tenant or passerby smells smoke hours later. That delay matters. The longer smoke sits on drywall, plaster, and fabric, the deeper the odor and acidic residue set in.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Passaic

We start with a walkthrough to separate structural fire damage from smoke-only damage, since insurers and the local construction office treat them differently. Board-up and tarping come first if the fire breached a roof or window, which is common in the older pitched-roof two-families near the Prospect Park border. From there we run containment to stop soot migration into untouched units (critical in attached rowhomes), then move into debris removal, soot and residue cleaning on remaining structural surfaces, and deodorization using equipment matched to the material: thermal fogging for porous plaster, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for occupied adjacent units where residents can't fully vacate. Multi-family buildings in Passaic often require a construction office sign-off before a fire-damaged unit can be reoccupied, and we document our work in a way that supports that inspection.

## Passaic Insurance Coordination

Fire claims in Passaic's rental-heavy housing stock frequently involve more than one party: an owner's policy, a tenant's renter's insurance, and sometimes a second unit's claim if smoke traveled through a shared wall or stairwell. We photograph and document room by room, including units that show smoke staining but no direct fire exposure, since adjusters often question whether those losses are related. As an IICRC Certified Firm, we follow documented drying and cleaning standards that carriers recognize, which speeds up approval on multi-unit claims.
