## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Newark

Much of Newark's housing was built in the early-to-mid 20th century: brick multifamily buildings, frame two- and three-families, and basement foundations rather than slab-on-grade construction. That basement-heavy stock means water intrusion travels into finished living space quickly, and older masonry foundations hold moisture longer than modern poured concrete. Plumbing in buildings from this era is frequently galvanized steel or cast iron, both prone to internal corrosion and sudden failure, and older housing stock citywide can carry asbestos-containing materials or lead paint, which a restoration scope has to account for before demolition or drying work begins. The city's combined sewer system and Passaic River proximity in low-lying areas like the Ironbound add drainage pressure during heavy rain that newer sewer-separated towns don't face. Structural repair permits in Newark are issued through the city's code enforcement and construction office, and flood-zone rules apply more strictly to properties near the river.
