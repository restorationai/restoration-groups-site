## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in New Brunswick

We start with a visual walkthrough and moisture mapping, using meters to check baseboards, subfloor, and any wall cavity near a known plumbing run or exterior wall. If conditions suggest active growth or if a client needs documentation for a sale, rental dispute, or insurance claim, we collect air samples with a spore trap pump in the suspect area and a control sample from outside, plus surface swabs on any visible growth. ## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Accurate testing depends on using the right tool for the building, not running the same checklist everywhere. We use non-invasive moisture meters for a first pass, then follow up with a borescope where we need to look inside a wall or subfloor cavity without opening it up unnecessarily, which matters in older plaster-and-lath construction around campus where repeated patching gets expensive. Thermal imaging helps flag temperature differentials that point to trapped moisture behind drywall or tile, especially useful in the downtown high-rises where a leak three floors up can show up as a stain two units away. Air sampling pumps and calibrated cassettes give us lab-verifiable spore counts rather than a guess based on smell or appearance alone, which matters if the report needs to hold up for an insurance claim or a landlord dispute.
