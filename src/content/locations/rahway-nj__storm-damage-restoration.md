## Why Rahway Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Rahway's geography works against it during severe weather. The river corridor through Rahway River Park and the downtown business district sits low enough that even a few inches of rise can push water into finished basements and ground-floor retail along Main Street. Victorian and 1920s homes near downtown often have additions, converted porches, or low-pitched roof sections that were never engineered for sustained wind uplift, while postwar capes and colonials out toward Milton Lake and Leesville carry mature street trees whose root systems compete with aging storm drains, so a heavy downpour backs up faster than it would in a newer subdivision. In Hazelwood and the blocks near the Rahway Train Station, older cast-iron storm laterals and original clay connections slow drainage even further, which means standing water lingers on streets and in window wells long after the rain stops.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Rahway

The first visit is about stopping further loss: tarping exposed roof sections, boarding broken windows or doors, and extracting standing water before it migrates into wall cavities or subflooring. On pre-1950 homes near downtown, we pull baseboard and test moisture behind plaster rather than assuming drywall-style drying times, since plaster releases water slowly and can hide a saturated lath layer for days. For properties closer to the Merck & Co. campus and the newer transit-oriented buildings around the train station, storm losses more often involve roof membrane damage, sprinkler head impact, or wind-driven rain intrusion at curtain wall seams, which calls for a different moisture mapping approach than a single-family basement. Every job is documented with photos and moisture readings dried to the IICRC S500 standard, which also gives your adjuster a clean record to work from.

Most Rahway storm claims we see fall into wind, tree impact, or flood categories, and each one is handled differently by carriers.
