## Why Staten Island Basements Flood

Staten Island's East Shore, South Beach, Midland Beach, and Oakwood Beach, took the brunt of Hurricane Sandy in 2012, and the ground-level and below-grade spaces along that corridor still sit closer to the water table than most of the borough. More recently, Hurricane Ida's 2021 rainfall overwhelmed storm drains on low-lying South Shore streets near Great Kills and Tottenville, backing water up through basement floor drains and window wells in neighborhoods that aren't technically coastal flood zones. Add in the borough's housing mix, early-1900s frame homes with fieldstone or parged foundations, postwar Cape Cods and split-levels with finished rec rooms below grade, and newer townhome developments with shared drainage, and you get a wide range of entry points: foundation cracks, failed sump pumps, window well seepage, and aging cast-iron sewer laterals that back up under heavy rain.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Staten Island

We start by identifying the water source and category, since storm surge or sewer backup water is handled differently from a clean supply-line break. Extraction comes first, using truck-mounted or submersible pumps depending on depth, followed by removal of saturated carpet, pad, and any drywall or paneling that's wicked water above the baseboard line. In older St. George and New Dorp homes with masonry or parged foundation walls, we check behind finished surfaces for trapped moisture, since block and stone hold water longer than modern poured concrete and can keep feeding humidity back into a room weeks after it looks dry. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: we set air movers and dehumidifiers based on room volume and material, then monitor moisture content in framing and subfloor until readings stabilize, not just until the surface feels dry.

## Staten Island Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies treat storm surge and overland flooding differently from a plumbing failure or appliance leak, which matters a lot on the East Shore where flood insurance through the NFIP often runs alongside a standard HO policy. We document water lines, affected materials, and moisture readings as we go, which gives your adjuster, or two adjusters if both policies are in play, a clear record of what happened and what it took to dry the space. For sewer backup claims, which we see more often in the older cast-iron plumbing common near Port Richmond and St. George, we note the backup source separately since many policies require a specific sewer/drain backup endorsement to cover that type of loss.
