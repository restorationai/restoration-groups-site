North Bergen's housing stock skews toward early 20th-century multi-family walkups and brick two- and three-family homes stacked on hillside lots along the Palisades ridge. Those buildings share walls, shared ductwork in some cases, and plumbing chases that let odor travel between units in ways a detached single-family home never would. Add in the low-lying flats near Tonnelle Avenue and the Meadowlands edge, where storm drains back up during heavy rain, and you get a recurring pattern: standing water in a basement or crawl space, followed weeks later by a musty, sour smell that outlasts the visible damage. Commercial spaces along the Bergenline Avenue business district and the Tonnelle Avenue commercial corridor deal with a different version of the same problem, roof leaks and sprinkler discharge that soak into drop ceilings and insulation, where odor sits and spreads through ventilation systems serving multiple storefronts.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in North Bergen

We start by identifying the actual source material, not just the room where the smell is strongest. In older North Bergen buildings that often means pulling baseboards or accessing wall cavities near shared plumbing lines, since cast-iron drains common in pre-1950 construction can leach sewer gas odor into surrounding framing long after a clog or backup is cleared. Once the source is addressed, we match the deodorization method to the material: porous surfaces like plaster, subfloor, and insulation generally need more than surface treatment. For smoke odor from a kitchen or appliance fire, thermal fogging penetrates the same cracks and pores the smoke originally did, neutralizing residue at a molecular level rather than covering it. For broader whole-room or whole-unit odor, we use ozone treatment or hydroxyl deodorization depending on whether the space can be vacated during treatment. Ozone works fast but requires the area be unoccupied; hydroxyl generators run safely in occupied buildings, which matters in North Bergen's multi-family stock where you can't always clear an entire building for treatment.

## Equipment & Methods We Use

The right tool depends on what's actually driving the smell. Standing water or sewage odor typically calls for antimicrobial treatment alongside deodorization, since bacterial activity is part of what's generating the smell in the first place. Smoke damage from a stove fire or electrical event often needs sealant applied to charred or scorched surfaces after thermal fogging, so the odor doesn't reactivate with humidity. We also run air scrubbers with carbon filtration during and after treatment in tighter spaces, which matters in older North Bergen units near Woodcliff and the Fairview border where windows don't always open wide enough for natural ventilation to help the process along.
