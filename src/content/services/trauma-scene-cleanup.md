## What Trauma Scene Cleanup actually involves

Trauma scene cleanup covers the decontamination and sanitization of areas affected by a death, violent crime, suicide, or serious accident. It is distinct from a general cleaning job: technicians wear sealed PPE, work under containment to prevent cross-contamination, and treat all affected materials as a biohazard until proven otherwise. Depending on what's involved, that can mean removing and disposing of saturated carpet, padding, or drywall, treating subfloor and framing members that absorbed fluid, and applying hospital-grade disinfectants and enzymatic cleaners rated for bloodborne pathogens. Work is typically scheduled once law enforcement or the medical examiner releases the scene, and our teams are reachable 24/7 to coordinate that timing with families, property managers, or funeral homes.

## Our process

1. **Confirm scene release and scope the affected area.** We coordinate with the family, property manager, or responding agency to confirm the scene is released and identify every surface or material that may have been affected, not just what's visible.
2. **Stage PPE and establish containment.** Technicians suit up in sealed protective equipment and set up containment barriers to keep the work area isolated from the rest of the property.
3. **Remove and dispose of contaminated materials.** Porous materials that can't be fully decontaminated, carpet, padding, certain drywall sections, are removed and bagged as regulated waste under a documented waste manifest.
4. **Decontaminate and treat remaining surfaces.** Hard surfaces and salvageable structural materials are cleaned with hospital-grade disinfectants and enzymatic treatments designed to neutralize bloodborne pathogens and biological odor at the source.
5. **Verify and deodorize.** A final inspection confirms no visible or odor-producing residue remains before the area is cleared for normal use or further repair work.

## What separates a good trauma scene cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating this like a standard cleaning job: wiping visible surfaces without checking whether fluid has wicked into subflooring, baseboards, or wall cavities. Blood and other fluids travel along grout lines and gaps in flooring, and if that path isn't traced and treated, odor and contamination can resurface weeks later. A careful crew documents every material removed with photos and a waste manifest, both for the family's peace of mind and for insurance or liability records. Adjusters reviewing these claims generally want to see proof of proper biohazard waste disposal through a licensed handler, not just a description of cleaning performed, so documentation matters as much as the physical work.
