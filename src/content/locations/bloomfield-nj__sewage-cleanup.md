## Why Bloomfield Properties See Sewage Backups

Much of Bloomfield's housing stock, the colonials, two-families, and Tudors built between the 1900s and 1930s, still runs on original cast-iron waste lines feeding into aging municipal sewer mains. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out over a century of use, narrowing the pipe until roots, grease, or a heavy rain event is enough to send wastewater back up through the lowest drain in the house, almost always a finished basement. The Third River (Yantacaw) and Second River both run close to Watsessing and Brookdale, and both neighborhoods took on significant sewer backup during Ida in 2021 when stormwater overwhelmed the combined system faster than it could drain. Postwar capes further from the rivers aren't immune either: a failed sump pump during a sudden downpour is one of the most common calls we get from basements across North Center and Brookside.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Bloomfield

Category 3 water, which is what raw sewage is classified as, requires full removal of any porous material it touched, not just extraction. In Bloomfield's finished basements that often means carpet, pad, and baseboard, and sometimes drywall up to the flood line, especially in homes near Watsessing Park or Brookdale Park where backups have historically run deeper and sat longer before being noticed. We extract standing wastewater, remove and bag contaminated materials per IICRC S500 protocols, and apply EPA-registered antimicrobials to framing and subfloor before structural drying equipment goes in. Cast-iron lateral failures mean the plumbing fix and the cleanup often happen on parallel tracks, we coordinate timing with a plumber or the homeowner's own contractor so drying isn't undone by a second backup.
