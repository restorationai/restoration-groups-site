Maplewood's housing stock is dominated by 1910s through 1930s Tudors, colonials, and craftsman homes, many with original plaster walls, stone or parged foundations, and finished basements that were never designed for today's storm intensity. When a nor'easter or a remnant hurricane dumps several inches of rain in a few hours, the East Branch of the Rahway River, which runs through Memorial Park and past Maplewood Village, backs up quickly. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Maplewood

The first visit focuses on stabilizing the structure: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows or door openings, and extracting standing water before it works into plaster and subfloor. In homes with original lath-and-plaster walls, we're deliberate about drying times; plaster holds and releases moisture far slower than drywall, so a wall that reads dry at the surface after 24 hours can still be wet behind the lath. We track moisture with meters at the stud and joist level, not just the surface, before we call a space dry. Stone foundation basements, common throughout Tuscan and The Fields, get particular attention for seepage through old mortar joints, which storm surges tend to expose even in homes with no prior flooding history. Debris removal, including downed branches and damaged fencing, typically runs alongside the structural work so the property is secured the same visit. Everything is documented and dried in line with IICRC S500 drying standards, and our crews operate as an IICRC Certified Firm and under New Jersey's Home Improvement Contractor license.
