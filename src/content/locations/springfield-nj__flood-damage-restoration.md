## Why Springfield Floods the Way It Does

Springfield sits in a low basin where the Rahway River's west branch collects runoff coming off the Watchung ridges before it has anywhere to go. During a sustained storm, that water doesn't stay in the riverbed. It pushes into the low spots around Springfield center, along the Route 22 corridor, and into basements near the Baltusrol area that sit only a few feet above the water table. Most homes here were built in the 1920s through the 1950s as colonials and capes, with finished basements added decades after original construction. Newer townhome complexes toward the Millburn line have drywall basements that take on water differently than the plaster-and-lath construction common closer to Mountain Avenue, and that difference changes how fast a space actually dries, not just how fast it extracts.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Springfield

Flood water in Springfield is rarely clean. It has typically picked up runoff from Route 22, lawn chemicals, and sediment from the riverbank before it reaches a basement, so we treat it as contaminated water from the first visit and sanitize affected materials rather than assuming a simple dry-out will do. Extraction comes first, followed by moisture mapping so we know what's actually wet behind the walls, not just what looks wet on the surface. In older capes and colonials with plaster basement walls, extraction moves quickly but full drying takes longer because plaster releases moisture gradually. In the newer construction near the Millburn line, drywall and modern insulation dry faster but are more likely to need replacement once they've been sitting in category 3 water. We dry per the IICRC S500 standard and document moisture readings throughout, since that record matters as much to your claim as it does to the drying process.

## Springfield Insurance & HOA Coordination

A lot of Springfield homeowners assume a standard homeowners policy covers river flooding, and a lot are surprised to learn it doesn't; flood coverage through the NFIP is usually a separate policy, and whether you have one often depends on whether your street has flooded before. We photograph and document the loss in detail so that claim, whichever policy it falls under, has the paper trail it needs. For the townhome communities built toward the Millburn line, flood cleanup often touches common-area drainage or shared basement walls, which means coordinating with the HOA on top of the homeowner's own claim. We work directly with property managers on those jobs so repairs on one unit don't get held up waiting on a board decision about the shared system.
