## Restoration emergencies common in Cranford

Cranford earned its old nickname, the Venice of New Jersey, because the Rahway River runs directly through town, and that same geography has produced the most serious repeat-flood history in the county. Floyd in 1999, Irene in 2011 and Ida in 2021 each put whole sections of Cranford underwater, and the pattern isn't random. Homes along the Riverside Drive area and other low blocks near the river flood from the Rahway itself, often fast and with real current behind it. The rest of town, including streets further from the water, floods when storm sewers get overwhelmed faster than they can drain, pushing water back up through basement floor drains and window wells. Either way, the result is the same: saturated basements under 1900s to 1940s colonials and capes, many of which were never built with flood events like Ida in mind. Mold risk follows close behind, since these basements often stay damp for days after the water recedes if nobody intervenes.
