## Why Highland Park's Older Housing Stock Changes the Fire Response

Much of Highland Park, from Downtown Highland Park through the Triangle and up toward North Highland Park, was built between the 1910s and 1940s as colonials, bungalows, and converted two-families. A lot of that stock uses balloon framing, where the wall cavity runs uninterrupted from the basement sill to the attic. That means a fire that starts contained in a kitchen or a wall outlet can travel vertically much faster than it would in a home built to modern fire-stop requirements, and soot often reaches the attic and roof sheathing even when the visible flame damage never left the first floor. ## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Highland Park

We start by securing the structure, boarding openings and tarping roof damage so the property isn't exposed to weather or further loss overnight. From there we document and photograph affected materials room by room, including attic and wall-cavity intrusion in older framing, before any demolition begins. Soot and smoke residue get cleaned from framing, subfloor, and salvageable finishes using methods matched to the surface, since plaster, old plaster lath, and original trim each hold residue differently than modern materials. Contents that can be saved move through our pack-out and cleaning process off-site, and the remaining structure goes through odor removal and deodorization before we release it back for repair work.
