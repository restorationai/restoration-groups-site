## Why Plainfield's Older Homes Hold Onto Smoke Damage Longer

Plainfield is known for its Victorian and Queen Anne housing stock, especially around the Van Wyck Brooks Historic District, and that same character that makes these homes special also makes them harder to clear of smoke. Plaster walls, horsehair lath, and original wood millwork are porous in ways modern drywall isn't, so smoke residue migrates into the material itself rather than sitting on the surface. In denser pockets like Netherwood and West End, where houses sit close together, soot and odor can also travel through shared attic spaces or older duct runs, affecting a structure even when the fire itself was next door. New Jersey's humid summers make this worse: wet smoke residue is acidic, and in high humidity it etches into paint, metal fixtures, and glass faster than it would in a dry climate, which is part of why we prioritize early response over waiting on an insurance adjuster's schedule.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Plainfield

We start with a walkthrough to separate protein, wet, and dry smoke residue, since each reacts differently to cleaning agents and dictates whether we're wiping, dry sponging, or using wet cleaning methods. On pre-1950s plaster and original woodwork, especially in the Historic District, we avoid aggressive abrasive techniques that can strip finish or damage trim profiles, favoring controlled chemical sponges and HEPA vacuuming instead. HVAC systems get inspected and, where needed, professionally cleaned, because duct contamination is one of the most common reasons smoke odor returns weeks after a job looks finished. For heavier odor loads we bring in thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators rather than masking the smell with fragrance, and contents that can be safely cleaned on-site or packed out are documented and photographed before anything leaves the property.
