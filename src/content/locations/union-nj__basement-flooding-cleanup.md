## Why Union Properties See Basement Flooding Issues

Most of Union was built out in the 1940s through 60s: capes, split-levels, and colonials with basements that were finished or semi-finished decades ago, often with supply lines and cast-iron drains original to the house. Those systems are past their expected service life, and when a pipe fails or a drain backs up, it happens below grade where nobody sees it until the carpet is soaked. Low-lying stretches off Morris Avenue and around Vauxhall take the brunt of it during hard summer storms, and the same tributary network that jumped its banks during Ida still pushes groundwater against foundation walls after a few consecutive days of heavy rain. Sump pumps installed in the 1980s and 90s are frequently undersized for what the ground is producing now, and a lot of Union basements don't have battery backup, so a power outage during the storm that caused the flooding is often the reason the pump failed at the worst possible moment.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Union

We start by identifying the source, because a cracked foundation wall, a failed sump, and a sewer backup each call for different handling, and treating a sewage event like clean groundwater intrusion is a mistake we won't make. Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and truck-mounted extraction, then we check what's underneath: Union's older finished basements often have carpet over plywood subfloor or carpet laid straight on slab, and each holds water differently. Baseboards, drywall cut lines, and built-in paneling common in these split-level rec rooms get opened or removed where moisture has tracked up the wall, because capillary action can pull water a foot or more above the waterline within a day. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the room and monitored with moisture meters until the readings confirm the structure, not just the surface, is dry.
