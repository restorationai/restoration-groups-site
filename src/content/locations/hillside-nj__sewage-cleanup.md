## Why Hillside Sees Sewage Backups

Hillside's older homes, concentrated around Hillside Center, Saybrook, and Winans, were built with clay or cast-iron lateral lines that have had seventy-plus years to corrode, shift, and collect roots. When those laterals partially clog, the first sign is often a slow-draining basement floor drain or a gurgling toilet on the lowest level, hours before an actual backup. Add in the township's low-lying ground near the Elizabeth River, and heavy rain events (Ida in 2021 pushed river water and sewage into basements across town) overwhelm the municipal system faster than homeowners expect. Sump pumps that can't keep pace with summer downpours compound the problem, pulling groundwater and sewage into the same finished or semi-finished basement space. Around the Route 22 corridor and the Conant Street industrial area, the pattern looks different: overnight warehouse and commercial losses tied to sprinkler discharge, roof leaks, or sewer lateral failures that nobody discovers until a Monday morning walkthrough.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

Sewage is a Category 3 loss under IICRC S500 guidelines, meaning every porous material it touches, carpet, drywall, baseboard, subfloor, gets treated as contaminated and typically removed rather than dried in place. Our process starts with containment to stop cross-contamination into unaffected rooms, followed by extraction of standing wastewater, removal of saturated porous materials, and antimicrobial treatment of structural surfaces. In Hillside's finished basements, that often means cutting drywall above the flood line, pulling baseboard, and treating exposed framing before air movers and dehumidifiers go in to bring the space back to a dry standard. Odor control follows, since sewage residue left in subfloor seams or wall cavities will keep smelling long after the water is gone. We document every step with photos and moisture readings for your insurance file.
