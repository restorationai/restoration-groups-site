## Why Hoboken Properties See Basement Flooding Issues

Hoboken's geography works against basements. The city's low elevation and marsh-fill soil mean groundwater sits close to the surface, and storm surge from events like Superstorm Sandy in 2012 and the remnants of Ida in 2021 pushed river and sewer water into garden-level units across the city, not just near the waterfront. Southwest Hoboken and parts of Downtown Hoboken tend to sit at the lowest grades and see water first when the storm sewers can't keep pace with rainfall. Add in the city's dense stock of 19th-century brownstones and brick rowhomes, many converted into multi-unit buildings with below-grade apartments, and you get aging cast-iron and galvanized plumbing stacks shared across party walls. A pipe failure two floors up can flood a basement unit just as fast as a storm can.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Hoboken

We start by identifying the source, storm or sewer backup versus a plumbing failure, because that determines whether the water is contaminated and how we handle disposal and sanitizing. Standing water comes out first with submersible pumps and truck-mounted extraction, then we pull baseboards and check behind finished basement walls, which is common in Hoboken's converted garden units. Moisture mapping with meters and thermal imaging tells us what's actually wet under flooring and inside wall cavities, since surface-dry drywall can hide a saturated stud bay. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard with placed air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the space, and we recheck moisture levels daily until materials read dry, not just until the room looks dry.

## Hoboken Co-op, Condo, and Insurance Coordination

A large share of Hoboken's basement and garden-level units sit inside buildings governed by condo or co-op associations, which means a flooding event often involves more than one owner's policy. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings that both the unit owner's carrier and the building's master policy can use, which matters when a shared stack or a common-area drain is the source. For buildings near the Washington Street business district with ground-floor commercial tenants above residential basements, we coordinate scheduling with property management so cleanup doesn't conflict with business hours or shared-hallway access.
