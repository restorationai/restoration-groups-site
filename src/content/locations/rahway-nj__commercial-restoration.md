## Why Rahway Businesses See Water and Fire Losses

Downtown Rahway and the low-lying blocks near Rahway River Park carry a flood history that property managers already know well, Hurricane Ida in 2021 pushed water into ground-floor commercial space along the riverfront corridor, and the Irene-era storms before it did the same. Buildings in Central Rahway and the Main Street business district often date to the early-to-mid 1900s, with commercial basements and mechanical rooms sitting below grade and tied into aging municipal drain lines that back up faster than a newer system would. Add the transit-oriented development around the train station, where multi-unit buildings mix residential and retail plumbing in the same stack, and a single failure can affect several tenants at once. Office buildings near the Merck & Co. campus and the Clark border tend to be newer construction with sprinkler systems, which means fire-related losses there are frequently water losses first: a tripped head or a frozen line in an unoccupied suite can flood a floor before anyone notices.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Rahway

Commercial work starts with a different set of priorities than a home. We assess the loss against your business operations first: what areas need to be isolated to keep the rest of the space open, what the insurance carrier or property manager needs documented before demolition starts, and what code or fire marshal sign-off applies before equipment comes out. For a retail space on Main Street, that might mean containment barriers so the storefront stays visible and partially open during drying. For a multi-tenant building near the train station, it means coordinating access and notice across units so one leak doesn't become a liability issue for every lease in the building. We document moisture readings and affected materials as we go, which matters for commercial claims that often involve business interruption coverage in addition to property damage.
