## Why Somerville Floods the Way It Does

Somerville sits in a floodplain shaped by two waterways, the Raritan River and Peters Brook, both of which left their mark during Hurricane Ida in 2021 and in earlier storms going back to Floyd and Irene. Low-lying blocks near these channels see water rise through foundation walls and floor drains rather than just through a broken window or door, which changes how a flood behaves indoors. A lot of Somerville's housing stock, the early-1900s frame two-families and Victorians around East Main and Mercer Street in particular, was built with finished basements added decades after original construction. Those basements often carry galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains that were never designed for repeated submersion, so a flood event doesn't just wet the floor, it stresses plumbing joints and baseboard trim that were already aging before the water arrived.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Somerville

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out of basements and ground floors before it has a chance to wick further into plaster, subfloor, or insulation. In Somerville's older homes, that means checking behind baseboards and under finished basement flooring in Fairview and Gaston Avenue properties, where water tends to pool against fieldstone or parged block foundations rather than draining away. Moisture mapping comes next: we use meters to track how far water traveled vertically into framing and horizontally under flooring, since flood water in a century-old frame house rarely stops where it looks like it stopped. Once the extent is documented for insurance and for our own drying plan, we set extraction equipment and air movers calibrated to the material, plaster dries slower than drywall, and a finished basement with carpet over concrete needs a different airflow pattern than an open crawl space. We also keep an eye on downtown storefronts along the Main Street business district, where flood water can mix with runoff carrying debris from the sidewalk level and needs a different sanitizing step than a residential basement.

## Somerville Insurance Coordination

Many of the properties we see near the Raritan River and in low sections of Downtown fall inside mapped flood zones, which usually means a separate flood policy from NFIP or a private carrier rather than standard homeowners coverage. We document room dimensions, affected materials, and moisture readings in a format adjusters recognize, and we coordinate directly with your carrier so you are not stuck translating between two different conversations. For borough residents near Wallace House or the newer apartment buildings downtown, we also watch for permit requirements tied to Somerset County floodplain rules before any wet material is removed, since documentation before demolition matters for both the claim and the eventual permit sign-off.

## Local note

Homes near Peters Brook and the lower blocks of Gaston Avenue often have fieldstone or parged block foundation walls rather than poured concrete, and those walls hold moisture differently than newer construction. Even after visible water is gone, the stone and mortar keep releasing moisture into the basement air for days, so we run dehumidification longer on those jobs than a standard poured-foundation basement would need, and we tell homeowners up front so the extra equipment time doesn't come as a surprise.
