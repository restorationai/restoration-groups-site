## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Millburn

We start by identifying the source material, not just the room where the smell is strongest. In older Millburn homes, that often means pulling baseboards or accessing a crawl space to check for saturated subfloor or insulation that's been quietly off-gassing for weeks. Once we've removed or treated the source (porous materials that can't be saved are discarded, salvageable ones are cleaned and dried per IICRC S500 drying standards), we select a deodorization method based on the space: hydroxyl generators for occupied homes where people and pets are present, since they work without the evacuation ozone treatment requires, and ozone treatment for vacant properties or sealed areas where a deeper oxidation cycle is appropriate. For smoke odor that's worked into plaster, wood trim, or HVAC ductwork, thermal fogging reaches into the same microscopic pores the smoke did, neutralizing odor molecules rather than covering them.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

No single tool handles every odor case, which matters in a town with this much variation in building age. Hydroxyl generators are our default in finished, occupied basements, since Millburn's older homes often have residents or home offices downstairs even during remediation. Ozone generators go into unoccupied spaces, commercial units, or post-fire jobs where we need a stronger oxidizing reaction and can control re-entry time. Thermal fogging is reserved for smoke and heavy odor penetration in wood-heavy interiors, which describes a large share of the Tudor and colonial stock around Short Hills. We pair all of it with air scrubbing and, where source material was removed, moisture readings to confirm the area is dry before any deodorization step begins, since treating odor without solving the underlying moisture just invites it back.
