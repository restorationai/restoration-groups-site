## Why Manhattan Fire Damage Spreads Differently

A kitchen fire on the 9th floor of a pre-war co-op near the Upper West Side can push smoke through the original plaster chases and shared vent stacks straight into the apartments above. Many buildings between Midtown and the Upper East Side still carry original lath-and-plaster walls, cast-iron radiators, and decades of paint layers that off-gas differently than drywall when heated, which changes how deep smoke odor sets into the structure. In newer condo towers, sealed-tight construction traps smoke rather than letting it vent, so odor and soot can settle into carpet, ductwork, and upholstery in rooms the fire never reached. Either way, the damage a fire department report calls "contained" often isn't contained for restoration purposes at all.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process Here

We start with a unit-by-unit assessment, documenting soot pattern, heat damage, and water used during suppression, since most Manhattan fire losses are really combined fire and water jobs. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with moisture logs kept for every affected unit, which resident managers and managing agents routinely request before signing off on repairs. Content that can be saved goes through pack-out, cleaning, and deodorization rather than disposal where possible, since replacement and delivery logistics in a Manhattan building add real time and cost. Odor treatment is the step people underestimate: thermal fogging and HEPA air scrubbing address what surface cleaning can't reach inside wall cavities and old ductwork.
