## Why Irvington Properties See Mold Issues

Much of Irvington's housing stock dates to the early 1900s, rowhouses and two- and three-family homes built with plaster walls, cast-iron drains, and galvanized supply lines that were never designed to last a century. When those pipes weep slowly behind a wall or a cast-iron stack develops a hairline crack, the leak often goes unnoticed until someone smells it. Add in the finished and semi-finished basements common throughout Irvington Center and Grove Park, and you get enclosed, poorly ventilated spaces where a small plumbing drip can turn into a colonized wall cavity within days. The Elizabeth River watershed that drains this part of Essex County also means low-lying blocks near Union Avenue and the Springfield Avenue corridor hold groundwater longer after a hard rain, which keeps basement slabs and sill plates damp well after a storm has passed.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Irvington

We start with a visual walkthrough of the property, paying close attention to the areas Irvington's older construction tends to hide problems: behind baseboard in finished basements, around cast-iron drain penetrations, and along exterior walls facing the prevailing weather. A moisture meter checks framing and subfloor readings at each suspect area, and a thermal imaging pass can flag temperature differentials that point to trapped moisture behind plaster or drywall without opening the wall. If conditions warrant, we collect air samples and, where visible growth is present, surface samples, then send them to an independent, accredited lab for spore counts and species identification. You get a written report with lab results, photos, and moisture readings, which is the same documentation most insurance carriers and landlords want to see before any remediation work begins.
