**Smoke or sewage odor that won't leave a Brick Church walk-up or an Ampere two-family?** Odor is often the last thing to clear after a kitchen fire, a sewer backup, or a slow appliance leak, and in East Orange's older brick buildings that smell can work its way into plaster, subfloor, and shared stairwells long after the visible damage is gone. We treat the source first, then deodorize the structure with equipment matched to the material, not just an air freshener pass.

## Why East Orange Properties See Odor Issues

A large share of East Orange's housing stock, the three-story brick walk-ups around Brick Church and the Victorians and two-families scattered through Elmwood and Doddtown, was built before 1940. Plaster, horsehair lath, and old wood subfloor absorb smoke particulate and sewage gas differently than drywall does, holding odor compounds deep in the material long after surfaces look clean. Add in aging cast-iron stacks and galvanized risers that back up during heavy rain, and basement mechanical rooms that flood in low-lying sections near the Second River, and you get a recurring pattern: water or sewage intrusion today, a musty or sour odor weeks later once the hidden moisture has had time to interact with old building materials.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in East Orange

We start by identifying the odor source rather than masking it, which in multi-family buildings often means checking shared chases, under-cabinet plumbing, and crawl spaces that run between units. Porous materials that have absorbed smoke or sewage odor (plaster, insulation, some subfloor) sometimes need to come out; everything else gets cleaned, sealed, or treated in place. For smoke jobs we also wipe down hard surfaces to remove the oily soot film that re-releases odor when the building warms up, which matters in older radiator-heated units around Presidential Estates where room temperatures swing more than in newer forced-air buildings.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

The treatment method depends on what caused the odor and who's occupying the space during the work. Hydroxyl generators break down odor molecules without producing ozone, which lets us run them in occupied apartment buildings where tenants can't vacate for the treatment, a common situation in East Orange's denser rental blocks. Thermal fogging pushes a deodorizing agent into the same cracks and crevices smoke penetrated, useful for fire-damaged units with a lot of old trim and baseboard. Ozone treatment is reserved for vacant, sealed spaces, since it requires the area to be unoccupied for a set period; we schedule it accordingly and confirm the space is clear before running equipment.

## East Orange Insurance Coordination

Most odor removal and deodorization work in East Orange ties back to a water, fire, or sewage claim already in progress, so we document the odor source with photos and moisture readings as part of that same claim file rather than opening a separate one. For multi-unit buildings near the Central Avenue business district, that documentation also helps sort out which unit's claim covers shared-wall or stairwell treatment, a detail adjusters ask about often in attached housing.

## Local note

Buildings within a few blocks of the Brick Church station and the Central Avenue corridor tend to have sealed-up transoms and painted-shut windows from decades of renovations, which traps treated air and actually helps hydroxyl and fogging treatments work faster since the agent isn't venting out through gaps. We check for that before setting equipment runtimes, because a building that holds air well needs less treatment time than one with open stairwell vents pulling fresh air through the whole floor.
