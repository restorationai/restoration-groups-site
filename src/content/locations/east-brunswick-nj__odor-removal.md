## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process

We start by identifying the actual source material, not just the room where the smell is strongest, because masking an odor without removing or sealing what's producing it means it comes back within a season. For basement and water-related odor, that usually means pulling baseboard, checking subfloor moisture content, and in some cases removing contaminated pad or insulation before any deodorizing step begins. Once the source is addressed, we apply hydroxyl generators or ozone treatment depending on whether the space is occupied, ozone needs an unoccupied, sealed-off area and longer dwell time, while hydroxyl units can run in spaces where people or pets are present, which matters in attached condo buildings where neighboring units share ventilation. For smoke and fire-related odor, thermal fogging reaches into the same microscopic cracks and crevices that smoke particles penetrated, neutralizing odor at a molecular level rather than coating it. Every job is documented and dried or treated according to IICRC S500 guidelines so the process holds up if an insurance carrier requests records later.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Which method we deploy depends on the material and the building type. Thermal fogging works well on fire-damaged wood framing and textiles where smoke residue has bonded to the surface. Hydroxyl generators are our default for occupied townhomes and condo clusters because they don't require evacuation and won't damage rubber seals or electronics the way ozone can. Ozone treatment gets reserved for vacant properties, post-move-out rentals, or sealed basement sections where we can control exposure time precisely. For lingering sewage or appliance-leak odor in finished basements, we often pair deodorization with targeted drying equipment so the treatment doesn't just mask moisture that's still actively feeding the smell.
