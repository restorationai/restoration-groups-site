## Why Bloomfield Basements Flood

Much of Bloomfield was built out between the 1900s and 1930s: colonials, two-families, and Tudors with finished basements sitting on original galvanized supply lines and cast-iron waste stacks that are now well past their expected service life. Those systems don't usually fail gradually, they fail at a joint, all at once, often while nobody's home. Add postwar capes built after World War II with shallower foundations and less perimeter drainage, and you get a town where a single heavy storm can turn a finished rec room into standing water within an hour.

Geography compounds it. The Third River, known locally as the Yantacaw, and the Second River both run through Bloomfield, and the Watsessing and Brookdale areas sit close enough to feel it when either waterway rises. During Hurricane Ida in 2021, that combination pushed stormwater back through municipal sewer lines and into basements across both neighborhoods, not just from rain falling on the house but from water with nowhere else to go. Summer cloudbursts do a smaller version of the same thing every year, and sump pump failure during those storms is the single most common call we get from Bloomfield homeowners.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process

We start by identifying the source, because a sewer backup and a sump failure get handled differently from a day one safety standpoint, and sewage-contaminated water changes what materials can be saved. Once the source is controlled, we extract standing water with truck-mounted pumps, then move to drying.

Bloomfield's finished basements usually mean paneling, carpet over concrete, and sometimes drywall partitions down to the slab, all of which hold moisture differently than an unfinished space. We pull baseboards and float drywall where needed, set up negative-pressure drying per the IICRC S500 standard, and monitor moisture in the framing and subfloor daily rather than assuming a dry surface means a dry wall cavity. In pre-1930s homes with masonry foundations, the block itself can wick moisture for days after the visible water is gone, so we extend monitoring instead of closing out early.

One thing crews learn fast working Bloomfield: homes near the Third River in Watsessing and Brookdale often have finished basements built below the actual flood-prone grade, meaning the pump and discharge line are doing more structural work than the original builder ever accounted for.
