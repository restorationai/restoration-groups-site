## Why Cranford Properties See Burst Pipe Issues

Cranford's housing stock runs heavy on 1900s through 1940s colonials and capes, many with original or early-replacement galvanized steel supply lines feeding full basements. That pipe material corrodes from the inside out, and by the time a pinhole becomes a split, the surrounding fitting is often just as weak. Add in winters where unheated crawl spaces and basement corners near exterior walls dip below freezing, and you get the classic Cranford pattern: a pipe fails at a joint near the foundation, and water finds its way toward the lowest point in the house before anyone notices. Homes in Cranford West and Lincoln Park East, built on similar vintage plumbing, see this just as often as the riverside blocks, even though their flood risk from the Rahway River itself is lower.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Cranford

First call is always the same: shut off the water at the main, then get a technician on site to isolate the break and stop ongoing loss. From there we extract standing water, pull baseboards and wet drywall where moisture has tracked into wall cavities, and set up directed airflow and dehumidification sized to the room, not a generic basement estimate. Because so many Cranford basements have seen prior flood damage from the river or from overwhelmed storm sewers, we check for pre-existing moisture staining or prior mold treatment before we start, so the documentation for your claim clearly separates this pipe break from any older flood history on the property. Repairs to the pipe itself are coordinated with a licensed plumber where code requires it, and we photograph the failed section before it's replaced.

## Cranford Insurance & Documentation

Many Cranford homeowners have filed a flood claim before, whether through NFIP after Floyd, Irene, or Ida, or through a standard homeowner's policy for a plumbing failure. Carriers treat these very differently, and adjusters pay close attention to whether water damage originated from a burst pipe (typically covered under a standard policy) versus river or surface flooding (an NFIP matter). We document moisture readings, photograph the pipe failure point, and keep a clear timeline so your claim isn't miscategorized or delayed while two policies argue over which one applies.
