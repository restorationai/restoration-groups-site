**Burst pipe cleanup and repair emergency in Manhattan?** We answer 24/7. A split riser or a blown radiator valve on the 14th floor doesn't stay on the 14th floor long: water tracks down through electrical chases and finds the path of least resistance into every ceiling below. In a borough where pre-war co-ops and glass condo towers share the same vertical plumbing stacks, a single pipe break is almost never a one-unit event, and the managing agent on the call needs a crew that already understands how these buildings work before the first wet-vac gets turned on.

## Why Manhattan Buildings See Burst Pipe Emergencies

Much of the Upper West Side and Upper East Side housing stock dates to the early 1900s, with cast iron and galvanized supply lines that have outlived their expected service life by decades. Those older risers corrode from the inside out, so a pipe can look fine on a plumber's last inspection and still fail without warning the following winter. Add exterior-wall units in prewar buildings where insulation was never designed for today's heating setbacks, and a hard freeze can split a line overnight. Harlem and Chelsea buildings on older Con Edison steam loops see a different failure pattern: a stuck radiator valve builds pressure until the joint gives, often in a unit nobody has checked in days.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Manhattan

The first call is almost always about stopping the source, so we confirm the unit's shutoff and, if the break is in a shared riser, work with the super or managing agent to isolate the stack without cutting water to the whole line. From there we extract standing water, pull baseboards and open ceilings only where moisture readings call for it, and set drying equipment sized to the unit rather than a blanket approach. Because a single break can affect several units stacked on top of each other, we build a moisture log for each one individually, which boards and insurers both expect to see before a claim moves forward. Repairs to the damaged line are completed before drying wraps up, so the space isn't opened up twice.

## Manhattan Co-op and Condo Board Coordination

Almost every building we work in asks for a certificate of insurance before a crew sets foot past the lobby, and most want it faxed or emailed to the management office the same day. Freight elevator reservations, protective floor runners in hallways, and a sign-in with the doorman are standard, not optional, and we build those steps into the schedule instead of treating them as delays. For buildings with a board-appointed engineer, we're glad to walk the unit with them before closing out the file, since that sign-off is often what releases the insurance payment to the owner.

Crews who don't know that pattern end up chasing the wrong wall for hours.
