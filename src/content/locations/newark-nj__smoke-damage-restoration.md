## Why Newark Properties See Smoke Damage Spread Between Units

Newark's housing is older and tighter together than most of the suburbs we serve. Brick multifamily walk-ups in the Ironbound and Downtown Newark sit close-set along narrow lots, and frame two- and three-family homes in Vailsburg and Weequahic share foundation lines and sometimes a single gas or electrical riser. When a kitchen or electrical fire breaks out in one unit, smoke doesn't stay contained to that apartment. It travels through shared cocklofts above the top-floor ceiling, through party walls with old mortar joints, and through return-air ducts that weren't designed with fire dampers. Forest Hill's larger colonials have more separation between structures, but their older plaster and original woodwork absorb smoke odor deeply, which means a fire in one room can leave a smell throughout the house long after the visible soot is gone.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Newark

We start with a walkthrough to separate dry smoke residue (fine, powdery, typical of fast-burning fires) from wet smoke residue (sticky, oily, common in smoldering fires involving synthetics and upholstery), because they respond to different cleaning agents and the wrong approach can smear soot deeper into porous brick and plaster. In Newark's older buildings, we pay close attention to the cockloft and any shared chase where the original fire wasn't, because that's where odor and soot particulates travel into neighboring units even after the fire department has cleared the scene. Hard surfaces get HEPA vacuuming and wet cleaning calibrated to the substrate. Porous materials like drywall, insulation, and some flooring assemblies in the affected zone are often removed rather than treated, since smoke odor embeds into cellulose in a way surface cleaning can't fully reverse. We finish with thermal fogging or ozone treatment in sealed, unoccupied spaces to neutralize residual odor molecules, followed by air scrubbing with HEPA filtration before we call the space clear.
