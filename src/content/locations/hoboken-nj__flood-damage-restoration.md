## Why Hoboken Properties Flood

Much of Hoboken's housing stock is 19th-century brownstone and brick rowhome construction, built with below-grade units that were never designed for modern stormwater volumes. Those party-wall buildings share plumbing stacks between adjoining units, so a blocked line or surcharging sewer in one building can push water into a neighbor's basement with no warning. Newer high-rise construction along the Waterfront handles stormwater differently, but it isn't immune: parking garages and mechanical rooms below street grade are still vulnerable when the sewer system backs up during a named storm or even a fast summer downpour.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Hoboken

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out of flooring, baseboards, and crawl spaces before it has time to wick further into plaster and subfloor. In older rowhomes with lath-and-plaster walls, that distinction matters: plaster can look dry at the surface within a day while moisture is still trapped behind it, so we track readings with moisture meters rather than guessing from appearance. Once extraction is complete, we set containment and air movers sized to the room, document every affected material with photos for your claim file, and monitor drying daily until readings confirm the structure has dried to an appropriate standard. For units below grade, we also check for contamination risk if the flooding originated from a sewer backup rather than clean groundwater, since that changes how materials get handled.
