Mold remediation calls in Piscataway follow a pattern tied directly to the town's geography: a finished basement near River Road that took on water during Ida and never fully dried out, or a Society Hill condo unit where a slow HVAC condensate leak went unnoticed behind a closet wall for months. Mold doesn't need a flood to take hold, just sustained moisture and 48 to 72 hours, and Piscataway's building stock and flood history give it plenty of opportunities.

## Why Piscataway Properties See Mold Issues

The Raritan River corridor has flooded repeatedly, from Floyd and Irene through Ida in 2021, and homes along River Road and the lower Johnson Park flatlands carry that exposure in their framing and subfloors even after the visible water is gone. Postwar capes and split-levels throughout Possumtown and Stelton often still have galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains feeding finished basements, and a slow pinhole leak behind paneling can run for weeks before anyone notices a musty smell. Newer construction isn't immune either: the large townhome and condo clusters in Society Hill and the Arbor section concentrate shared walls and HVAC systems, so a mold problem in one unit's bathroom or laundry closet can migrate into a neighbor's space through a common chase. Add in student-housing turnover near the Rutgers Livingston and Busch campuses, where units sit vacant between leases with HVAC systems powered down, and you have a steady stream of moisture-driven growth that has nothing to do with a storm at all.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Piscataway

Every job starts with containment, because the goal is to stop spores from traveling into the rest of the house or, in a Society Hill or Arbor condo, into an adjoining unit. We set up negative air pressure with HEPA filtration, seal off the work area with physical barriers, and only then begin removing affected material, whether that's drywall, insulation, or subfloor compromised by past river flooding. Older Possumtown and Stelton homes with finished basements often reveal more hidden growth once wall cavities are opened, particularly around those original cast-iron drain stacks where condensation has been feeding mold for years. Once the source is addressed and the space is dried and cleaned, we run air scrubbers and verify conditions before anyone moves contents back in. We don't offer mold testing or inspection as a separate add-on here; it's built into how we scope and close out the remediation itself.

## Piscataway Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies treat mold as a secondary damage item tied to a covered water loss, so documentation matters, especially for River Road and Johnson Park properties with a flood history on record. We photograph and log the affected materials and moisture readings as we go, which gives the adjuster a clear chain from the original water event to the growth we're removing. For Society Hill and other HOA-governed developments, association rules sometimes require notifying the management company before work begins and may dictate shared-wall access procedures; we coordinate that scheduling so the job isn't held up waiting on a board approval.

## Local note

Basements in the Possumtown and Stelton blocks built in the 1950s and 60s commonly have a sump pump installed decades after the original slab was poured, which means the perimeter drain tile often terminates at an odd angle or misses low spots entirely. When we're scoping mold growth along a basement wall in these homes, we check that drainage layout first, because remediating the mold without correcting the drainage gap just invites the same growth back within a season.
