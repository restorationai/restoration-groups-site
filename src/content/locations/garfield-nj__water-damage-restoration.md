## Why Garfield Properties See Water Damage Issues

Garfield sits low against the Passaic River on its western edge, and blocks along River Drive took on serious water during Ida in 2021, with basements in that corridor among the hardest hit in the county. Saddle River tributaries add a second risk layer: after hard rain, low-lying spots around the Dahnert's Lake area and parts of Plauderville pond quickly even when the main river stays within its banks. Those lines corrode and narrow from the inside, so a clog that would be a minor backup in a newer home can surcharge and push water into a finished basement here instead.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Garfield

We start by tracing the water to its source, which in Garfield's older multifamily buildings often means checking the shared stack before assuming a single-unit fixture failure. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water off finished basement floors and out from under flooring in first-floor units built close to grade. Structural drying follows, and we calibrate equipment placement to the building: plaster and lath walls common in pre-1950 Garfield housing hold moisture longer than drywall, so we monitor those assemblies more closely rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule. Moisture readings in framing, subfloor, and party walls guide when the job is actually done, not a generic drying calendar.

## Garfield Insurance Coordination

Most Garfield water losses fall into one of two buckets for insurance purposes: a sudden pipe or appliance failure, which standard homeowner's policies typically cover, or river and groundwater flooding, which usually falls under a separate flood policy. We document the source and the water's path on arrival so your carrier has the information it needs either way, and as a New Jersey licensed home improvement contractor we handle that paperwork as part of the job rather than leaving it to you to sort out after the fact.
