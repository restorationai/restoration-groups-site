## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Union

A visit starts with a walkthrough and moisture mapping, using a moisture meter and thermal imaging to find damp framing or subfloor behind finished walls, which matters in Union's older homes where a previous owner may have finished a basement over an unresolved leak. From there, air sampling and surface swabs are taken in suspect areas and compared against a baseline sample pulled from outdoor air or an unaffected room, since spore counts only mean something in context. Samples go to a third-party lab, and results typically come back within a few business days with species identification and spore counts. We walk the findings through with you in plain language, not just a lab report, and outline what level of response the numbers actually support.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Because Union homes vary so much in age and construction, a single tool rarely tells the whole story. We pair non-invasive moisture meters with borescope cameras to look inside wall cavities without cutting them open unless the readings justify it, and we use particle counters alongside the physical air samples to catch elevated spore activity that isn't yet visible as surface growth. In crawl spaces and older basements with dirt or partial slab floors, which are still common in Union Center and Washington-area homes, humidity logging over a short period can catch a pattern that a single-visit reading would miss entirely.
