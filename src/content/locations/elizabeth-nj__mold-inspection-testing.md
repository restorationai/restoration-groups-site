## Why Elizabeth Properties See Mold Growth

Much of Elizabeth's housing, particularly around Peterstown and Midtown, dates to the early 1900s: two- and three-family frame construction built tight to the lot line, often with shared party walls and basements that were never meant to handle modern plumbing loads. When a riser fails or a roof leaks in one of these buildings, moisture tracks into wall cavities shared by multiple units, and mold can establish in wall framing within 24 to 48 hours of a sustained leak. Low-lying sections of Elizabethport and Bayway add another layer: these neighborhoods saw real flooding exposure during Hurricane Ida, and tidal influence from the Elizabeth River means crawl spaces and basements near the water stay damp longer after storms than inspectors might expect further inland. Add Elizabeth's combined sewer system, which can push wastewater backward into basements during heavy rain, and you get a city where mold growth often has a documented water event behind it rather than a mystery source.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Elizabeth

We start with a visual walkthrough and moisture mapping using a moisture meter and thermal imaging, paying close attention to shared walls in multifamily properties and to foundation walls in homes near the river or the port corridor. From there, air sampling and surface swabs go to an independent lab, comparing indoor spore counts against outdoor baseline air so the results mean something rather than just listing names of organisms. For rental properties, which make up a large share of Elizabeth's housing stock, we document findings unit by unit since New Jersey landlord-tenant law generally holds property owners responsible for habitability, and a vague report doesn't help anyone make repair decisions. Lab turnaround typically runs a few business days, and we walk the property owner through what the results mean before recommending next steps.

## Elizabeth Insurance & Multi-Unit Documentation

Most Elizabeth mold claims we see trace back to a specific water event: a burst pipe, a sewer backup, or storm flooding rather than long-term neglect, and insurers want that distinction clearly documented. For landlords with multiple tenants affected by the same source, such as a shared basement laundry room or a common roof leak, we provide separate assessment documentation for each unit so claims and any required tenant notifications aren't held up by incomplete paperwork. As an IICRC Certified Firm, we follow recognized sampling protocols that carriers and property managers generally accept without pushback.
