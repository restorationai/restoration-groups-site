## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Westfield

We start by identifying the odor source rather than treating the symptom, pulling back baseboards or flooring where needed to confirm whether we're dealing with sewage contamination, smoke residue, or trapped moisture in original lath and plaster. Porous materials that can't be fully cleaned, like saturated subfloor or insulation, get removed; everything salvageable is cleaned with products matched to the contaminant before any deodorization treatment begins. For smoke odor in older homes with plaster walls and original woodwork, we rely on thermal fogging to drive deodorizing agents into the same tiny cracks and grain patterns that carried the smoke in the first place. For occupied homes, especially where kids, pets, or elderly residents are staying on-site during the work, hydroxyl generators handle airborne odor without the off-gassing restrictions that come with ozone. Ozone treatment still has its place for unoccupied vacant spaces or severe sewage and fire odor, run in sealed, unoccupied rooms with a clear re-entry window built into the schedule.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Which equipment we deploy depends on the material and the odor type, not a one-size routine. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously during source removal to keep particulate from resettling on clean surfaces. Thermal foggers are reserved for smoke and fire residue because the fog particle size mimics smoke behavior and reaches surfaces a wipe-down can't. Hydroxyl generators are our default for occupied Westfield homes because they're safe to run around people, pets, and houseplants while still breaking down odor molecules in the air and on soft surfaces like upholstery and drapery. Ozone generators go to work only after the space is cleared and sealed, typically on vacant properties or isolated rooms, always with documented clearance time before anyone goes back in.
