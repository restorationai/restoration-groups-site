## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Bridgewater

We start with safety and extraction, pumping standing water and running moisture mapping across walls, subfloor, and framing before we commit to a drying plan. In Bridgewater's older housing stock, that mapping matters more than usual: homes with finished basements built in the 1960s and 70s frequently have cast-iron drain lines that have already started to fail, so what looks like flood intrusion from outside is sometimes compounded by backup from inside the same walls. Once the source is confirmed, we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's actual moisture load and dry materials to the IICRC S500 standard rather than a fixed number of days. Flooring, drywall, and insulation that can't be dried in place get removed and documented before replacement, with everything photographed for your claim file.

## Bridgewater Insurance & HOA Coordination

Flood losses in Bridgewater often involve two separate policies: a standard homeowners policy for interior water damage and a flood-specific policy, usually NFIP-backed, for water that enters from outside through grading or river rise. We document which applies before filing so you're not waiting on a denial to figure it out. For the newer townhome developments along the retail corridors, HOA agreements frequently require written notice and sometimes HOA-approved vendors for any work touching shared foundation walls or exterior drainage, and we coordinate that paperwork alongside the restoration timeline so it doesn't become a second delay on top of the flood itself.
