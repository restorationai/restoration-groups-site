## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Millburn

The first step is always stopping active water intrusion and mapping how far it traveled, which matters more in Millburn's older homes than in newer construction. Plaster-and-lath walls common in the Short Hills and Glenwood sections absorb and hold water differently than drywall: surface readings can look dry within a day while the lath behind it stays saturated for much longer, so we track moisture with meters rather than guessing from appearances. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water from basements and crawl spaces before it migrates further into framing. Then we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the materials in the house, not a generic drying plan, because a 1920s colonial with original subflooring dries on a different timeline than a renovated split-level. Where cast-iron or copper lines contributed to the loss, we document the failure point for your insurance file and for whatever plumbing repair follows. Contents in finished basements, wine storage included, get inventoried and either dried in place or moved offsite depending on the material and the extent of saturation.
