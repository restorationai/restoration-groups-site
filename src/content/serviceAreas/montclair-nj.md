## Restoration emergencies common in Montclair

Montclair's water problems start with its geography. The Third River (Yantacaw) and its tributaries cut through low-lying sections of town, and when heavy rain overwhelms them, the South End and Frog Hollow see flash flooding and sewer backups, a pattern that turned severe during Ida in 2021. Hillside runoff coming down off the Watchung ridge adds pressure on basement sumps during any sustained storm, which is why finished basements in homes near the ridge are frequent callers for cleanup after a hard rain. Add in an aging housing stock, slate roofs that eventually let water behind the decking, plaster walls that hide a slow leak for weeks, and cast-iron drain lines original to early-1900s Tudors and center-hall colonials, and you get a township where water damage shows up in more forms than just a flooded basement.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Montclair

Much of Montclair's housing was built in the early 1900s through the 1930s: large Victorians, Tudors, and center-hall colonials with plaster walls, slate or aging asphalt roofs, and full basements, with the Estate Section holding the township's largest and oldest homes. Supply and drain systems from that era are commonly galvanized steel or cast iron, both of which corrode and fail from the inside long before a leak is visible, and older homes may carry asbestos-containing materials or lead paint that a restoration scope has to account for before demolition starts.

Low-lying ground near the Third River and its tributaries holds water against foundations and finished basements during heavy rain, while homes higher up on the Watchung ridge deal with runoff overwhelming sump systems rather than river flooding directly.
