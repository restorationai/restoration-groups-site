## Why Hoboken Properties See Water Emergencies

Hoboken sits low against the Hudson on reclaimed marshland, and that geography shapes almost every water call we get here. Superstorm Sandy put much of the city underwater in 2012, and Ida in 2021 showed the same vulnerability in a different form: heavy rain overwhelmed the combined sewer system and pushed water back into basements and garden-level units across the city, not just along the waterfront. Add to that a housing stock dominated by 19th-century brownstones and brick rowhomes with below-grade apartments, plus shared party-wall plumbing stacks running between units, and you get two recurring patterns: storm and sewer intrusion from outside, and pipe failures that travel unit to unit from inside. Newer construction along the Hudson adds its own wrinkle, with sprinkler lines and mechanical rooms in high-rise basements that flood fast when a line lets go.

## Our Emergency Water Removal Process in Hoboken

The first call is always about the source, because a sewer backup during a rainstorm gets handled differently than a burst pipe behind a Southwest Hoboken row house wall. Once the crew is on site, we pull standing water with submersible pumps and truck-mounted extraction units, then map moisture in flooring, baseboards, and subfloor with meters so nothing gets missed under original hardwood or century-old plaster. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and monitored until readings confirm the structure is actually dry, not just dry to the touch. In below-grade units near Stevens Institute or along the lower blocks toward the river, we also check for groundwater intrusion separately from plumbing failure, since the two require different containment approaches even when the visible damage looks the same.

## Hoboken Insurance & HOA Coordination

A lot of Hoboken water losses are not single-owner problems. Waterfront condo buildings often route common-area losses through a property management company before a claim moves forward, and brownstones split into multiple units mean a pipe burst on one floor is rarely contained to one owner's insurance policy. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings as we go, which gives both the building and the individual unit owner something solid to hand their carrier, and we are an IICRC Certified Firm, which matters when an adjuster wants to see that drying followed a recognized standard rather than guesswork.
