## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Springfield

We start with a visual assessment of the areas most likely to hold moisture for a township with Springfield's drainage profile: basement corners along the foundation wall, behind baseboard in rooms that back up to grade, and HVAC returns in homes near the Baltusrol area where finished basements sit closer to the water table. From there we use a moisture meter to map readings across suspect materials, not just the spot where the smell is strongest, because mold colonization often starts a few feet from where the water actually entered. If conditions suggest airborne spore activity, we collect air samples alongside a surface sample where growth is visible, and send both to a third-party lab so the results aren't coming from the same company recommending the remediation. You get a written report with lab findings, which is the document most insurance adjusters and HOA boards want to see before approving next steps.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Testing accuracy depends on calibrated equipment, not guesswork. We use thermal imaging to spot temperature differentials that often correlate with trapped moisture behind drywall or under flooring, since mold frequently grows in spots that read dry to the touch at the surface. Air sampling pumps pull a measured volume of indoor air through a cassette that a lab analyzes for spore types and concentration, then we compare those numbers against an outdoor control sample taken the same visit, which is standard practice for giving the indoor reading context rather than an isolated number. For finished basements near Meisel Avenue Park and similar low-lying sections of town, we also check sump pump function and discharge lines as part of the visit, since a pump that's cycling constantly during storm season is often the real source feeding a mold problem upstairs.
