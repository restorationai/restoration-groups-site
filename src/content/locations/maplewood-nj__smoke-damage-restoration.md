## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Maplewood

We start with a walk-through to identify what burned, how hot it ran, and how far the smoke traveled through the structure, including attic space and HVAC returns. Protein smoke from a stovetop fire and the heavier soot from a structure fire require different cleaning agents and different equipment, and using the wrong approach on original plaster or old wood trim can set the stain instead of lifting it.

From there we do dry and wet cleaning of affected surfaces, seal and treat porous materials that can't be fully cleaned, run air scrubbers with HEPA filtration to pull particulate out of the air, and use thermal fogging or ozone treatment where odor has penetrated deep into wood framing or subfloor. On homes with original plaster, we're slower and more deliberate than we would be on drywall, because over-aggressive cleaning can crack a century-old wall finish that's expensive to match. Contents, including furniture, textiles and anything stored in a basement, are evaluated separately since soot settles differently on fabric than it does on hard surfaces.
