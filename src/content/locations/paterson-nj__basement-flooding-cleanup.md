## Why Paterson Basements Flood

Paterson sits directly on the Passaic River, and the low-lying blocks below the Great Falls have flooded repeatedly, most notably when the river crested during Hurricane Irene and again during Ida. But river overflow isn't the only driver. Much of the city's housing was built for mill workers in the 19th century: dense rowhomes, brick multi-family walkups, and two- and three-family frame houses with cast-iron drain lines that were never sized for today's heavier rain events. In neighborhoods like Sandy Hill and Hillcrest, those original foundations often have hairline mortar cracks that let groundwater seep in slowly after a storm, sometimes showing up as a damp basement floor days after the rain has stopped. Converted silk-mill buildings add another wrinkle: aging sprinkler and standpipe systems in these retrofitted apartment buildings occasionally fail or leak, sending water straight down into ground-floor and basement units.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Paterson

We start by identifying the water source, river backflow, a cracked foundation, a failed sump pump, or a sewer lateral surcharge, because each one changes how we categorize and handle the water. Standing water comes out with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on basement access, which matters in rowhome basements where stairwells are narrow and bulkhead doors are the only other way in. Once the water is out, we pull baseboards and open wall cavities where moisture has wicked into plaster or old lath, since those materials hold water far longer than drywall. Air movers and dehumidifiers are set to pull moisture out of masonry foundation walls, a slower process than drying a finished basement, and we track moisture content daily with meters so drying time is based on readings, not guesswork. Anything porous that stayed wet long enough to be unsalvageable gets removed before we close the job out.
