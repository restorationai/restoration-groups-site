## Why Cranford Basements Flood

Cranford earned its "Venice of New Jersey" nickname honestly, and the same river that makes Nomahegan Park and the Cranford Canoe Club so pleasant is the reason Riverside Drive and the blocks closest to the Rahway River flood first and hardest when storms hit. Floyd, Irene, and Ida each pushed river water into basements across town, but river overflow isn't the only mechanism. Downtown Cranford and neighborhoods set back from the river, including parts of Cranford West and Sunny Acres, flood when storm sewers can't keep pace with rainfall and water backs up through foundation drains or floor drains instead. Housing stock matters too: a lot of Cranford's basements sit under 1900s to 1940s colonials and capes with fieldstone or aging poured-concrete foundations that were never built to handle today's storm volumes, so hairline cracks and old mortar joints become entry points long before anyone sees standing water upstairs.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process

We start by identifying the source, river backflow, sewer surcharge, or a failed sump, because that determines whether the water is classified as clean, gray, or contaminated for drying and disposal purposes. Extraction comes next using truck-mounted or submersible pumps depending on depth, followed by removal of saturated materials that won't dry in place: wet baseboard, soaked insulation, drywall that's wicked moisture above the flood line. Cranford's older foundations often hold moisture in the masonry itself longer than newer poured walls, so we set drying equipment to account for that and monitor moisture content in the block or stone, not just the air, before calling a basement dry. Photos, moisture logs, and psychrometric readings are taken throughout, which matters a great deal to Cranford homeowners who've filed a flood claim before and know what documentation their adjuster expects.

## Cranford Insurance Coordination

Most Cranford basement flooding falls under a separate flood policy rather than standard homeowners coverage, and many residents near Riverside Drive carry NFIP policies specifically because of the Rahway River's history. We photograph pre-existing conditions, document water lines and affected materials, and provide the kind of itemized drying report that NFIP adjusters and private carriers both ask for. If your loss involves both a flood policy and a homeowners claim, for example sewer backup coverage versus river flooding, we document in a way that supports whichever claim applies.
