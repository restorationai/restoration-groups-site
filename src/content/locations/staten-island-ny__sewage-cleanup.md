**Raw sewage in a Staten Island basement is a biohazard, not a cleanup chore you handle yourself.** We answer 24/7 and dispatch a crew trained in Category 3 water protocols to stop the source, extract contaminated water, and begin sanitization before bacteria and odor work deeper into flooring and framing.

## Why Staten Island Homes See Sewage Backups

A lot of Staten Island's housing stock, from the early-1900s frame homes near St. George and Port Richmond to the postwar Cape Cods spread across Great Kills and New Dorp, still runs on original cast-iron or clay sewer laterals. Those lines crack and let tree roots in over decades, and once a root mass narrows the pipe, a heavy load or a storm surge of groundwater is enough to send everything back up through the lowest drain in the house, usually a basement floor drain or laundry tub. The East Shore adds another layer: South Beach and other low-lying streets still carry the flood exposure from Hurricane Sandy, and the flash flooding Ida brought to South Shore blocks in 2021 showed that even inland properties can see sewer lines surcharge when storm drains back up faster than the borough's system can move water. Basements and crawlspaces, common across the island's older and postwar construction alike, sit right in the path of that backup.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Staten Island

We treat every sewage call as Category 3 water, which means porous materials that absorb contaminated water, carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, and in many cases subfloor, come out rather than get dried in place. After extraction with truck-mount pumps, we remove and bag affected materials, then clean and disinfect remaining structural surfaces to the IICRC S500 standard. Air scrubbers and antimicrobial treatments follow to address odor and airborne contaminants before we verify the space with moisture meters, not guesswork, to confirm it is dry before any new material goes back in.

## Staten Island Insurance Considerations for Sewage Losses

Standard homeowners policies often exclude sewer backup unless a separate rider was added, which catches a lot of Staten Island owners off guard after a first event. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings and can work directly with most major carriers on the claim, but it is worth checking your policy for a backup or sump endorsement before an event happens, particularly if you're in a flood-mapped ZIP like 10306 or one of the other East Shore-adjacent areas.

## Local note

In Port Richmond and parts of St. George, the clay sewer laterals running under older lots are narrower and more root-prone than the cast iron used elsewhere on the island, so a backup there often recurs within the same season unless the line itself gets addressed by a plumber, not just the cleanup. We flag that pattern for homeowners so they know cleanup alone won't stop a repeat call.
