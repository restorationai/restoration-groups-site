## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Paterson

We start with a visual and moisture-meter survey of the areas most likely to be compromised given the building's age and flood history, basements, exterior-wall closets, and any space that backs up to a shared wall in a multi-family structure. In pre-1950 rowhomes with plaster-and-lath construction, moisture reads differently than it does in drywall, so we check subsurface readings at multiple depths rather than trusting a single surface pass. Air samples get pulled from suspect rooms and compared against an outdoor baseline sample taken the same day, since spore counts shift with weather and season. Surface swabs or tape lifts go out to an independent lab when we see staining, bubbling paint, or a musty smell with no visible source. Results typically come back as a clear written report you can hand directly to an insurance adjuster, a landlord, or a buyer's attorney.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

A moisture meter alone won't tell the whole story in a building with cast-iron drains and knob-and-tube remnants still in the walls, since metal and old wiring throw off readings if you don't know how to read around them. We use thermal imaging to spot temperature differentials that suggest trapped moisture behind finished surfaces, then confirm with a pin or pinless meter before deciding where testing makes sense. Air quality testing uses calibrated spore traps, and we always run a control sample outdoors so the lab has something to compare the indoor air against. None of this replaces a lab result. It just tells us where to actually pull a sample instead of guessing.
