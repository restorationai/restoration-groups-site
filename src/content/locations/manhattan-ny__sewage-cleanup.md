**Raw sewage in a high-rise is a different emergency than sewage in a single-family basement.** When a stack line backs up on an upper floor in a Midtown or Upper West Side building, contaminated water doesn't stay put, it travels down risers, saturates ceilings below, and reaches finished units that had nothing to do with the original failure. The Restoration Group handles sewage cleanup and sanitization across Manhattan's co-ops, condo towers, and commercial ground floors, working inside the access and documentation rules those buildings require.

## Why Manhattan Buildings See Sewage Backups

Much of Manhattan's plumbing infrastructure predates modern code, and vertical stacks carrying waste from dozens of units down to a single sewer lateral are a common point of failure. A clog or collapsed section in a pre-war building's cast iron stack can send sewage backward into lower units rather than down to the street, and because many buildings share a single lateral connection to the city sewer, a blockage anywhere in that line affects every stacked unit above it. Storm surge is a separate but related risk: Sandy pushed sewage-contaminated floodwater into basements and ground-floor mechanical rooms across the Financial District and Lower East Side, and heavy rain events like Ida have overwhelmed aging combined sewer lines as far north as Harlem and Chelsea, backing sanitary waste into low-lying units during peak storm flow.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

We start by identifying the contamination category and shutting off the source where the building's engineer or super gives access. Standing sewage and saturated porous materials, carpet pad, baseboard, drywall below the flood line, are removed rather than cleaned, since category 3 water cannot be safely dried in place. Remaining structural materials are cleaned, treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials, and dried following IICRC S500 guidelines, with moisture logged room by room so the building's managing agent has a written record for every affected unit. In towers where a leak on one floor has soaked the ceiling below, we document and dry both the source unit and the exposed unit underneath before closing out either one.

## Manhattan Insurance & HOA Coordination

Co-op boards and condo managing agents in Manhattan typically require a certificate of insurance before a crew steps on site, along with advance notice to book the freight elevator and a unit-by-unit moisture log for the building's file. We build those requirements into scheduling up front rather than treating them as paperwork after the fact, which keeps a sewage loss from stalling in the lobby while residents wait. For co-op shareholders and condo owners, we document the loss and affected materials in a format carriers and building management both accept, which helps separate what the resident's policy covers from what falls under the building's master policy.

## Local note

In Manhattan's older multi-family stacks, a sewage backup rarely stays on one floor. Because waste risers run vertically through shared chases, a blockage at a lower connection point can push contaminated water up and out through fixtures several floors above the actual clog, so a unit reporting a sewage smell with no visible overflow often isn't the source of the problem, it's downstream of one. We trace the stack before we start sanitizing so the actual point of failure gets fixed, not just the symptom.
