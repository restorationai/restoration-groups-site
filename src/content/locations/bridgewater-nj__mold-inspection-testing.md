## Why Bridgewater Properties See Mold Issues

Bridgewater's housing stock splits into two risk categories, and both end up calling us for different reasons. The postwar ranches and split-levels around Milltown, Bradley Gardens and Martinsville were mostly built before finished basements were standard, so when a sump pump fails or a cast-iron drain backs up, the water sits against framing that was never meant to handle it. Add the flooding history in Finderne and the Bound Brook-adjacent flats, areas that took on water during Floyd and again during Ida in 2021, and you have a lot of basements that were pumped out but never professionally dried or tested afterward. On the other side, newer colonials and the townhome developments near the Route 22/287 interchange are tighter and better insulated, which is good for energy bills but traps humidity in bathrooms, laundry closets and HVAC returns if ventilation isn't sized correctly. Both patterns produce the same end result: mold colonizing somewhere you can't see it from the hallway.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Bridgewater

We start with a visual walkthrough, checking the usual failure points for this area: sump pump discharge lines, basement window wells, crawl space vapor barriers, and the grading around foundations in older Chimney Rock-area lots where soil has settled over decades. From there we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map dampness inside walls and under flooring without cutting anything open first. If conditions warrant it, we collect air samples (comparing an indoor reading to an outdoor baseline) and, where visible growth is present, surface or tape-lift samples for species identification. Samples go to an independent lab, and you get a written report with findings you can hand to an insurance adjuster, a real estate attorney, or a property manager. We don't do the remediation work off our own test results without disclosing that upfront, since that's a conflict most homeowners want to avoid.

## Bridgewater Insurance & HOA Coordination

Mold coverage through homeowners insurance is inconsistent and usually hinges on whether the moisture source was sudden (a burst supply line) versus gradual (a slow roof leak or long-term grading problem). A documented inspection report, done close to when the water event happened, is often the difference between a covered claim and a denied one. For the townhome and condo communities near Bridgewater Commons, HOA bylaws frequently require an independent air quality or clearance report before any unit owner can start remediation work in shared wall cavities, since the association has liability for common structural elements. We provide the lab documentation those boards typically ask for, separate from any remediation proposal.

## Local note

Split-level homes common through Martinsville and Green Knoll often have a crawl space under one wing and a full basement or slab under the other. The two zones share ductwork but not moisture conditions, so mold can establish in the crawl space side for months before anyone smells it in the living area above, usually a damp sock or boxes you can't quite place. If your split-level has one side that always feels colder or smells different near the floor vents, that's worth testing before it's an odor problem in the whole house.
