## Why Newark Basements Flood

Newark's combined sewer and stormwater system serves most of the city, and during intense rain events the pipes simply run out of capacity. Water and waste have nowhere to go but back up through the lowest point in a building, which is almost always the basement floor drain or laundry hookup. Ironbound properties near the Passaic River face this on top of river-level rise during named storms. Further west, Forest Hill's larger pre-war foundations and University Heights' mix of institutional and rental housing often have aging perimeter drain tile that was never designed for today's storm volumes. Add clay or dense silty soil common across much of the city, and water has nowhere to drain except straight into the foundation wall.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Newark

We start by identifying whether the water is clean, gray, or contaminated, because Newark's sewer-backup pattern means a flooded basement here is more likely to involve sewage than in a lot of towns we serve. If contamination is present, extraction and sanitizing follow the same protocols we use on sewage jobs, not standard water removal. Once the source is confirmed and the water is extracted, we pull baseboards, assess drywall and framing, and set drying equipment calibrated to the basement's size and construction. Pre-war basements with masonry walls and dirt or old concrete floors hold moisture longer than newer slab construction, so we track drying with moisture meters rather than guessing on a fixed timeline, and we document everything with photos for your claim file.

## Newark Insurance Coordination

A large share of Newark basement flooding calls involve rental properties, two- and three-family homes, and buildings managed by landlords who need documentation that satisfies both their insurer and, in some cases, a mortgage servicer or city housing inspector. We photograph pre- and post-mitigation conditions, log moisture readings, and provide the kind of itemized scope insurers expect before approving drying equipment and any affected material removal. Because sewage backup and flood coverage are often handled under different policy riders in New Jersey, we flag which category applies as soon as we identify the source, so there are fewer surprises when the claim is reviewed.
