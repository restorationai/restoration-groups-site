## Why Passaic Properties See Mold Issues

Passaic is one of the most densely built small cities in New Jersey, and that density shows up in the housing stock: early 1900s brick multi-family walkups, two- and three-family frame houses, and rowhomes near Downtown Passaic and the Third Ward with knob-and-tube wiring remnants and original cast-iron drain lines that are now decades past their intended service life. When those drains fail, or when a sewer line backs up into a basement, moisture gets into floor joists, sill plates, and wall cavities that have no mechanical ventilation to begin with. Add the city's flood history, including significant inundation during Irene in 2011 and again during Ida in 2021 along riverfront blocks and low-lying streets, and you have a building stock that has absorbed moisture more than once in the last fifteen years. ## Our Mold Remediation Process in Passaic

Work in a Passaic two- or three-family starts with containment that accounts for shared stairwells and party walls, since airflow in attached and semi-attached buildings doesn't stop at a unit line the way it might in a detached single-family home. We seal the work area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure before any demolition of affected drywall, plaster, or subfloor begins, following the same source-removal and drying principles outlined in the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard. In older Fourth Ward and Rutherford Heights properties where plaster walls are still original, we're careful about how much material actually needs to come out versus what can be cleaned and HEPA-vacuumed, since over-demolition in a pre-war building creates its own repair headaches for the owner. Once the source of moisture, whether that's a failed drain, a grading issue, or a basement window well that floods during storms, has been corrected or flagged for the property owner, we run air scrubbers and dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the space, not a one-size-fits-all unit count.
