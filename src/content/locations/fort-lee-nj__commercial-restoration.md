## Why Fort Lee Businesses See Commercial Damage

Fort Lee's commercial footprint splits roughly into two building types, and each fails in its own way. The high-rise towers that line the cliffs above the Hudson concentrate dozens of units on shared risers and supply lines, so a single failure in one unit routinely becomes a multi-floor loss by the time it's reported. Around the Hudson Lights complex and the retail corridor near Main Street, ground-floor commercial spaces sit closer to grade and take on water differently, often through door thresholds, loading dock seams, or roof drains that can't keep up during a heavy Nor'easter. Older mixed-use buildings near Coytesville and The Hill add another variable: original plumbing and electrical that wasn't sized for the retail or office tenants now occupying the space. Bergen County saw how fast that combination turns into a serious loss when Ida hit in 2021, and property managers here have been more aggressive about moisture monitoring ever since.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Fort Lee

Commercial jobs move differently than residential ones, and in Fort Lee that usually means coordinating with a building manager or HOA board before a single piece of equipment comes off the truck. We start with a walkthrough to document the affected units or suites, moisture-map adjoining spaces (critical in stacked condo buildings where damage travels vertically through risers and shared walls), and set containment that keeps common areas and other tenants operational. Extraction and drying equipment gets placed to minimize disruption to business hours wherever possible, and we document everything with photos and moisture readings so the building's insurance carrier, and any tenant's separate policy, has what it needs. For retail and office spaces, that documentation step matters more than people expect: a lease dispute over downtime can hinge on how quickly the loss was recorded and dried.

## Fort Lee Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most of Fort Lee's commercial losses touch more than one policy. A high-rise along the Palisades typically has a master policy for the building and separate HO-6 or commercial tenant policies for individual units or storefronts, which means a single pipe break can generate two or three insurance claims running in parallel. We've found that getting ahead of this early, identifying which party's policy covers what before drying even starts, saves weeks of back-and-forth later. For condo and co-op boards in Central Fort Lee and Linwood, we also work directly with property management to satisfy HOA documentation requirements before equipment is removed and the space is signed off.

## Local note

Buildings along the Palisade side of town sit on bedrock close to the surface, which changes how water moves once it's inside a structure. Instead of draining down through soil the way it might in a flatter town, water in a Fort Lee basement or ground-floor commercial unit tends to pool and sit, because there's nowhere for it to go. We factor that into drying plans for ground-level retail near the escarpment: extraction has to be more thorough up front, since the usual slow seepage into subsoil isn't happening the way it would elsewhere.
