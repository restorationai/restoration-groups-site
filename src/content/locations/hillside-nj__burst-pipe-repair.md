## Why Hillside Homes See Burst Pipe Failures

Hillside's housing stock, colonials and two-families built mostly before 1955, with a strong run of postwar capes around Saybrook and Conant, was plumbed with galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drains that were never meant to last a century. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the bore until a weak elbow or a frozen snap during a hard January cold front lets go all at once, usually behind a finished basement wall where nobody notices until water is already under the baseboard. Semi-finished basements, common throughout the township, compound the problem: carpet pad, paneling, and stored belongings absorb water fast, and a slab-on-grade foundation with little drainage slope means the water finds the lowest closet or stairwell before it finds a drain. We also see a steady run of pipe breaks tied to pressure spikes after township water main work, which stresses old joints that were already close to failing.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Hillside

The first call is always about the water source, so our crew isolates the shutoff and confirms the line is dead before extraction starts. From there we pull standing water with truck-mount extractors, pull wet baseboard and drywall to the documented waterline, and set air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the room, not a generic number. Because so many Hillside basements have cast-iron drain stacks original to the house, we check for backup at the same time we're addressing the supply break: a burst pipe upstream can mask a partially blocked drain downstream, and missing that means a second flood a week later. Repair itself, cutting out the failed section and reconnecting with modern PEX or copper, is coordinated with a licensed plumber so the drying and the fix happen on the same visit window instead of a multi-day gap that lets mold start colonizing wet framing.
