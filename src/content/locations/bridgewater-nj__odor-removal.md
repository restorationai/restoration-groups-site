## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Bridgewater

Odor work starts with finding and removing the source material, not covering it up. In a flooded Finderne or Milltown basement, that often means pulling baseboard, cutting contaminated drywall to the documented waterline, and extracting carpet pad that's held sewage-tinged water against subfloor for days. Once source material is out and the space is dried per the IICRC S500 standard, we select the deodorization method based on what's left: hydroxyl generators for occupied homes where people and pets are still living on-site, ozone treatment for vacant structures or short unoccupied windows, and thermal fogging when smoke or soot residue has worked into porous trim and cabinetry after a fire. Each method gets paired with air scrubbing and, where needed, sealing of framing members that still carry odor at the cellular level.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Ozone breaks down odor molecules effectively but requires an empty building and a measured re-entry period, so it's typically reserved for vacant properties or commercial spaces in the office parks off Route 22 that can be cleared overnight. Hydroxyl generators do similar work through oxidation without the occupancy restrictions, which makes them the better fit for an occupied split-level in Bradley Gardens or Green Knoll where a family needs to stay in the house during treatment. Thermal fogging, which carries a deodorizing fog into the same cracks and crevices smoke penetrated, is reserved for fire-related jobs where the odor has bonded to wood grain and fabric rather than sitting on the surface. We also address duct systems directly rather than just treating living space, since recirculated air is often why a basement odor shows up on the second floor.
