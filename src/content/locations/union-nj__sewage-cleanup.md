Much of the backup pattern in Union traces to the Elizabeth River and Rahway River tributaries that run through town. Low-lying stretches off Morris Avenue and parts of Vauxhall take on stormwater fast during hard summer thunderstorms, and township sewer mains can surcharge when heavy rain outpaces capacity, pushing wastewater back up through basement fixtures at the lowest point in the house. Properties near these flood-prone pockets saw this firsthand during Ida, when sewage and stormwater mixed and backed into basements that had never flooded before. On the commercial side, the Route 22 retail corridor and the restaurants along it generate their own sewage and grease-line failures, usually overnight or over a weekend when a kitchen line backs up unnoticed. Student housing around Kean University adds a different pattern: older plumbing stacks serving multiple units mean one clogged line can send sewage into more than one apartment at a time.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Union

Sewage is a category 3 loss no matter where it originates, but how we approach it shifts with the house. In Union's finished basements, we typically have to remove baseboard, carpet pad, and often the bottom few inches of drywall before we can even begin extraction, because contaminated water has already wicked into framing. We extract standing sewage with truck-mount or portable pumps, remove and bag all porous materials that contacted contamination (carpet, pad, insulation, damaged drywall), and treat remaining structural surfaces with EPA-registered antimicrobials. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run throughout to manage airborne contaminants while the space dries, and we verify drying with moisture meters before any rebuild work begins. For older cast-iron drain failures, we coordinate with a plumber to confirm the line is cleared or repaired before we close the space back up, since re-contamination from the same failed line is the most common reason a sewage job has to be redone.

## Union Insurance & HOA Coordination

Homeowners insurance in New Jersey typically excludes sewer backup unless a specific rider was added, which catches a lot of Union homeowners off guard after the fact. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings regardless, because that record matters whether a claim goes through a standard policy, a sewer backup endorsement, or a township liability claim tied to a surcharged main. For the condo and townhome developments near Union Center and along the Route 22 corridor, we also coordinate directly with property management or the HOA on access and shared-wall documentation, since a single backup in those buildings often affects more than one unit.
