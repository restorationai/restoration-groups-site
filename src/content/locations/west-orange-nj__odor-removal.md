## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process

We start by identifying the source material, not just the smell. Smoke odor removal after a fire means checking HVAC ductwork, insulation, and framing cavities, since smoke travels through a house's air pathways and settles on cool surfaces far from the original fire. For standing water and sewage-related odors, we pull affected flooring and baseboards where needed and treat the subfloor directly rather than masking the smell on top of it. Depending on the material and severity, we use thermal fogging to drive a deodorizing agent into the same pathways smoke or odor compounds followed, hydroxyl deodorization for occupied spaces where ozone isn't appropriate, and ozone treatment for unoccupied structures or contents that can be sealed off for a treatment cycle. Odor testing at the end confirms the space reads clean before we close out the job, which matters most in finished basements and older homes with plaster or horsehair lath that can trap residual compounds.

## Equipment & Methods We Use

Hydroxyl generators are our default choice in homes where people and pets are still living on-site, since they don't require evacuation and work on a slower but steady cycle breaking down odor molecules. Ozone generators go to work in vacant properties, garages, or sealed-off rooms, particularly after a fire where smoke has penetrated fabric, insulation, or HVAC components. Thermal fogging pairs well with both, since the fog particle size mimics smoke behavior and reaches into the same cracks, outlet boxes, and duct runs the original odor traveled through. For contents that absorbed odor but are worth saving, like upholstered furniture or area rugs, we treat them separately so they're not re-contaminating a room we've already cleared.
