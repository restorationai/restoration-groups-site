## Why Staten Island Properties See Water Damage Issues

Staten Island's housing stock tells the story. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Staten Island

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable units sized to the job, whether that's a flooded crawlspace in Tottenville or a commercial stockroom off Hylan Boulevard. From there we map moisture through walls, subfloor, and framing with meters, because surface-dry drywall over a saturated stud cavity is one of the most common misses in older Staten Island homes. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to match the building's construction, plaster and lath hold and release moisture differently than modern drywall, so dry-down schedules in pre-1960s houses often run longer than in newer construction. We document everything with photos and moisture logs as we go, which matters later when a claim moves to adjustment.

## Staten Island Insurance Coordination

Most Staten Island water losses fall into one of two insurance buckets: a homeowner's policy claim for a burst pipe or appliance failure, or a flood policy claim for surge and surface water tied to a named storm. The distinction matters because flood coverage usually runs through a separate NFIP or private flood policy, not the standard homeowner's form, and East Shore properties in FEMA-mapped flood zones frequently carry both. We photograph and log water intrusion the way carriers expect to see it, so the paperwork lines up whether the claim is a pipe break in a 10304 two-family or storm surge damage in a 10306 ranch near the beach. For the newer townhome developments on the South Shore, we also coordinate directly with property management when a shared wall or common area is involved.
