## What should you actually check before hiring one of these companies?

Three things matter more than a star rating alone: certification, response time, and whether the company documents the loss the way your insurer expects. Ask whether the technicians on site hold [IICRC certification](https://www.iicrc.org/) and whether drying will follow the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), which sets the benchmark for how a wet structure should be measured, dried and verified dry. Ask how fast a crew can actually be on-site, not just whether the company says "emergency service" on its website. And ask how they'll document moisture readings and photos for your claim before any drying equipment is set up, since most homeowners policies only cover water damage that's sudden and accidental, not a slow leak that built up over months (see our guide on [what homeowners insurance covers for water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/)).

If the loss involves a sump pump failure or a backed-up drain, check your policy for a water backup endorsement specifically. Most standard homeowners policies exclude sewer and sump pump backups unless that coverage was added separately, regardless of which restoration company you hire.

A Fair Lawn-specific factor worth asking about: does the company have local experience with the Passaic and Saddle River flood patterns, or is it dispatching from out of county? A crew that already knows which basements along River Road flood first during a storm surge gets ahead of secondary damage faster.
