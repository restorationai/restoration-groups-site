## Why Manhattan Buildings Trap Smoke Differently

Pre-war co-ops built with plaster walls, horsehair insulation, and original millwork absorb smoke odor into porous surfaces in ways modern Sheetrock doesn't. In Midtown and Chelsea high-rises, pressurized stairwells and shared mechanical shafts can pull soot and odor several floors from the source, so an apartment with no visible fire damage can still smell like smoke days later. Central air risers common in post-war towers near the Financial District move particulate efficiently through the building when a fire starts low, which is why boards often ask us to inspect units well above and below the loss before anyone signs off that a floor is clear.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Manhattan

We start with a walk-through to separate what's salvageable from what isn't, since not every smoke-stained surface needs to be stripped, only sealed and cleaned correctly. HVAC filters and ductwork get inspected early, because recirculated air is often how odor spreads from a Harlem rowhouse kitchen fire into an adjoining unit. Hard surfaces are cleaned with agitation and the right chemistry for the soot type (protein, dry, or wet smoke each respond differently), porous materials that can be saved go through thermal fogging or ozone treatment in a sealed space, and contents that need deeper work are cataloged for pack-out. We document everything room by room, which matters in buildings where a managing agent wants proof the adjacent units were checked, not just told they were fine.
