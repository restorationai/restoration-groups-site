## Why Mountainside Homes Are Prone to Water Damage

Much of the housing stock across this part of Union County went up during the postwar suburban build-out of the 1950s and 1960s, and Mountainside is no exception. Split-levels and raised ranches from that era often have finished or semi-finished basements with copper or galvanized supply lines that are well past their expected service life. Galvanized pipe in particular corrodes from the inside out, so a slow leak behind a wall or under a slab can go undetected for months before it finally shows up as a stain on the ceiling below or a soft spot in the flooring. Combine aging plumbing with clay-heavy soil that doesn't drain quickly after storms, and foundations with even small hairline cracks can let groundwater seep in during a wet spring.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Mountainside

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much volume we're dealing with and whether the loss is contained to a basement or has traveled up through flooring. From there, we check moisture content in the subfloor, drywall, and framing with meters, because a floor that feels dry to the touch can still be holding significant water in the material underneath. Structural drying comes next: air movers positioned to create cross-flow across wet surfaces, paired with dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, running until moisture readings confirm the materials are back to a normal range, not just until things look dry. In homes with finished basements, that often means pulling baseboards or cutting small inspection openings in drywall to check the wall cavity, since trapped moisture behind an intact wall is exactly what sets up mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.

## Mountainside Insurance Coordination

Most water losses we see in this area fall under a homeowner's policy as a sudden and accidental discharge, which typically covers pipe bursts and appliance failures but usually excludes gradual leaks or groundwater flooding. We document moisture readings, photograph affected materials before removal, and provide the itemized scope adjusters ask for, so you're not stuck interpreting insurance language while also trying to keep your household running. If your Mountainside property has a sump pump or French drain system installed after a prior water event, it's worth confirming with your carrier whether sump failure is specifically covered, since that's a common gap we run into.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Mountainside basements specifically: finished rec rooms built directly over a slab with carpet glued down rather than installed over padding. That construction method traps water underneath the carpet backing in a way that's easy to miss on a surface check, and it can hold moisture against the slab for days if it isn't pulled back and dried properly. We lift the edges and check underneath before calling a basement dry, even when the carpet on top feels fine.
