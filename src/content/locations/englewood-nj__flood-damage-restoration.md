## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Englewood

The first call is about stopping the water's path, not just removing what's already pooled. On a finished East Hill basement, that usually means pulling baseboards and checking behind drywall before it looks wet on the surface, because water migrates up through porous block and stone far slower than it does through modern poured concrete. In West Englewood's denser multifamily stock, we're also checking adjoining units and shared mechanical spaces, since water doesn't respect a lease line.

Once the source is controlled, extraction comes first: pumps and truck-mount vacuums for standing water, followed by moisture mapping with meters to find what's hidden in subfloor, framing, and wall cavities. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed based on the actual moisture readings rather than a standard room count. For flooring that's been submerged, especially original hardwood common in East Hill homes, we document condition before removal since insurance adjusters often want photographic evidence of swelling or cupping before approving replacement.
