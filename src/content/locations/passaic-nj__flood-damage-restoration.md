## Why Passaic Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Passaic sits low against the Passaic River, and the city has lived through two major river floods in the last fifteen years, Irene in 2011 and Ida in 2021, both of which pushed water into riverfront blocks and low-lying sections of Downtown Passaic. But river flooding isn't the only driver. Passaic is one of the densest small cities in New Jersey, built up with early 20th-century brick multi-family walkups and two- and three-family frame houses, many still running on original cast-iron drain lines that were never sized for today's household volume. When those lines slow down or fail during a heavy storm, the result is often a basement flood that looks and smells very different from a clean river overflow, and it needs to be treated that way from the first visit. Overcrowded rental stock in the Third Ward and Fourth Ward also means a single flood event can affect multiple units and multiple leases at once, which changes how the cleanup gets coordinated.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Passaic

We start with an assessment of water source and category, river floodwater, groundwater intrusion, or backed-up sewer and drain lines are handled under different protocols, and in a building like the frame houses common near the Prospect Park border, that distinction matters because floodwater often tracks differently through balloon-frame wall cavities than through modern platform framing. Extraction comes first, using truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on access. In multi-family walkups common to the Third Ward, that often means staging equipment on narrow stairwells and coordinating with more than one tenant before we can even start pulling water. Once standing water is out, we document moisture readings room by room, remove unsalvageable materials like saturated subfloor or baseboard, and set drying equipment calibrated to the structure, not a generic drying plan. Drying is tracked and logged against the IICRC S500 standard until materials hit a safe moisture reading, not just until surfaces look dry.

## Passaic Insurance & Flood Zone Coordination

Many Passaic properties near the river fall inside mapped flood zones, which means claims often route through NFIP flood insurance rather than a standard homeowner's policy, and the documentation requirements are different. We photograph and log affected materials room by room specifically so that distinction is clear when a claim gets filed, whether it's a standard water loss or a federally backed flood claim. For multi-family buildings in the Fourth Ward where a single storm affects several units, we also document per-unit damage separately so each tenant's or owner's claim can move independently.

## Local note

Basements in the blocks closest to the river, particularly below the grade near Pulaski Park, tend to hold groundwater pressure for days after a storm even once surface water has receded. Pumping those spaces too aggressively, too fast, can actually pull in more water through hydrostatic pressure on the foundation walls. We stage extraction in phases on these properties rather than running pumps continuously, which is a detail that matters a lot more in Passaic's riverfront basements than it does further inland.
