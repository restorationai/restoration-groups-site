## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Piscataway

Odor work only holds if the source material is dealt with first, so we start by identifying what's actually carrying the smell, whether that's saturated subfloor in a Possumtown-area basement, smoke residue coated across drywall after a kitchen fire, or sewage-contaminated material from a backed-up lateral. Porous materials that can't be fully cleaned (soaked carpet pad, charred insulation, water-swollen baseboard) get removed rather than just treated. From there we apply deodorization matched to the contamination type: thermal fogging to drive deodorizer into the same microscopic spaces smoke penetrated, hydroxyl generators where the space is occupied and ozone isn't appropriate, and ozone treatment in vacant units or commercial spaces that can be sealed off and aired out properly afterward. We confirm the air and surfaces are clear before we call the job finished, not just that the smell has faded to the nose.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Ozone is effective but it's also aggressive, it oxidizes rubber, degrades certain plastics, and isn't safe to run around people or pets, so it's reserved for spaces that can be vacated for the treatment cycle. Hydroxyl generators produce a similar oxidizing effect without the occupancy restrictions, which makes them the better fit for the Society Hill townhomes and Rutgers-area rentals where residents need to stay in the unit. Thermal fogging is our go-to for smoke odor removal because the heated fog particles mimic how combustion byproducts originally traveled into cracks, seams, and HVAC returns, reaching odor pockets that surface cleaning or air freshening sprays never touch.
