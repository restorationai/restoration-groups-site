## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Passaic

We start with a room-by-room assessment to separate dry smoke residue, which sits on surfaces and lifts with dry-chemical sponges, from wet smoke residue, which smears and bonds into porous material like plaster and unsealed brick. In Passaic's older buildings, that distinction matters: plaster walls hold odor-causing particles deeper than drywall, so we often need thermal fogging or ozone treatment in addition to surface cleaning, not just a wipe-down. HVAC and shared duct systems get inspected and cleaned where smoke traveled through a building's ventilation rather than staying confined to one unit. Contents go through a separate cleaning and deodorization pass, since upholstery, clothing, and wood furniture hold smoke odor long after walls test clean.
